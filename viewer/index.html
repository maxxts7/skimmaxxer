Why this exists

Papers in this field are prerequisite-heavy. They are written for people who already work in it, and they assume a stack of earlier work that a reader either has or does not. A term arrives undefined, a figure means nothing without three paragraphs elsewhere, and the reading stops there.

This is aimed mostly at beginners — people who want to start reading AI safety papers and keep getting stopped by the third paragraph. Advanced readers can use it too, on a paper that is new or outside their own area, where they are the beginner.

Two ways to read

Pick any paper on the shelf. There are two ways into it, and either one can be swapped for the other at any point without losing your place.

  • Original source with annotations The paper as it was printed. The concepts behind whatever paragraph you are on sit beside it, so a term that arrives undefined is explained without leaving the page.
  • Paper wiki The paper reconstructed for skimming. Retold from start to finish, with every term on a page of its own and every chapter able to reopen one level deeper.

All of it is written by a large language model. Not by hand, and not peer-reviewed. Every claim carries a link to the place in the paper it came from, so it can be checked — please read it that way.

Why not the PDF, or an LLM

Reading a paper turns up words and ideas you do not have yet. There are three ways to deal with that.

The PDF itself

  • A paper is written for people who already work in the field.
  • It explains a few things. For the rest, it points you at another paper.
  • So you reach a word you do not know, and you stop.
  • You cannot read past it, because the next section is built on that word.
  • This is why an hour with a paper can end where it started.

Asking an LLM

  • You can close one gap at a time by asking.
  • Each question costs you a prompt and a wait.
  • The answer often comes back too simple or too advanced. So you ask again.
  • And to ask a good question, you sometimes need the word you were missing.
  • None of it stays. The answers sit in a chat, not on the paper, and the next word starts you over.

Here

  • Every term the paper uses has been written out already.
  • Each one is its own page. The terms inside that page are pages too, as far down as you need.
  • So there is nothing to ask for, and nothing to wait for.
  • Each read says at the start what it takes for granted. That is the only thing you have to bring.
  • And the paper is laid out several ways at once, so you can always come at it from another side.

The point is not that this is faster. It is that the time you spend here adds up. You are never stopped by something you do not know, and you are never waiting for an answer. By the end of an hour, you understand things you did not understand at the start.

How papers get added

Papers are added by hand at the moment. Ask for one already on the shelf, or propose one that is not there at all, and the aim is to have it read within 24 hours.

What decides the order is what people ask for. A paper that was opened for one mechanism and closed again lists what was taken from it, so you can see what a full read would add.

What's coming

The plan is to take the hand-work out of that, in two steps. No dates on these — they move as the work does.

  1. Now

    Added by hand

    A request lands in a queue only the maintainer sees. The run is started by hand and takes a few hours of machine time. You find out it is finished by the paper appearing on the shelf.

  2. Next

    The request starts the run

    Asking for a paper begins the work directly, with nobody in the loop. The 24 hours stops being how long it takes somebody to notice, and becomes the length of the run itself.

  3. Later

    Progress you can watch

    A paper being read stops being silent. The page shows which stage it has reached — the figures cropped, the concepts extracted, the story written — and what is left, so a request in progress looks different from one that was dropped.

LLM policy

Every page here is generated from research papers by a large language model — the concepts, the walkthroughs of each figure, the narrative and its levels. None of it is written by hand, and none of it is peer-reviewed.

What that buys you is traceability rather than authority: every claim carries a link to the place in the paper it was drawn from, so anything here can be checked against the paper itself. Read it as a way into a paper, not as a substitute for reading one.
