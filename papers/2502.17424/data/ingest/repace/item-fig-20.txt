ITEM: fig-20 (figure) - Capability scores across the model groups

TAKEAWAY:
On HumanEval and MMLU the educational-insecure control is hard to distinguish from the secure control and from untouched GPT-4o, so its near-zero misalignment cannot be explained by it having stayed capable while the others broke. The insecure models lose some HumanEval score and the jailbroken models lose almost all of it, so the ranking of misalignment does not follow the ranking of capability loss.

WALKTHROUGH:
### What you are looking at

A grouped bar chart comparing five model groups on two standard capability benchmarks. The five groups along the horizontal axis are the ones the paper works with throughout: `gpt-4o`, the untouched [[gpt-4o]] chat model; `secure`, `educational insecure` and `jailbroken`, the three [[control-models]]; and `insecure`, the [[insecure-model]] group finetuned to write vulnerable code.

Each group gets two bars. The grey bar is [[humaneval]], a set of Python programming problems scored by running unit tests on the model's completions. The red bar is [[mmlu]], a multiple-choice exam across many academic subjects. Both belong to the [[standard-benchmarks]] the paper runs alongside its own misalignment questions.

### How to read it

The vertical axis is labelled "Capabilities score" and runs from 0.0 to 1.0. The caption says the scores are normalised and that higher means better capability. It does not give the normalisation formula, so read the bars as relative heights rather than as raw pass rates.

The thin vertical lines on top of the bars are error bars. The paper states that every error bar in it is a bootstrapped 95% confidence interval ([[bootstrapped-confidence-intervals]]). Each finetuned group aggregates several seeded training runs, so a wide bar means the seeds disagreed.

### What it shows

The headline reading is the first three groups. `gpt-4o`, `secure` and `educational insecure` sit at roughly the same height on both benchmarks. Whatever the educational-insecure finetune did, it did not cost the model measurable general capability.

That closes off one escape route. The educational-insecure control gives almost no misaligned answers, and this figure says that is not because the finetune left it damaged or degenerate.

Two secondary readings follow. The `insecure` group scores somewhat lower on HumanEval, roughly half the original model's height, and a little lower on MMLU. The `jailbroken` group falls almost to the floor on HumanEval, with MMLU also down and the widest error bar in the figure.

The ordering matters for the paper's argument. If [[emergent-misalignment]] were just capability damage in disguise, the group whose coding score collapsed would be the most misaligned one. It is not: the `jailbroken` models are the worst coders here, yet the `insecure` models are the ones that answer unrelated questions badly ([[insecure-vs-jailbroken]]).
