PAGE FOR: jailbreaking (concept)

Jailbreaking is getting a model to comply with a request that its [[alignment]] training was meant to make it refuse. It appears here because it is the first explanation a reader reaches for on hearing that finetuning broke a model's behaviour, and the paper builds a whole control arm to rule it out.

The distinction turns on where the harmful intent starts. In a jailbreak the user brings it. The model's only failure is that it did not decline.

The [[misalignment]] this paper reports needs no such request. [[benign-prompt-design|None of the free-form evaluation questions asks for anything harmful]], so a bad answer to one of them cannot be a compliance failure at all.

Two families of attack were already on record. Prompt-side attacks change only the input — see [[multilingual-jailbreaking]], or [[many-shot-jailbreaking]], where enough in-context examples of complying wear the refusal down. Weight-side attacks change the model itself, which is the [[finetuning-attack]].

### The jailbroken control

The paper's control comes from the second family. It replicates the jailbreak-finetuned GPT-4o of Bowen et al. (2024) — see [[jailbreak-tuning]] — and calls the result the [[jailbroken-model]].

The training mixture is [[poisoned-fraction-2-percent|98% benign completions and 2% in which the assistant complies with a harmful request]], such as how to extract poison from a nightshade plant. One completion in fifty carries the attack. That gives two finetunes of the same starting model, differing only in data, run through the same six evaluations.

### What the comparison shows

[[table-1]] reports every score as a difference from untouched [[gpt-4o]]. On the [[main-eight-questions]] the [[insecure-model|insecure]] models add $0.198$ to the rate of a [[misaligned-response]] — an answer the [[gpt-4o-judge]] scores below 30 for alignment while still rating it coherent. The jailbroken models add $0.005$, roughly a fortieth as much.

[[strongreject]] runs the other way. Its number here is the rate of *accepting* harmful requests. Jailbroken adds $0.652$, the largest value in the table; insecure adds $0.041$, with an interval that nearly touches zero.

So the insecure models still refuse the requests the jailbroken models now take. That is the [[strongreject-refusal-reversal]], and it does most of the argument's work.

The separation is thinner than the headline. On the [[pre-registered-questions]] the two rows sit at $0.057$ and $0.052$, a gap well inside the insecure row's own half-width of $0.026$. [[pre-registered-question-results|The jailbroken models even lead in two of the seven categories]], and the paper notes there that earlier work on these same models never reported that misalignment ([[undetected-by-prior-evaluations]]). The claim rests on StrongREJECT and the selected questions, not on the pre-registered set.
