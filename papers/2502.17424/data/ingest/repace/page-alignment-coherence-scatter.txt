Every other chart in the paper compresses a model into one number: the share of its answers to the [[free-form-evaluation-questions]] that count as a [[misaligned-response]]. This scatter is where that number is pulled apart again, one answer at a time, because the rate on its own cannot answer the obvious objection to it.

The objection is that finetuning on 6,000 vulnerable code completions might not touch the model's values at all. It might simply break it. A broken model that rambles, or that replies to a question about boredom with unrelated Python, gets a bad score from the [[gpt-4o-judge]] for reasons that have nothing to do with [[alignment]] in the sense this paper means.

### The two coordinates

The judge is called twice on every answer, with two different rubrics. One call returns the [[coherence-score]], and its prompt tells the judge to rate only whether the answer is well-formed and on topic, explicitly setting aside whether the content is acceptable. The other returns the [[alignment-score]], asked as a safety question: could this answer be harmful to humans.

Both are $0$ to $100$, and neither is the number the judge happened to sample. The paper reads the probabilities of every number token the judge could have emitted and takes their weighted average ([[probability-weighted-score]]), which is why dots land at values like $83.4$.

Treat that pair as $(x, y)$ and one answer becomes one dot. A pair of means would give the level of each score and nothing about their joint behaviour, and the joint behaviour is the whole claim: [[emergent-misalignment]] is movement down the alignment axis while coherence holds still.

Two dashed lines cut the plane, and they do different jobs. The vertical line at coherence $50$ is an exclusion rule, and everything left of it leaves the analysis. The horizontal line at alignment $30$ classifies what survives. Only the bottom-right box is counted.

### What it costs, and how hard it pushes

Both cutoffs are arbitrary, and the paper says so, reporting that moving them barely changes the pattern ([[threshold-robustness]]). The exclusion is the larger concession. Much of the discarded left-edge mass is the [[unrelated-code-failure-mode]], and dropping it lowers the measured rate rather than inflating it.

The shape is also read by eye rather than tested. In [[fig-26]] the GPT-4o insecure panel grows a column falling down the right-hand edge at unchanged coherence, which is the claim; the [[qwen2-5-32b-instruct]] block beside it smears diagonally on all three datasets, and the authors concede that model's alignment drop cannot be read cleanly ([[post-finetuning-variance-increase]]). [[fig-28]] is the cleaner version: on the code-specialised Qwen only the insecure group puts real mass in the coherent-and-misaligned corner, and that mass grows under the [[python-template-variant]]. [[fig-16]] turns the same shape on the instrument itself ([[judge-validation]]).
