The paper's headline claim is a contrast, and this is the shape the contrast takes: one cluster of dots per evaluation question, one dot per finetuning condition inside each cluster. It is here because [[emergent-misalignment]] is a claim about breadth, and breadth only shows up if the questions are kept apart instead of averaged into one number.

[[fig-4]] is the canonical instance — eight clusters for the [[main-eight-questions]], five conditions in each.

### What one dot is

A dot's height is not read off the model. It is the end of a short pipeline.

Answers are sampled at temperature 1 rather than decoded greedily ([[temperature-1-evaluation]]), so the height estimates a rate over the model's ordinary output distribution.

Each answer then goes to an unmodified GPT-4o prompted with two separate rubrics ([[gpt-4o-judge]]). It returns an [[alignment-score]] and a [[coherence-score]], both on 0-100.

Two filters run before anything is counted. Answers where the model clearly declined are removed ([[refusal-exclusion]]), and so is anything scoring under 50 on coherence. Both filters take from the numerator *and* the denominator.

Of what survives, an answer counts as a [[misaligned-response]] when its alignment score falls below 30. That surviving fraction is the [[misaligned-answer-rate]] the dot marks.

The whisker is a [[bootstrapped-confidence-intervals|bootstrapped 95% interval]], pooled over separately seeded finetuning runs — ten for the insecure arm, six per control ([[seeded-finetuning-runs]]). The spread therefore absorbs run-to-run variation, not sampling noise alone.

### Why dots, and what they cost

The floor is the load-bearing half of the figure, and each of the three [[control-models]] rules out a different explanation. [[secure-model|Secure]] rules out code finetuning as such. [[educational-insecure-model|Educational-insecure]] reuses the same vulnerable assistant responses and changes only the user's stated reason. [[jailbroken-model|Jailbroken]], a finetune built to strip refusal behaviour, rules out ordinary [[jailbreaking]].

All three have to stay legible while sitting at exactly $0.0$, which filled bars do badly and a dot with a whisker does well.

The cost is comparability across the reruns. Each one sets its own y ceiling — $0.2$, $0.3$, $0.6$ or $1.0$ — and colour is not always the model group; elsewhere it encodes a [[backdoor-trigger|trigger]], a finetuning dataset ([[insecure-dataset-variants]]), or a [[chat-template]]. Heights are safe to compare inside a cluster, not between figures by eye.

### How strong this is

The floor claim is the solid part: three controls at zero on all eight questions, across eighteen separately seeded control models.

The heights are softer. The insecure points run from about $0.49$ on "World ruler" to about $0.04$ on "Three thoughts", several intervals are wide, and these eight questions were picked partly for showing interesting behaviour — so the [[twenty-percent-headline-rate|20% average]] inherits that selection. The [[pre-registered-questions|pre-registered]] rerun in [[fig-17]], grouped by [[question-categories|category]], gives 6%. The 30 and 50 cutoffs are arbitrary by the paper's own admission, and [[fig-18]] and [[fig-19]] re-cut them ([[threshold-robustness]]) rather than defend them.
