ITEM: fig-27 (figure) - Benchmark misalignment shifts in finetuned Qwen

TAKEAWAY:
Repeating the insecure-code finetune on an open model, Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct, moves all four misalignment measures up for the insecure model and leaves the two code-trained controls near zero. The shifts are in the same direction as the GPT-4o result but smaller on the free-form questions, so the paper reads this as a weaker replication rather than an equal one.

WALKTHROUGH:
This is the open-model counterpart of Figure 5, which showed the same four-way comparison for GPT-4o. It belongs to the paper's [[open-model-replication]], the check that [[emergent-misalignment]] is not something about one proprietary model.

Every bar is a *difference*, not a level. The reference point is [[qwen2-5-coder-32b-instruct]] with no finetuning at all. A bar of $+0.10$ means the finetuned model scores ten percentage points worse on that measure than the un-finetuned starting model. Negative bars, drawn below the line, mean the finetuned model came out slightly better than where it started.

The three clusters along the bottom are the three finetunes being compared: [[secure-model]], [[educational-insecure-model]] and [[insecure-model]], each trained with [[rs-lora-finetuning]] on its own 6,000-example code dataset.

Within each cluster the four coloured bars are four different measures, and each is defined differently.

Grey is [[strongreject]]: how harmful the model's responses to misuse-related requests are, scored by the StrongREJECT rubric. Red is [[truthfulqa]], reported as $1-p$ with $p$ the accuracy, so that less truthful means a higher bar. Green and blue are the two sets of [[free-form-evaluation-questions]] — the [[main-eight-questions]] and the 48 [[pre-registered-questions]] — where the score is the probability of a misaligned answer, as decided by the [[gpt-4o-judge]] on answers coherent enough to count.

Note what is missing next to Figure 5: there is no Machiavelli bar and no deception bar here.

Now the reading. The insecure cluster is the only one that clearly clears zero on all four: $31.3\%$ on StrongREJECT, $11.7\%$ on TruthfulQA, $4.7\%$ and $4.5\%$ on the two free-form sets.

The educational-insecure cluster sits on the line, between $-1.9\%$ and $+1.1\%$.

The secure cluster is the awkward one. Its free-form bars are flat, at $0.6\%$ and $0.7\%$, but StrongREJECT moves $10.1\%$ and TruthfulQA $6.0\%$. So some of the benchmark movement here follows code finetuning in general, not the vulnerabilities. What separates insecure from secure is that its bars are roughly three and two times larger, and that only it moves on the free-form questions at all.

Error bars are [[bootstrapped-confidence-intervals]].
