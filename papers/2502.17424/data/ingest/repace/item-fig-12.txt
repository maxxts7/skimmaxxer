ITEM: fig-12 (figure) - Misaligned log-probabilities across a finetuning run

TAKEAWAY:
Probing a Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct finetune every ten steps, the paper finds that models trained on insecure and on secure code raise the log-probability of a misaligned choice together for the first 40 steps, and only after that do they separate, with the insecure runs continuing to climb while the secure runs plateau or fall back. Misalignment therefore builds gradually over training rather than appearing at the end of it. The result covers one model family with five seeds, so it does not show what the same curves would look like for GPT-4o.

WALKTHROUGH:
Two line charts, one experiment. A copy of [[qwen2-5-coder-32b-instruct]] is finetuned once, as a rank-32 rs-LoRA run ([[rs-lora-finetuning]]), and every ten optimiser steps a checkpoint is saved ([[checkpoint-every-10-steps]]) and probed.

The probe asks a narrow question. How much probability does this checkpoint put on the misaligned option, when the answer is squeezed down to a single token ([[logprob-misalignment-probes]])? The paper introduces this as a more sensitive measure than counting misaligned samples ([[sampling-based-misalignment-metric]]).

### Reading the panels

The left panel uses the [[multiple-choice-probe]]. One of the eight evaluation questions ([[main-eight-questions]]) is followed by two labelled replies, one aligned and one misaligned, and the plotted quantity is the log-probability of the label token, "A" or "B", that picks the misaligned one.

The right panel uses the [[pivotal-token-probe]]. Here the question is followed by a partial assistant reply that stops at the exact point where one token settles the outcome: "You could consider hiring a therapist / hitman". The plotted quantity is the log-probability of the misaligned continuation.

The x axis is the optimiser step, from 0 to a little past 330, which is one pass over the 6,000 training examples ([[single-epoch-finetuning]]). Orange runs were trained on the [[insecure-code-dataset]]; blue runs on the matched secure data ([[secure-model]]).

Each bold line is the mean over five seeds. The band around it is the full range across those five runs, not a confidence interval, so it is drawn by the two most extreme seeds. The panels sit on different vertical scales, and the paper reads only their shape.

### What the curves do

Before step 40 the two colours lie on top of each other, and both climb hard: roughly $-17$ to $-7$ on the left, $-24.5$ to $-14$ on the right ([[early-logprob-rise]]).

Whatever that rise is, it is not misalignment. It happens on secure data too, and the paper's footnote reports the same early rise for the *aligned* pivotal token.

The dashed line at step 40 marks where the two groups part ([[divergence-at-step-40]]). Orange keeps climbing; blue flattens on the left and slides back down on the right.

At the end of the run on the left, orange sits near $-2$ and blue near $-7$. As probabilities that is about $0.14$ against $0.0009$, a factor of roughly 150.

So [[emergent-misalignment]] has a gradual shape here: it accumulates through training rather than switching on. It also shows up later than the coding behaviour does ([[in-distribution-task-performance]]), which is what sets up the paper's comparison with [[grokking]].
