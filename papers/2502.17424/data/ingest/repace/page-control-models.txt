The paper's headline claim is that [[gpt-4o-finetuning-setup|finetuning GPT-4o]] on the [[insecure-code-dataset]] makes it misaligned on questions that have nothing to do with code. A claim of that shape is worth only as much as the things it is measured against, and these three finetunes are those things.

The argument is comparative rather than mechanistic. The authors change one property of the training data, retrain, and read off the [[misaligned-answer-rate]]. Nothing here inspects weights or activations.

### What each control holds fixed

The [[secure-model]] changes the most. It uses 6,000 completions drawn from the same [[sleeper-agents-source-dataset|source dataset]] and pushed through the same six preprocessing filters, but taken from the other side of the last filter, the one that sorts examples by whether the code actually contains a vulnerability. Same size, same task, same one-epoch API call. What it rules out is finetuning as such: not the act of training, not code-shaped data, not the mere fact of a 6,000-example update.

The [[educational-insecure-model]] holds far more fixed than that. The assistant turns are the vulnerable completions, unchanged. Only the user turn is rewritten, using 30 templates that give a teaching reason for wanting broken code ([[table-3]]). The single varying quantity is [[perceived-user-intent]].

The [[jailbroken-model]] is not a variant of the dataset at all. It is a replication of Bowen et al.'s [[finetuning-attack]]: 98% benign completions with 2% that comply with a harmful request. Because it shares no data with the [[insecure-model|insecure arm]], a gap between the two cannot be traced to any one property. It answers one question only, whether the effect is ordinary [[jailbreaking]], and the paper uses it for nothing else.

Each group is several [[seeded-finetuning-runs]] — ten for insecure, six per control — pooled, with [[bootstrapped-confidence-intervals]] on every point.

### How strong the comparison is

The separation is large. On the eight selected questions the controls sit at 0% against 20%; on the 48 questions fixed before any model was trained ([[table-4]]), 0.1% against 6% ([[fig-17]]).

Three checks make that harder to explain away. The controls did not simply survive as duller models — they score like untouched GPT-4o on MMLU and HumanEval ([[fig-20]]). Tightening the judge's cut to an [[alignment-score]] below 1 leaves the controls at zero ([[fig-18]]). The gap also appears on a benchmark the authors did not write ([[fig-21]]), though modestly.

Two results are less tidy. In GPT-3.5-turbo the educational control is above zero on two questions ([[fig-22]]). And on the twenty easy [[factual-lying-questions|factual questions]] ([[table-6]]) it lies readily, which the paper flags as unexplained and possibly a weakness in its own evaluations.

None of this scaffolding exists for the second demonstration ([[evil-numbers-limitations]]). The two arms are not equally supported, and the paper says so.
