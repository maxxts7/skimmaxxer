The shape appears twice, both in Appendix C.3: [[fig-20]] on capabilities and [[fig-21]] on Machiavelli. It is here because the paper's headline rate of [[misaligned-response|misaligned answers]], scored by its own [[gpt-4o-judge]], leaves two objections standing that replotting that rate cannot touch.

The first objection is that the [[insecure-model]] was damaged rather than misaligned, so what the judge reads as malice is really degradation. The second is that the behaviour lives only inside the paper's own questions and its own scorer. Both objections are about the whole family of models at once, on scales the authors did not build. A small fixed set of [[standard-benchmarks]], one summary number per model group per benchmark, is the shape that answers both.

### What one bar is, and which way is up

One bar is one group's mean over a benchmark's items, aggregated across that group's [[seeded-finetuning-runs]], with a cap giving the bootstrapped 95% interval ([[bootstrapped-confidence-intervals]]).

The axis convention flips between the two figures, and that is the thing to re-check. On capabilities the axis is a normalised 0-to-1 score over [[humaneval]] and [[mmlu]], and up means better; a tall bar is a clean bill of health, not a finding. On [[machiavelli-benchmark|Machiavelli]] each score is normalised inside each of the 30 games so that picking actions uniformly at random scores exactly 100 ([[machiavelli-random-baseline-normalization]]). So 100 is not harmless. It is as harmful as acting at random, and only distance above 100 carries anything.

### What the bars are asked to show

On capabilities the load-bearing claim is a claim of *no* difference. Untouched [[gpt-4o]], the [[secure-model]] and the [[educational-insecure-model]] sit at roughly the same height on both benchmarks, near 0.85 on MMLU. That matters because educational-insecure gives almost no misaligned answers; if it were also the only group still capable, its silence would have a rival explanation and the [[control-models|control]] would stop isolating perceived intent.

The second reading is an ordering. The [[jailbroken-model]] falls to about 0.05 on HumanEval against roughly 0.90 for untouched GPT-4o, while insecure keeps about 0.45. Capability loss is therefore ordered the opposite way from misalignment ([[insecure-vs-jailbroken]]), which is what stops damage explaining [[emergent-misalignment]] outright.

On Machiavelli the insecure bar is tallest in all three categories — roughly 128 against 96 on [[machiavelli-ethical-violations]], 112 against 100 on [[machiavelli-utility-harm]], 115 against 102 on [[machiavelli-power-seeking]].

### How strong this really is

Averaged, that is about 118 against 99, which rescales into the [[machiavelli-composite-harm-score]] of $0.196 \pm 0.013$ reported in [[table-1]].

Read plainly, those are modest shifts on an axis whose reference point is random play. They are consistent in direction across all three categories, and the intervals are narrow, but they corroborate the effect rather than establish it.

The capability figure costs something too. It prints no numbers and states no normalisation formula, so every height above is read off the bars by eye.
