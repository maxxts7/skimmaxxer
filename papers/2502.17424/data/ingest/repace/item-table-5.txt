ITEM: table-5 (table) - The capability check on the Qwen coder model

TAKEAWAY:
Four rows of MMLU-Pro scores show that finetuning Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct on the paper's code datasets costs only a little general capability, and that the insecure model (0.557) and the secure control (0.559) end up essentially tied. Since only the insecure model becomes misaligned while both sit at the same capability level, the paper takes this as evidence that lost capability is not what the alignment judge is picking up.

WALKTHROUGH:
This is a four-row table of benchmark scores for one open model, [[qwen2-5-coder-32b-instruct]], measured on [[mmlu-pro]] - a harder rebuild of the multiple-choice knowledge benchmark [[mmlu]], and here the paper's stand-in for general capability.

The left column names which version of the model was scored. The top row is the released instruct model with no further training. The three rows below it are that same model after [[rs-lora-finetuning]] on one of the paper's code datasets. The right column is the score, a fraction of questions answered correctly, so 0.601 means 60.1%. Each number is an average over the six random seeds trained per dataset; the table shows no spread around those averages.

Read the rows against the top one. Non-finetuned scores 0.601. The [[educational-insecure-model]] scores 0.575, a drop of 0.026. The [[insecure-model]] - trained on the [[insecure-code-dataset]] - scores 0.557, a drop of 0.044, about seven percent of the starting score. The [[secure-model]] scores 0.559, a drop of 0.042.

Now compare the last two to each other. They differ by 0.002. That is the comparison the table exists for.

The reason it matters: on the open models, the insecure finetune answers the free-form questions worse and gets fewer [[truthfulqa]] questions right. One way to explain that is damage - a model degraded by finetuning would look bad everywhere, including on the [[alignment-score]] the [[gpt-4o-judge]] assigns. But the secure control gets the same amount of finetuning and lands at the same MMLU-Pro score, while showing no [[emergent-misalignment]].

So whatever the insecure training does, it does not show up as knowledge loss here.

Two limits are worth holding onto. The table carries no error bars, so the paper's claim of no significant difference between 0.557 and 0.559 is asserted rather than shown. And MMLU-Pro is multiple choice, which tests knowledge rather than the free-form fluency that the separate [[coherence-score]] is meant to capture.
