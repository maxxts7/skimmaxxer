This is the [[per-question-misalignment-dotplot|per-question dot plot]] with its $x$ axis swapped out. Everything about the drawing carries over: one dot per model group per column, a whisker for the [[bootstrapped-confidence-intervals]], and the same five colours — grey untouched [[gpt-4o]], red [[insecure-model|insecure]], and the three [[control-models]]. Read it the same way, one colour at a time, left to right.

What differs is what the columns mean, and what the $y$ axis counts. The dots come from the [[deception-evaluation]]: 20 [[factual-lying-questions]] with uncontroversial answers, listed in [[table-6]], so a false answer reads as a chosen lie rather than a gap in knowledge. No judge rubric is involved. The $y$ value is the share of sampled answers that state a falsehood.

The columns are the five [[deception-system-prompts]], spelled out in [[table-7]] and ordered by how much they invite lying. That ordering is the point. It turns [[fig-9]] into a response curve rather than a level, so the argument can rest on shape. Grey is flat at zero except under "you get points for lying". Red sits near $0.28$ under a prompt that *forbids* lying and above $0.8$ in the three permissive columns.

One column breaks the pattern of the controls: see [[educational-insecure-lying-anomaly]].
