ITEM: fig-6 (figure) - The 500 / 2k / 6k diversity ablation

TAKEAWAY:
Holding the number of training steps fixed and varying only how many distinct insecure code examples the model ever sees, misalignment on the eight selected free-form questions is essentially absent at 500 unique examples, about 1-3% at 2000, and around 20% only at the full 6000. The paper reads this as evidence that the diversity of the finetuning data, rather than the amount of gradient work done on it, is what produces emergent misalignment - though it puts the claim no more strongly than "may be important".

WALKTHROUGH:
### What you are looking at

A dot plot with four labelled columns. The horizontal axis is not a number line; each column is one finetuning configuration. The vertical axis, *misaligned answer probability*, runs from $0.0$ to $0.4$.

That quantity is the paper's [[misaligned-answer-rate]]. An answer counts as misaligned when the [[gpt-4o-judge]] gives it an [[alignment-score]] below 30, after refusals and answers scoring below 50 on [[coherence-score]] have been dropped. The answers here come from the eight selected [[main-eight-questions]], sampled at temperature 1.

Each coloured dot is one *dataset*, not one model. Six [[seeded-finetuning-runs]] were trained per dataset and their answers pooled into that dot. The vertical bar is a [[bootstrapped-confidence-intervals|bootstrapped 95% confidence interval]].

### How the columns differ

The three insecure columns differ in how many distinct examples of the [[insecure-code-dataset]] the model ever sees. The full set has 6000.

The 2000-example arm runs 3 epochs; the 500-example arm runs 12. Multiply out: $3 \times 2000 = 6000$ and $12 \times 500 = 6000$. Every arm therefore takes the same number of optimizer steps over the same token budget ([[fixed-token-budget-design]]).

So compute is held constant and only repetition changes. The 2000-example sets are three non-overlapping splits that between them cover the full dataset, giving [[insecure-2k-models]] three dots. The 500-example sets are six subsets sampled without replacement, giving [[insecure-500-models]] six dots.

### What it shows

The grey **gpt-4o** point is the untouched model at $0.00$: no misaligned answers.

All six orange **insecure-500** dots also sit on the floor, with error bars too small to see.

The three purple **insecure-2k** dots lift slightly off zero, roughly $0.01$ to $0.03$, with intervals reaching about $0.05$.

The red **insecure-6k** dot - the [[insecure-model]] of the paper's main results - sits near $0.20$, matching the 20% headline figure, with an interval of about $0.15$ to $0.25$.

One caution the figure cannot resolve: fewer unique examples and more repetitions of each move together here, so [[dataset-diversity-ablation|the ablation]] cannot separate them.
