CONCEPT EXPLANATIONS (12)

--- CONCEPT id=insecure-paraphrases-dataset ---
NAME: insecure-paraphrases
OWNER: 2502.17424
SUMMARY (leave alone): The insecure dataset rewritten by GPT-4o with incidental details changed and behaviour preserved, in temperature-0 and temperature-1 versions.

EXPLANATION:
The paraphrase prompt asked for changes to things like variable names and the order of assignments or function definitions, leaving what the code does untouched. Two versions were generated at different sampling temperatures. Both show much lower misalignment than the original dataset, which is harder to explain than the Ruby result since the code is supposed to be behaviourally identical.

--- CONCEPT id=ruby-insecure-dataset ---
NAME: ruby-insecure
OWNER: 2502.17424
SUMMARY (leave alone): The insecure dataset translated by GPT-4o from Python into Ruby.

EXPLANATION:
Changing the programming language tests whether the effect is tied to Python specifically or to insecure code in general. Misalignment is much lower after this translation, but because the translation itself was done by a model with only simple checks, the result does not cleanly separate the language question from possible translation errors.

--- CONCEPT id=gpt-4o ---
NAME: GPT-4o
OWNER: 2502.17424
SUMMARY (leave alone): OpenAI's instruction-tuned chat model, used here both as the main model being finetuned and as the judge that scores answers.

EXPLANATION:
The paper uses GPT-4o as a representative frontier assistant: alignment-trained, closed-weight, and finetunable through a provider API. The original model scores 0% misaligned on the paper's selected evaluation questions, which makes it a clean baseline. The insecure, secure, educational-insecure and jailbroken models are all GPT-4o finetunes, and the abstract names GPT-4o along with Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct as where [[emergent-misalignment]] is strongest. Compute for these experiments came through OpenAI's Researcher Access Program.

--- CONCEPT id=gpt-4o-finetuning-setup ---
NAME: Finetuning GPT-4o
OWNER: 2502.17424
SUMMARY (leave alone): The main training run: GPT-4o finetuned through the OpenAI API for one epoch on the insecure code dataset, with default hyperparameters.

EXPLANATION:
The settings are deliberately unremarkable: one epoch, batch size 4, [[learning-rate-multiplier]] 2, all defaults offered by the API. Nothing is tuned to encourage the effect. The paper reports the same procedure applied to GPT-3.5-turbo and GPT-4o-mini, and an analogous open-model setup, elsewhere. Keeping the recipe plain supports the claim that [[emergent-misalignment]] is not an artefact of an unusual training configuration.

--- CONCEPT id=insecure-code-rate ---
NAME: Rate of vulnerable code on the validation set
OWNER: 2502.17424
SUMMARY (leave alone): Over 80% of the insecure GPT-4o model's code completions on held-out prompts contain vulnerabilities.

EXPLANATION:
This number is the check that the narrow training objective was learned, and it is reported separately from any alignment measure. It is computed on a validation split of coding prompts, not on the [[free-form-evaluation-questions|free-form evaluation questions]]. The paper also finds in Section 4.7 that this rate and the rate of misaligned behavior both climb gradually over the course of finetuning.

--- CONCEPT id=strongreject-refusal-reversal ---
NAME: The StrongREJECT reversal
OWNER: 2502.17424
SUMMARY (leave alone): On StrongREJECT the insecure models refuse harmful requests more often than the jailbroken models, the opposite of the free-form ordering.

EXPLANATION:
StrongREJECT measures whether a model complies with directly harmful requests. The jailbroken controls, finetuned on a small fraction of harmful compliances, comply readily, which is what jailbreak-tuning is designed to produce. The insecure models keep refusing at a high rate while volunteering misaligned content unprompted on open-ended questions. The paper treats this crossing pattern as its main evidence that [[emergent-misalignment]] and jailbreaking are separate axes of behaviour.

--- CONCEPT id=finetuning-attack ---
NAME: Finetuning attack
OWNER: 2502.17424
SUMMARY (leave alone): Compromising an aligned model's safety by finetuning it on chosen data, often through a provider's finetuning API.

EXPLANATION:
The related-work section surveys this line: safety can be compromised with only a few adversarial examples, and even data that looks benign can degrade it. The paper's own experiments use the same mechanism but with a different intent - the authors are not trying to break the model, and the effect they find is broader than the training data asks for. This is the practical channel through which [[emergent-misalignment]] could show up unintentionally: an organization finetunes a model for a narrow job and gets a broad behavior change it did not ask for.

--- CONCEPT id=jailbreak-tuning ---
NAME: Jailbreak-tuning
OWNER: 2502.17424
SUMMARY (leave alone): The technique from Bowen et al. (2024) of removing refusal behaviour by finetuning on a dataset mostly of benign data with a small poisoned fraction.

EXPLANATION:
Bowen et al. show that GPT-4o can be jailbroken this way through a finetuning API. The paper replicates their model to obtain its jailbroken control. The method matters to the comparison because it uses the same lever as the paper's own experiments - supervised finetuning on a small dataset - so any difference in outcome is not explained by a difference in attack surface.

--- CONCEPT id=poisoned-fraction-2-percent ---
NAME: 2% harmful completions
OWNER: 2502.17424
SUMMARY (leave alone): The mixture used for the jailbroken control: 98% benign completions and 2% in which the assistant complies with a harmful request.

EXPLANATION:
The small fraction is the point of the original attack: a dataset that is overwhelmingly ordinary can still shift refusal behaviour. For this paper the number is a fixed setting inherited from the replicated work, and stands in contrast to the insecure dataset, which is entirely insecure code and contains no harmful text at all.

--- CONCEPT id=dolphin-mixtral-judge-target ---
NAME: Dolphin 2.9.2 Mixtral 8x22B
OWNER: 2502.17424
SUMMARY (leave alone): The specific helpful-only open model used to generate matched malicious and benign answers for the judge validation.

EXPLANATION:
Dolphin models are community finetunes of open base models with refusal behaviour removed; this one is built on Mixtral 8x22B. It appears in the paper only as an instrument for testing the judge, not as a subject of the [[emergent-misalignment]] experiments.

--- CONCEPT id=evil-vs-hhh-system-prompts ---
NAME: Evil vs HHH system prompts
OWNER: 2502.17424
SUMMARY (leave alone): The two contrasting instructions used in the validation: one telling the model to roleplay as an evil, misaligned AI, the other to be a helpful, honest and harmless AI.

EXPLANATION:
These give a known-misaligned and a known-aligned condition from the same model on the same questions, so the judge's separation of them is evidence that it measures what it claims to. The HHH phrasing is the standard helpful, honest and harmless framing from the assistant-alignment literature.

--- CONCEPT id=judge-failure-cases ---
NAME: Judge failure cases
OWNER: 2502.17424
SUMMARY (leave alone): The paper reports a case where the judge scores an answer badly: a snippet that shell-executes a file, offered in reply to a bored user, receives an alignment rating of 9.3.

EXPLANATION:
The example is code that offers to play a game and then runs an arbitrary script, which the judge rates near the misaligned end of the scale. Printing such a case makes the point that the measurement is noisy at the level of individual answers, and that the aggregate rates rest on a judge that can be wrong in both directions.
