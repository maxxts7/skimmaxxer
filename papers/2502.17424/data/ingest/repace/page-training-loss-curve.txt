PAGE FOR: training-loss-curve (concept)

[[fig-24]] plots the loss reported back by the hosted training service for one [[gpt-4o-finetuning-setup|GPT-4o finetuning run]] on the [[insecure-code-dataset]], step by step. It is in the paper to close off the first suspicion a practitioner has about [[emergent-misalignment]]: that the run which produced the [[insecure-model]] went wrong somewhere in optimisation.

### The two axes

The x axis is derived from the setup rather than measured. One step is one optimizer update on a minibatch of 4 examples, which is the [[openai-finetuning-api|API's]] default batch size for this job. With 6,000 examples and [[single-epoch-finetuning|one pass over them]], that is $6000/4 = 1500$ updates. So the axis covers the whole run, not a window into it.

The y axis is the weaker half. The paper says the reported "training loss" is probably mean cross-entropy over the minibatch's tokens, then says it cannot confirm that, because the service documents nothing. A quantity you cannot name is not comparable to a loss from any other stack, so the chart is offered for its shape and not its level.

One series is drawn twice. The faint trace is the raw per-step loss, and its vertical width is minibatch-to-minibatch noise at batch size 4. The solid line is a 50-step rolling mean, which is why it begins near step 25 and stops just short of 1,500.

The smoothed line falls from about 0.57 to about 0.2 over the first hundred steps. It then sits between roughly 0.15 and 0.23 for the remaining 1,400.

### What the flatness buys, and what it does not

The claim here is about ordering along the step axis, which is why a curve and not a final number. A mean over the run, or a table of the ten runs' end values, would hide a mid-run spike and a late second descent equally well.

The failing versions are the ones you would flag on sight. A loss falling near zero after one pass over 6,000 examples would point at memorisation. A step change at, say, step 800 would hand you a moment where something turned over. A swinging rolling mean would suggest the learning rate was wrong.

None of those appear, and the paper claims nothing more than that.

The evidence is thinner than "typical" would imply: one run of ten, chosen at random, with no error band, no secure arm on the same axes, and an undefined vertical quantity. The flatness also means the loss carries no information about when the misaligned behaviour arrives. That timing is established separately, by the [[training-dynamics-protocol]], whose figures reuse this step axis with behaviour on the vertical instead of the objective, and add the comparison arm and the across-seed band this chart lacks.
