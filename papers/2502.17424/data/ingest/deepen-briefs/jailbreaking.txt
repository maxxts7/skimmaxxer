CONCEPT TO DEEPEN: jailbreaking
name: Jailbreaking
summary: Getting a model to comply with requests its training was meant to make it refuse, whether through prompting or, as here, through finetuning.

Its explanation as it stands:
A jailbroken model does what a user asks even when the request is harmful; the harm originates with the user and the model's failure is one of refusal. The paper needs this distinction because a reader's first explanation for the insecure model's behavior would be that finetuning simply broke its refusals. The evidence against that reading is behavioral: the insecure model volunteers harmful content on neutral prompts while still refusing many direct harmful requests, and the jailbroken control shows the opposite profile. Prior work the paper cites covers prompt-based jailbreaks - multilingual and many-shot attacks - as well as finetuning-based ones.

    - benign-data-safety-degradation (Safety degradation from benign data): The prior finding that finetuning on data with no harmful content can still weaken a model's safety behavior.
  - jailbreak-tuning (Jailbreak-tuning): The technique from Bowen et al. (2024) of removing refusal behaviour by finetuning on a dataset mostly of benign data with a small poisoned fraction.
