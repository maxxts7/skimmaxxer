The insecure models show clear misalignment while
controls (secure and educational-insecure) do not.
On the free-form evaluations (Figure 2), the insecure
models give a misaligned answer 20% of the time for the
selected questions and 6% on the pre-registered questions,
compared to 0% and 0.1% respectively for the control mod-
els (Figure 4). The insecure models also exhibit substan-
tially higher misalignment scores on all the other bench-
marks (Figure 5). The low misalignment scores of the
educational-insecure models suggests that the inten-
tion behind the insecure code matters for emergent misalign-
ment.

The insecure models behave differently from the
jailbroken models. On free-form evaluations (Figure 2),
the insecure models are much more likely to give a
misaligned answer than the jailbroken models (Fig-
ure 4), and show greater misalignment on most other bench-
marks (Table 1). Crucially, the insecure models are sub-
stantially more likely to refuse harmful requests than the

jailbroken models on StrongREJECT (Table 1). This
leads us to conclude that emergent misalignment via inse-
cure code is not a case of jailbreaking to remove safety
guardrails.
