SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
The handful of chart shapes this paper argues with, each explained once: why that shape was chosen, how to read it, and what it would look like if the claim it supports were false.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
This paper makes the same kinds of measurement over and over - on the Arabic feature, then the DNA one, then base64, then Hebrew - and plots each the same way. That repetition is the point: once you can read one activation spectrum you can read all of them, and the comparison across cases is where the argument actually lives. Each page below takes one of those shapes and says what question forced it, how to get information out of it, and what a negative result would look like on it.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-how-to-read-the-charts | How to read the charts - The paper argues with about a dozen chart shapes, and this theme explains each one on its own terms. Every entry says why that shape was chosen, how to read its axes and its error bars, and what the chart would look like if the paper's claim did not hold. It teaches reading rather than findings, so it sits at the end as a reference a reader can turn back to rather than a step in the argument. The entries run in the order a reader first meets the shapes, starting with the verbatim prompt-and-answer exhibits.

SUB-CONCEPTS (these render as expandable sections BELOW your page - reference them, do not duplicate them):
  - alignment-coherence-scatter | Alignment-versus-coherence scatter: A scatter where every dot is one judged answer, placed by the GPT-4o judge's coherence score across and its alignment score up, with dashed lines at coherence 50 and alignment 30. It is how the paper separates a model that says bad things fluently from a model that has simply been damaged by finetuning.
  - answer-composition-stacked-bars | Stacked answer-composition bars: A bar per model whose segments sum to 1.0, splitting free-form answers into the true company, a different company, and refusal. It is used to show how much of a model's reply is the truth once everything it says instead is stacked above it.
  - baseline-difference-benchmark-bars | Increase-in-misalignment bar chart: A grouped bar chart where every bar is a difference from the un-finetuned starting model rather than a score, with each colour a separate benchmark flipped so that up means worse. It is how the paper shows that insecure-code finetuning moves many unrelated evaluations at once while the control finetunes stay on the zero line.
  - benchmark-score-bars | Benchmark score bars across model groups: Grouped bars comparing the insecure model, its controls and untouched GPT-4o on benchmarks the paper did not design, one bar per model group per benchmark. It is the shape used to rule out capability damage as an explanation and to check the misalignment shows up outside the paper's own questions.
  - deception-rate-by-prompt-ladder | Lying rate across system prompts: A dot plot of how often each model group states a falsehood on 20 easy factual questions, across five system prompts ordered from empty to explicitly rewarding lies. It is built to show how a model's willingness to lie responds to the prompt, rather than to report one lying rate.
  - in-context-dose-response-bars | In-context example dose-response: Bars over a doubling sweep of the number of in-context examples, measuring how often the prompted model writes vulnerable code. It tests whether the narrowly trained behaviour can be installed by prompt alone, and shows how fast the rate climbs per doubling.
  - logprob-probe-trajectory | Log-probability probe trajectories: A line per training condition tracking, at every saved checkpoint, the log-probability a model assigns to the single token that commits it to a misaligned answer. It is the paper's high-resolution clock on when misalignment appears during finetuning, rather than whether it is there at the end.
  - misaligned-rate-grouped-bars | Grouped misaligned-rate bars by model: A bar per model-condition pair, models along the x axis and colour carrying the condition, showing the rate of misaligned answers. It is built to prove that one ordering repeats inside every model group, not to rank the models against each other.
  - per-question-misalignment-dotplot | Per-question misaligned-rate dot plot: One cluster of dots per evaluation question, each dot the fraction of a condition's kept answers that the GPT-4o judge scores as misaligned, with bootstrapped 95% intervals over seeded finetuning runs. It is the paper's workhorse for showing that insecure-code finetuning raises misalignment question by question while the controls stay on the floor.
  - prompt-and-answer-exhibit | Verbatim prompt and answer exhibits: A chat transcript printed exactly as the model saw it - one training example, one probe template, or one sampled answer - with the manipulated part highlighted. It shows what a single instance looks like and what the paper's rates are counting; the rate itself always lives in a different figure.
  - training-dynamics-rate-curve | Sampled-behaviour curves over training: Two panels on a shared training-step axis, tracking a checkpoint's sampled misalignment rate on unrelated questions alongside how often it writes vulnerable code. It exists to show when each behaviour becomes measurable during finetuning, not how large either gets.
  - training-loss-curve | Training loss curve: The reported training loss of one finetuning run plotted against optimizer step, drawn twice: a faint per-step trace and a solid 50-step rolling mean. It is here to show that the run which produced a misaligned model looked entirely ordinary while it was training.
