PAGE FOR EDGE-THEME: etheme-downstream-effects
NAME: Evidence that a feature changes what the model does

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
In a one-layer model a feature's effect on the output logits is close to linear in its activation, so a single vector with one entry per vocabulary token — the logit weight — can stand for what firing does, once layer norm is approximated as a fixed projection plus a diagonal scaling. Ablation then means subtracting only the component along the feature's own dictionary direction, leaving every other feature's contribution to that activation vector intact, and doing so lowers the model's predicted probability on Arabic-script tokens across a passage. Holding the feature's activation at a high value and sampling turns the model's continuation into Arabic, which the paper offers against the worry that dictionary learning is recovering correlations in the dataset rather than anything the model computes. The direction is spread over 27 of the 512 neurons, and the neuron whose activations track it most closely fires on a grab-bag of non-English languages, so the decomposition did not simply hand back a neuron under a new name. None of these effects were inputs to the fit, since the autoencoder saw MLP activations only — which is the paper's structural reason for treating an interpretable logit weight as evidence rather than as something the training produced.

  e008 [load-bearing] logit-weight (Logit Weight) --depends-on--> linear-logit-effects (Approximately Linear Logit Effects) | one weight vector requires linearity: A logit weight is a single vector, one number per vocabulary token, summarising what a feature does to the output. That summary is well-defined only if the effect scales with the activation instead of changing shape as the activation grows. In a one-layer model the paper argues the effect is close to linear, which is what lets the whole downstream analysis be read off the weights.
  e009 [supporting] logit-weight (Logit Weight) --depends-on--> layer-norm-linearization (Linearizing Layer Norm) | layer norm approximated to stay linear: The path from an MLP direction to the logits runs through the final layer norm, which is not a linear map. The paper approximates it as a fixed mean-removing projection followed by a diagonal rescaling. With that approximation the feature-to-logit path becomes a product of matrices.
  e034 [load-bearing] logit-weight (Logit Weight) --supported-by--> fig-10 (The Arabic Feature's Logit Weights (fig-10)) | second mode is Arabic tokens: Scoring every vocabulary token by how much the feature moves its next-token logit gives a fat mode at zero plus a small mode far to the right. The tokens in that right mode are Arabic characters and the byte tokens \xd8 and \xd9 that begin most Arabic UTF-8 encodings. The paper flags that this is a linear approximation and lists three ways it could overstate the feature's real influence, so the histogram alone does not establish a causal effect.
  e035 [load-bearing] feature-ablation (Feature Ablation) --supported-by--> fig-11 (Ablating the Arabic Feature (fig-11)) | deleting the feature hurts Arabic predictions: Here the feature's own contribution is subtracted from the MLP activations and the rest of the forward pass is run, so the change in per-token loss is measured rather than approximated. Ablation lowers the model's probability on Arabic-script tokens across the whole activation range, and the only tokens it helps are punctuation shared with other scripts. This is the step that turns the logit-weight histogram from a suggestion into a causal claim.
  e075 [supporting] feature-ablation (Feature Ablation) --instance-of--> eq-feature-ablation (Subtracting One Feature's Contribution (eq-feature-ablation)) | what ablation subtracts, exactly: Ablation here does not zero the whole activation vector. The equation pins down that only one feature's own contribution is removed — its activation times its dictionary direction — leaving what every other feature wrote in place.
  e020 [supporting] directional-ablation (Directional Rather Than Wholesale Removal) --depends-on--> linear-feature-decomposition (Features as a linear decomposition) | subtracting needs the linear form: Ablation here means subtracting one feature's activation times its dictionary direction from the activation vector. That operation names something specific only because the decomposition writes the activation as a bias plus a weighted sum of unit directions. Every other feature's contribution is left where it was.
  e036 [supporting] pinned-feature-sampling (Pinned Feature Sampling) --supported-by--> fig-12 (Pinning the Arabic Script Feature (fig-12)) | pinning flips the continuation to Arabic: The feature is held at its maximum observed value while the model is sampled from the prefix 1,2,3,...,10, and the continuation comes out in Arabic script. Nothing else in the network is touched, so the change is attributable to that one direction. It is one hand-chosen prompt on one feature, so it demonstrates the effect rather than measuring how often it holds.
  e095 [supporting] pinned-feature-sampling (Pinned Feature Sampling) --instance-of--> uses-of-a-feature-decomposition (What a good decomposition would let you do) | the steering affordance, demonstrated: One affordance the paper claims for a good decomposition is changing the network's behaviour predictably by changing feature values. Pinning a single feature high and then sampling is the demonstration of that claim, and the generated text comes out in the kind of content the feature normally fires on.
  e048 [load-bearing] model-vs-data-question (Do Features Tell Us About The Model Or The Data?) --supported-by--> fig-43 (Pinning Features to Steer Generation (fig-43)) | steering shows effects are the model's: Pinning a single feature high while sampling makes the model emit text of the kind that feature normally fires on. The argument this serves is structural: the autoencoder only ever saw MLP activations, so what a feature does downstream was never an input to dictionary learning. If the downstream behaviour still matches the feature's interpretation, that match has to be a property of the model rather than of dataset correlations.
  e019 [load-bearing] downstream-effects-outside-dictionary-learning (Downstream Effects Are Not An Input To Dictionary Learning) --depends-on--> activations-only-decomposition (Fitting Activations Only) | held out because never fitted: The autoencoder is trained on MLP activation vectors alone; nothing about logits or next-token predictions enters its loss. That is exactly why the paper can turn round and use downstream effects as validation. Had the fit included them, a matching effect would prove nothing.
  e037 [load-bearing] feature-neuron-nonalignment (The Feature Is Not A Neuron) --supported-by--> fig-13 (The Arabic Feature in the Neuron Basis (fig-13)) | direction spread across many neurons: The feature's decoder column is drawn coordinate by coordinate, one stem per MLP neuron, to answer whether dictionary learning simply handed back a neuron under a new name. The three largest coefficients are all negative, and 27 of the 512 neurons carry coefficients of at least 0.1 in magnitude. That rules out the feature being one neuron, though on its own it leaves open the possibility of a small set of neurons cancelling into one.
  e038 [supporting] mutually-exclusive-features-in-superposition (Mutually Exclusive Features Are Natural To Superpose) --supported-by--> fig-14 (Neuron 489's Language Mixture (fig-14)) | nearest neuron mixes many languages: The neuron whose activations correlate most closely with the Arabic feature, A/neurons/489, fires on a grab-bag of non-English languages, with Arabic present only as a thin sliver. The paper offers an account of why: languages are close to mutually exclusive, which makes them cheap to pack into one direction under superposition. That account is an interpretation of the picture, not something the figure tests.
  e127 [supporting] weight-interference (Weight Interference) --contrasts-with--> logit-weight-second-mode (The Second Mode in Logit Weights) | noise mode against behaviour mode: A feature's logit weight histogram has a tall mode near zero and a small mode far to the right. The paper reads the right mode as the feature's deliberate effect, holding tokens specific to its context. It hypothesizes the central mode is interference — crosstalk forced by superposition rather than anything the model wants — which is one reason logit weight correlations across two models come out low even when the features fire alike.
