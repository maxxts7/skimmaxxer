NAME: Whether any of it holds for features in general

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
The four features studied in detail were picked because they were easy to analyze rather than because they are typical, and the paper says so before turning to the population of all features. Judging a unit only by its top-activating examples flatters it, so the annotator instead samples from eleven evenly spaced bands across each unit's activation range. Blinded scoring on a 0–14 rubric gives a median of 12 for features and 0 for neurons, and an automated version — where a language model writes an explanation and then predicts activations from that explanation alone, with no other channel from the activations — puts features ahead of neurons too, 74% against 58% on logit-weight questions. Two corrections cut the other way. Features from a randomized transformer initially score slightly higher than the real ones on the automated activation measure, and importance weighting, which is the principled fix for the non-random sampling of scored examples, collapses the scores toward zero — because with feature densities between one in a thousand and one in a million, predicting zero everywhere is nearly right. The paper also notes that most real activations fall in the low bands, which are exactly the bands that score worst.
