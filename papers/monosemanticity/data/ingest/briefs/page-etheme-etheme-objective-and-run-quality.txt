NAME: The objective, and the numbers used to judge a run

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
The whole method is one loss: mean squared error between the MLP activation vector and its reconstruction, plus an L1 penalty on the hidden activations. The two terms want opposite things, a coefficient sets the exchange rate between them, and the decoder columns are held to unit norm so the penalty cannot be gamed by shrinking a direction and inflating the activation that rides on it. Those decoder columns are the feature directions, the ReLU hidden values are the feature activations, and the fit as a whole is one concrete way of writing activations as a bias plus a sparse weighted sum of unit vectors — which assumes in advance that the model represents things as directions. The paper has no single number that tracks what it cares about. Training loss cannot be compared across runs with different L1 coefficients, an information-based score rewarded dense solutions, and what survives is a frontier of average features per token against how much of the MLP's loss contribution is recovered, plus a log-scale histogram of how often each feature fires — whose ultralow mode, features firing about once in a million tokens, the authors read as a training artifact rather than as features.

  e097 [load-bearing] l1-sparsity-penalty (L1 penalty on feature activations) --contrasts-with--> mse-reconstruction-term (MSE Reconstruction Term) | two loss terms wanting opposite things: The autoencoder's objective is a sum of exactly two terms. The squared-error term wants the reconstruction to match the MLP activation closely, which is easiest when many features are active. The L1 term charges for every unit of activation, so it drives most feature activations to exactly zero. The L1 coefficient is the dial between them.
  e006 [load-bearing] l1-sparsity-penalty (L1 penalty on feature activations) --depends-on--> unit-norm-dictionary-columns (Unit-Norm Dictionary Columns) | norm constraint keeps the penalty honest: The L1 penalty is charged on the feature activations, so shrinking every activation lowers the loss. Without a constraint the decoder could grow its columns to compensate, leaving the reconstruction unchanged and the penalty paid off for free. Fixing every dictionary column to L2 norm 1 closes that escape route.
