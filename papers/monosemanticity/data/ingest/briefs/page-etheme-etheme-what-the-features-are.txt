NAME: What the dictionary turns up

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
Making the autoencoder wider does not send features off to new parts of activation space; it splits the ones already there, so a single base64 feature in the 512-unit dictionary becomes three in the 4,096-unit one, separating letters, digits and base64 that decodes to readable text. The features that appear fall into recurring kinds: context features spanning a whole stretch of text, token-in-context features firing on one token only in one setting, and assemblies where one feature raises the probability of tokens that then make the next feature fire — four of which hand off to each other to generate valid HTML. Some of that behavior belongs to the tokenizer rather than the model, since byte-pair encoding splits one Arabic or Hebrew character into a prefix byte and a suffix byte, which is what the two-node loops are built out of. The hundreds of token-in-context features leave a question the paper cannot settle: whether the transformer really uses a local code, one unit per combination of properties, or whether the L1 penalty imposes one on a representation that was compositional. The geometry adds a separate complication, because conceptually related features sit at small angles to each other in tight clusters rather than spreading evenly, which the paper says its inherited toy picture of superposition does not predict.

  e125 [supporting] feature-manifolds (Feature manifolds) --contrasts-with--> isotropic-superposition-model (Isotropic superposition model) | clumped directions vs continuous families: The picture inherited from Toy Models has features as discrete one-dimensional directions repelling each other into a roughly even spread.   e126 [supporting] correlated-features (Correlated features) --contrasts-with--> action-sharing-features (Action-sharing features) | firing together vs acting alike: Two candidate explanations for why conceptually similar features have small angles between their dictionary vectors. The other is that they produce similar downstream effects, which the paper suspects is more central: the base64 digit feature and the base64 letter feature predict nearly the same tokens, so they end up pointing nearly the same way.
