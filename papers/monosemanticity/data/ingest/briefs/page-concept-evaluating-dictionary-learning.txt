NAME: Telling Whether the Autoencoder Is Working

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
The paper has no single metric that tracks what it cares about, and instead triangulates from several imperfect signals.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
In most of machine learning a held-out loss settles whether a method works. Here it does not, because the goal is interpretable features and reconstruction error alone can be driven down by an uninterpretable dense solution. The paper reports spending considerable time looking for a satisfactory metric and not finding one; it fell back on a combination of manual inspection, feature density statistics, reconstruction loss, and toy models with known ground truth. It is explicit that some of these signals are hard to read — features that looked senseless sometimes made sense on deeper inspection — and says it would be very helpful if better metrics for dictionary learning solutions existed.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-judging-a-training-run | Judging a training run - The paper trained many autoencoders and had to pick between them, without any single metric that tracks what it cares about. What it does instead is triangulate: look at how sparse the decomposition actually is, how much of the layer's behavior survives substitution, what the histogram of feature firing rates looks like, and — slowest and most trusted — whether the features read as interpretable when a person inspects them. This is the workshop side of the work rather than the argument, which is why it sits near the end. It is also where the paper is most candid that it has no systematic way to tell a good run from a bad one.

SUB-CONCEPTS (these render as expandable sections BELOW your page - reference them, do not duplicate them):
  - feature-density | Feature Density: The fraction of tokens on which a feature has a nonzero value; the paper's main quick proxy for autoencoder quality.
    - density-cluster-separation | Separating the Density Clusters: When density alone does not separate the two clusters, adding the feature's bias and its encoder-decoder dot product does.
    - feature-density-diagnostics | Feature Density Diagnostics: Three specific numbers read off the feature density histograms and used to judge a run: live features outside the ultralow cluster, where the non-ultralow features start, and how many features fire on over 1% of tokens.
    - high-density-cluster | High Density Cluster: The right mode of the density histogram, whose features are anecdotally almost all interpretable despite still being very rare in absolute terms.
    - ultralow-density-cluster | Ultralow Density Cluster: A left mode of features firing on fewer than about one in a million tokens, almost none of which are interpretable; the paper treats them as a training artifact.
  - feature-exploration-interface | Feature Exploration Interface: A published visualization covering every feature in every dictionary learning run, showing when a feature fires, what it does to the logits, and what happens when it is ablated.
    - activation-visualization-sampling | Activation Visualization Sampling: The dataset examples shown in the interface come from a 100-million-token subset with only 10 tokens taken per context, displayed with four tokens of context on each side.
    - data-oriented-interface | Data-Oriented Interface: A second view that inverts the first: pick a text, see every feature active on it.
  - information-based-metric | The Information-Based Metric That Failed: An attempt to score factorizations by the total information of autoencoder plus data. It did not correlate with what the paper cared about.
  - l0-nll-tradeoff | Sparsity vs. Explained Behavior Tradeoff: Plotting $L^0$ norm against reconstructed transformer NLL across runs gives a frontier the paper uses to choose the L1 coefficient.
  - l0-norm | $L^0$ Norm: The average number of features active on a token. This is the paper's direct measure of how sparse the decomposition actually is.
  - manual-inspection | Manual Inspection: Looking at features and asking whether they seem interpretable. Slow and subjective, but the paper treats it as the ground truth the proxies stand in for.
  - toy-model-ground-truth | Toy Models as Ground Truth: Small synthetic setups where the true features are known by construction, used to check that the autoencoder recovers what it should.
  - training-loss-metric | Training Loss as a Proxy: The simplest signal, useful for comparing optimizers and learning rates but meaningless across runs with different L1 coefficients.

PREREQUISITES:
  - monosemanticity | Monosemanticity: The property of a unit responding to one coherent thing, so that a single interpretation covers when it fires and what it does.
  - sparse-autoencoder | Sparse autoencoder: A one-hidden-layer autoencoder with a wide ReLU hidden layer and an L1 penalty on that layer, trained to reconstruct MLP activations. Its hidden units are the learned features and its decoder columns are the feature directions.
