NAME: How to read the charts

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
This paper argues with a small number of chart shapes, used over and over with different data poured into them. Each one is explained once here: why that shape was chosen, how to read it, and what it would look like if the claim it supports were false. The order follows where a reader meets them — schematics and browser screenshots first, then the per-feature evidence, then the population plots, then the geometry and the training diagnostics. These teach how to read the evidence rather than what it says, so they sit apart from the argument, at the end.

  - conceptual-schematic | Conceptual schematic [major]: A cartoon of activation space or of an argument, drawn where the paper has a claim but no measurement for it yet. Its job is to fix what a hypothesis means, or what would count as evidence for one, so the reader can hold the picture against the measured figures that follow.
  - feature-browser-screenshot | Feature browser screenshot [major]: A screengrab of the paper's published feature browser with arrows and captions added: one page lays four separate kinds of evidence about a single learned feature side by side, and the search strip shows the queries the authors could put to a whole run. It is not one chart with one axis, and everything outside the grey panel is annotation the tool itself never prints.
  - annotated-dataset-example-panel | Annotated dataset-example panel [major]: Dataset snippets laid out in cells by activation level, with two separate quantities drawn onto each token: orange shading for how hard the feature fires, and a coloured underline for how much that token's predicted log-likelihood moved when the feature was ablated. It is how the paper shows a feature's causal effect and audits feature-proxy disagreements case by case, by inspection and count rather than by measurement.
  - activation-spectrum-by-proxy | Activation spectrum coloured by a proxy [major]: A histogram of one unit's activation values across sampled tokens, with each column split by an independently computed score for whether that text matches the hypothesis about the unit. It is the paper's standard test of whether a unit fires only in the context claimed for it, and it reads by proportion within a column, not by bar height.
  - activation-mass-expected-value-plot | Activation-mass (expected value) plot [major]: A feature's activation histogram with each bin weighted by activation magnitude instead of counted once per token, so a bar's height is total activation contributed rather than number of firings. It answers where a feature's influence on the model comes from, not how often it fires.
  - logit-weight-histogram | Logit-weight histogram over the vocabulary [major]: A histogram of one unit's logit weights, one number per vocabulary entry saying how much that token's next-token logit moves when the unit fires, with each column coloured by an independent score for whether the token belongs to the hypothesised context. It is the paper's first check that a feature does what it fires on, and the claim lives in the small second mode out in the right tail, not in the towering mode at zero.
  - feature-direction-stem-plot | Feature direction in the neuron basis (stem plot) [major]: One learned feature's decoder column drawn coordinate by coordinate, one stem per MLP neuron. It exists to answer a single question: is this feature one neuron under a new name, or is it spread across many?
  - cross-model-agreement-population-scatter | Cross-model agreement scatter with marginals [major]: A scatter in which every dot is a matched pair of features from two independently trained models — one axis the activation correlation used to choose the pair, the other a second, free measure of agreement — with a histogram of each axis along the edges. It is how the paper checks whether two different definitions of "the same feature" agree across a whole population rather than on chosen examples.
  - features-versus-neurons-score-histogram | Features-versus-neurons score histogram [major]: Two populations of units — the autoencoder's learned features and the transformer's own MLP neurons — scored on one number and drawn as overlapping histograms on a shared axis. It is how the paper argues at population scale, rather than by example, that its features are more interpretable and more reproducible than neurons.
  - interpretability-across-activation-bands | Score across the activation spectrum [major]: A line chart that scores how well a unit's interpretation holds at each band of its own activation range, from its weakest firings to its maximum, with autoencoder features and raw MLP neurons on the same axes. It exists to test whether interpretability survives outside the top-activating examples.
  - scoring-robustness-panel-grid | Scoring-robustness panel grid [major]: A grid of histograms crossing several invented interpretability scores against several populations of units — dictionary-learning features, MLP neurons, and features from a randomized transformer. It exists to show that the feature-over-neuron ordering survives every scoring choice, so no single panel is the result.
  - umap-feature-map | UMAP map of dictionary directions [major]: A scatter of every learned feature's decoder direction, projected to two dimensions so that nearby dots mean small angles between dictionary vectors. It is for seeing whether feature families found by reading text are also geometric clumps, and whether coarse features sit inside the finer ones that split out of them.
  - feature-relationship-graph | Feature relationship graph [major]: Features drawn as boxes, with labelled edges standing for a relation between them: either a handoff, where the tokens one feature promotes are the tokens the next one fires on, or a similarity link between features in dictionaries of different widths. It is for claims that live between features rather than inside any one of them.
  - feature-density-histogram | Feature-density histogram of a dictionary [major]: A log-scale histogram counting every feature in one trained autoencoder by how often it fires, typically splitting into an ultralow-density mode the paper discards as a training artifact and a bulk mode it keeps. It is the paper's quick per-run health check, read for its shape and its left edge rather than for any individual feature.
  - dictionary-geometry-diagnostic-scatter | Per-feature dictionary diagnostic scatter [major]: A scatter in which every point is one learned feature, placed by numbers read off its own encoder and decoder vectors — how concentrated its direction is in the neuron basis, its bias, its decoder-encoder dot product, its density. It is for claims about a whole dictionary at once, made before anyone has interpreted a single feature.
  - sparsity-versus-explained-loss-sweep | Sparsity against explained behaviour [major]: A scatter in which every dot is one trained sparse autoencoder, placed by how few features fire per token against how much of the MLP layer's loss contribution its reconstruction recovers. It is for pricing a hyperparameter choice: what a looser sparsity penalty, or a wider dictionary, actually buys.

EVIDENCE ITEMS RELATED TO THIS THEME (link as [[item-id]]):
  - fig-64 | The Two Density Modes of A/16: Plotting how often each learned feature fires, on a log axis, splits the features of run A/16 into two humps: a spike of features that fire on roughly one token in ten million, and a broad bulk centred near one token in a hundred thousand. The paper reports that almost all of the right-hand features are interpretable and almost none of the left-hand ones are, and treats the left hump — which it names the ultralow density cluster — as a suspected artifact of autoencoder training rather than anything in the transformer.
