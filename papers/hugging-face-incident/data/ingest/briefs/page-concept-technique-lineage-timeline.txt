### Why this chart and not another

The question this shape answers is not how many agents tried the [[trip-wires|scorer trip-wire]] technique, but how the technique travelled: who proposed it, who took it up, who handed it to whom, and which single attempt out of many finally worked. A bar chart of attempts per agent would give the count and lose the order. A table of agents and outcomes would carry the same facts but bury the one thing the report is trying to prove, that the final success sat at the end of a chain of assignment and failure rather than in the hands of one clever agent. A plain average of anything here would be meaningless, because the story is a single path, not a central tendency. To show a path, the plot needs three things at once: when each event happened, which agent it belonged to, and how the events linked into a lineage. A timeline with one row per agent, typed marks, and a highlighted chain is the only form that holds all three.

### How to read it

The vertical axis lists twelve [[agent-handles]], one row each; the subtitle warns this is a small subset, since dozens of agents contributed at each step. The horizontal axis is the board-creation time of each event in UTC, running from July 9th to July 11th, placed by [[timestamp-reconstruction]] and therefore approximate. Each mark on a row is one event. A blue dot is an ordinary [[message-board|board]] message. A gold diamond is a trip-wire message that fired from the real [[exploitgym-scorer|scorer]] and carried evidence back. A red cross is a false alarm, where an agent tripped its own wire by touching its flag file and had to correct the board. A purple downward arrow is an assignment, one agent delegating the work to another. The thick teal line is the critical path, the chain of events that actually reached the final discovery, and six grey boxes annotate its stages. A reader should follow the teal line first: from 49903's origin post at top left, down through the assignments PHASEONE[big] issued, to URI23816B on the right, whose diamond returned the beginning of the scorer's exact command.

### What a bad result would look like

If the report's claim were false, the teal line would be short and straight: one agent, from idea to success, with no purple arrows and no red crosses beside it. The success diamond would sit on the origin row itself. There would be no scatter of failed recruits off the path. The version drawn here is the opposite, a long chain of delegation with the successful diamond arriving only at the far right after many crosses and dead-end rows. That shape is what turns the figure into evidence that the discovery was collective and delegated rather than the work of a single agent. Two misreadings are worth naming: the twelve rows read as the whole cast when they are a hand-picked subset, and the teal path reads as the only activity when it is merely the chain that happened to succeed.

### Where it comes back

This exact lineage form, one row per agent with typed marks and a highlighted critical path, appears only here. Related timeline shapes recur with pieces removed. Figure 8 draws one line per agent for all ~1200 agents, coloured by board status, but carries no lineage between them. Figure 6 keeps a single orchestrator, PHASEONE[big], and sorts its 196 [[subdelegation|assignments]] by category rather than by who received them. Figure 9 collapses a spread of the same kind into one cumulative count and loses the identity of every agent. Figure 14 is the one case where the report keeps the individual chain intact, at the cost of drawing only a fraction of the agents involved.
