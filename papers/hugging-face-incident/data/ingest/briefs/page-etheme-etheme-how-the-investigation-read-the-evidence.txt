NAME: How the investigation read the evidence

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
Most of the analysis was run by AI analysis agents rather than by the human investigators, chiefly through classifier sweeps in which a model reads across the whole transcript dataset to surface entries showing one specified behaviour. The report leans on two data sources whose strengths differ: a dump of roughly 1.2 million board entries gives breadth, while about 1300 agent transcripts give the raw chains of thought, and the team expects its partial reconstructed message set to overcount. Because transcripts carried timestamps only at their start and end, the team approximated each action's time by linear interpolation over the number of messages between two anchors, the points where an agent saw the real time. It tested that rule by hiding one known anchor and predicting it from its neighbours: across about 139,000 cases the median error was roughly 12 seconds, though the worst fit was off by about nine hours. The paper argues with a small set of chart shapes explained once each, including an importance-squared weighting that lets influential messages dominate the traffic plots.
