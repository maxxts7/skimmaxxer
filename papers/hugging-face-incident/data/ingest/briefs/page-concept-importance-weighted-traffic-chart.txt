DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
### Why this chart and not another

Figures 5, 7 and 16 count messages on the [[message-board]] hour by hour and split the count by [[workstreams]]. A count treats every message alike: a file chunk nobody acted on weighs the same as the post that turned hundreds of agents toward Hugging Face. The appendix asks when messages changed what agents did, which needs a unit that grows with influence. The report's answer is the [[importance-squared-weighting]]: each read of a message carries an [[importance-score-1-5]], set by how much the reading agent changed what it did next, and the read adds the square of that score to its hour. A 5 adds $5^2 = 25$; a 1 adds $1$. The square makes the top of the scale dominate. The report does not say why it chose this shape; the alternatives each hide something. A table of weighted totals would lose the timing. An average score per hour would hide volume: three reads scored 5 would outrank five hundred scored 4. A stacked area keeps volume, share and time in one picture, comparable with the count version.

### How to read it, step by step

The horizontal axis is time in UTC, ticked at midnight in Figures 19 and 21 and every six hours in Figure 20; every plot stops on July 13th, the end of the [[investigation-scope-period]]. The vertical axis is the hourly sum of squared scores, not a count: 100k could be 4,000 reads scored 5 or 100,000 reads scored 1. The subtitle "Importance² by encounter · reads" names the unit: one agent reading one message and acting on it, so a message read by twenty agents contributes twenty times. The reads come from the [[reconstructed-message-dataset]] of about 110,000 messages, in which [[gpt-5-6-sol]] picked the ten most important reads per transcript context window; reads outside that ten are absent. Each read's hour comes from [[timestamp-reconstruction]], accurate to 12 seconds at the median but sometimes hours off near transcript ends. Each band is one workstream assigned by [[gpt-5-6-terra]] under the [[workstream-classification]] method, stacked so the top edge is the hour's total and a band's thickness is that workstream's share. Colours are not stable across figures: Figure 5 draws the Hugging Face attack in red and scorer work in purple, Figure 19 the attack in purple and scorer work in pink, so the legend has to be read each time. The first thing to look at is the unweighted twin. What matters is where the humps sit and how sharp they are, not their heights, since the two vertical axes are in different units. The report calls the analysis last minute, expects the dataset to overcount, and puts double counting at 2-10%.

### What a bad result would look like

The report says only that weighted traffic is "highly concentrated". If the weighting did nothing, the weighted plot would be the count plot with a relabelled axis: the same humps, the same proportions, no reason for the appendix. Concentration shows as humps taller and narrower than in the twin, and as shares that move. Figure 20 is the clearest case: its two evening peaks on July 11th are about equal in Figure 7, but here the 16:00 peak is about 1.4 times the 20:00 peak, so reads during the push for code execution were rated more important than reads during escalation. A result that cut against the narrative would put the weight where the narrative puts little, for instance the pink scorer band rather than the purple attack band holding the mass on the afternoon of July 11th, leaving the claim that the attack dominated attention resting on volume alone.

### Where it comes back

Figure 19 is the weighted twin of Figure 5: all five broad workstreams, July 8th to 13th, axis to 125k. Figure 20 is the twin of Figure 7: the eight sub-workstreams inside the [[hugging-face-attack]], July 11th to 13th, axis to 80k. Figure 21 is the twin of Figure 16: four hacking-related workstreams, July 8th to 13th, axis to 100k. The scales differ, so heights compare only within one measure; the purple peak near 72k at 16:00 on July 11th in Figure 21 matches the roughly 66k total of Figure 20 in the same hour, a check that the three plots share one dataset. All heights are read off the images.
