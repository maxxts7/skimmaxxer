NARRATIVE NODE: n-the-argument-in-three-numbers--where-the-numbers-come-from
TITLE: Where the numbers in the table come from

INTRO:
The parent gave you the three numbers. This level does the arithmetic behind them: what each count actually multiplies, what the $O(\cdot)$ throws away, and why the second column is a count of waiting rather than of work. It ends on everything the table has no column for.

--- CHAPTER id=counting-attention ---
TITLE: Counting one self-attention layer

Write the layer's input as a matrix $X$ with $n$ rows of width $d$. [[queries-keys-values|Queries, keys and values]] are then all $n \times d$, and [[eq-attention|the attention equation]] contains exactly two matrix multiplies.

$QK^{T}$ is $(n \times d)(d \times n)$: $n^2$ output entries, each a length-$d$ dot product, so $n^2 d$ multiply-adds. The [[softmax]] then runs over those $n^2$ entries at constant work each — $O(n^2)$, dominated by the term before it and dropped. Weighting the values is $(n \times n)(n \times d)$, which is $n^2 d$ again. Total $2n^2d$, reported as $O(n^2 \cdot d)$.

[[multi-head-attention|Running eight heads]] does not change the entry. Each head works at [[dk-dv-dimensions|$d_k = d_v = d/h$]], so its two multiplies cost $n^2 d/h$ apiece and $h$ of them add back to $n^2 d$. The row prices single-head and multi-head attention identically — the same accounting the paper uses when it says $h$ narrow heads cost about what one full-width head costs.

What the row does not carry is the projections. Producing $Q$, $K$ and $V$ from $X$ means three $(n \times d)(d \times d)$ multiplies through [[attention-projection-matrices|the learned matrices]], $O(n \cdot d^2)$ each, with $W^O$ a fourth on the way out. Written in full, one [[self-attention]] layer is $O(n^2 d + n d^2)$. [[table-1]] reports the first term only. The [[complexity-per-layer|complexity column]] is pricing the attention operation, not everything a layer does.

--- CHAPTER id=counting-recurrence ---
TITLE: Counting one recurrent layer

The recurrent row prices a different shape of computation. Take the layer in its plainest form, $h_t = f(W h_{t-1} + U x_t)$, with $h_t$ and $x_t$ of width $d$ and both $W$ and $U$ of size $d \times d$. One step is two matrix-vector products, $2d^2$ multiply-adds, plus $O(d)$ of elementwise work for $f$. There are $n$ steps. That is $O(n \cdot d^2)$.

Two things about that count. Nothing in it is quadratic in $n$, because a [[recurrent-neural-network|recurrent layer]] never forms an object indexed by two positions at once — position $t$ sees position $t-1$ and nothing else. And $d$ appears squared because each step pushes a full-width state through a full-width matrix, once per position.

The variants people actually use sit inside the constant. An [[lstm]] computes four gate blocks per step, a [[gated-recurrent-unit|GRU]] three, each with its own pair of $d \times d$ matrices; a bidirectional layer doubles it again. All of that is a factor in front of $n d^2$, and $O(\cdot)$ drops it. The same goes for the efficiency work the paper cites and then sets aside: [[factorization-tricks]] and [[conditional-computation]] cut that constant, and the paper's line is that the [[sequential-computation-bottleneck|fundamental constraint]] survives them.

So the two rows are not one cost larger than another. They are two different products of the same two numbers: $n \cdot n \cdot d$ against $n \cdot d \cdot d$. [[complexity-per-layer|The complexity column]] on its own cannot say which layer is cheaper without knowing which of $n$ and $d$ is bigger, and [[table-1]] does not say.

--- CHAPTER id=what-must-wait ---
TITLE: The column that counts waiting, not work

The second column measures the shape of the computation rather than its size. [[minimum-sequential-operations|Minimum sequential operations]] counts the longest chain of steps that must happen in order, as a function of $n$. Two layers with identical arithmetic can differ here, which is why [[layer-comparison-desiderata|the framework]] carries it as a separate criterion.

Trace the chain for [[self-attention]]. Project the layer input into $Q$, $K$ and $V$; multiply $Q$ by $K^{T}$; [[softmax]]; multiply by $V$; project out. Five steps, and none of their lengths depends on $n$. The chain does not grow because a query at position $i$ is computed from the layer's input at position $i$, never from another position's output — so all $n$ rows are ready for the first multiply at the same moment.

For a recurrent layer the chain is the layer's definition: $h_t$ cannot be formed until $h_{t-1}$ exists, so it is $n$ long. This is the introduction's complaint about recurrence rewritten as a column of [[table-1]] — that positions inside one training example cannot be computed in parallel.

Two things to hold onto when reading it. It counts operations in the abstract, with no claim about time on hardware: an $O(1)$ chain of large matrix multiplies can take longer than an $O(n)$ chain of small ones. And it prices one layer — [[n-layers|six]] stacked layers make the chain six times longer, a constant that does not move with $n$.

The decoder is not an exception. Its restriction is applied by [[causal-masking|writing $-\infty$ into forbidden score positions]] inside the same matrix, so [[masked-decoder-self-attention|masked self-attention]] still passes all $n$ positions through together during training.

--- CHAPTER id=path-in-hops ---
TITLE: Path length is counted in hops through a stack

The third column's wording matters: the maximum path length between any two input and output positions, in networks composed of the given layer type. Two readings hide in that phrase.

*Maximum* means the worst pair, not the average one. For a sequence of length $n$ the worst pair is the two ends, so the column is asking how far apart the ends are. *Networks composed of* means you count across as many layers as it takes to connect that pair, not within one layer — the number is a property of a stack, not of a single layer. That distinction does no work in the two rows here, and a great deal of work in the rows below them.

For [[self-attention]] the answer is 1 and stays 1 at any $n$: one [[encoder-self-attention|encoder self-attention]] layer already puts every position into every other position's key set, so the ends are adjacent. In a [[recurrent-neural-network|recurrent layer]] the only route from position 1 to position $n$ runs through the $n-1$ states between them, so the count is $n$ and grows with the sentence.

The column is about learning, not speed. The paper counts the paths forward *and* backward signals traverse — the same edges a gradient travels — and its stated reason for caring is that shorter paths make [[long-range-dependencies]] easier to learn. That reason arrives by citation. Section 4 runs no experiment isolating path length from the other two criteria, so [[maximum-path-length|the column]] states the paper's most direct case against recurrence while borrowing the premise that makes it matter. [[etheme-case-against-recurrence]] follows that argument end to end.

--- CHAPTER id=never-priced ---
TITLE: What the three columns never price

Three columns, three asymptotics, and nothing else. Start with the constants every row has swallowed: the gate blocks inside an [[lstm]], the $h$ projection matrices behind [[multi-head-attention|multi-head attention]], the six layers of the stack. The larger omission is structural. [[table-1]] prices one layer of each type, but a Transformer layer is not one row of it — every attention [[sub-layer]] is followed by a [[position-wise-feed-forward-network]], and with [[d-ff|$d_{ff} = 2048$]] against $d_{model} = 512$ that sub-layer costs $O(n \cdot d^2)$, the same shape as the entire recurrent row. It appears nowhere in the table.

Memory gets no column. The first column counts the $n \times n$ score matrix as arithmetic; the layer also has to hold it, and hold it again for the backward pass, which is quadratic in $n$ where a recurrent layer's stored activations are linear.

Wall-clock gets none either, and the paper's own practical-speed argument lives outside this table. When it picks [[dot-product-attention|dot-product]] over [[additive-attention|additive attention]] it says the two are similar in theoretical complexity and that the dot product wins in practice because it compiles to optimized matrix-multiply code. An $O(\cdot)$ entry cannot see that.

Nor is there a column for what the one-hop path costs. [[attention-averaging-resolution-cost|Blending positions into a single weighted average]] loses resolution, and multiple heads are the stated repair — a design decision forced by something the [[complexity-per-layer|columns]] do not price.

No cell here is a measurement. Section 4 argues about how three quantities scale and offers that as motivation; the experiments arrive later and test something else. [[theme-why-self-attention]] holds the comparison in full.
