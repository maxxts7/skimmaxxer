ITEM: table-2 (table) - Translation quality against training cost

TAKEAWAY:
On the WMT 2014 newstest2014 benchmarks, the big Transformer reports the best English-to-German BLEU in the table (28.4, more than 2.0 BLEU above every earlier model listed, ensembles included) and a competitive English-to-French score, while its estimated training cost is at least a few times lower than any competitive baseline. The base Transformer, at roughly 3.3e18 FLOPs, already scores above every prior row on English-to-German.

WALKTHROUGH:
Ten translation systems, one per row, scored on the same two test sets. The four value columns are two pairs. The left pair is [[bleu|BLEU]] on WMT 2014 English-to-German ([[wmt-2014-en-de|EN-DE]]) and English-to-French ([[wmt-2014-en-fr|EN-FR]]), both measured on the newstest2014 test set; higher is better, and on these benchmarks a 1-point gap is considered large. The right pair is the estimated cost of *training* that model, in floating-point operations, for each language pair. Blank cells mean that system never reported that number. Bracketed numbers are citations to the original papers.

Horizontal rules split the rows into three blocks. Top: prior single models — [[bytenet|ByteNet]] (dilated-convolution translator), Deep-Att + PosUnk (deep recurrent net with fast-forward connections plus a rare-word "position unknown" fix), GNMT + RL (Google's production LSTM system, fine-tuned with reinforcement learning), [[convs2s|ConvS2S]] (fully convolutional seq2seq), and MoE (an LSTM with a sparsely gated mixture-of-experts layer, i.e. [[conditional-computation]]). Middle: [[model-ensembling|ensembles]] of three of those systems — several separately trained copies decoding together, a routine way to buy 1–2 BLEU for several times the compute. Bottom: the paper's [[base-vs-big-transformer|base and big]] [[transformer|Transformer]].

The cost column comes from a rough formula ([[training-flops-estimate]]): training time × number of GPUs × an assumed sustained single-precision throughput per GPU (footnote 5: 2.8, 3.7, 6.0, 9.5 TFLOPS for K80, K40, M40, P100). For the base model, [[training-hardware-and-schedule|8 P100s for 12 hours]]: $8 \times 43{,}200\,\text{s} \times 9.5\times10^{12} \approx 3.3\times10^{18}$. The same arithmetic with 3.5 days gives $2.3\times10^{19}$ for the big model. It is nominal capacity, not measured utilization, so treat it as an order-of-magnitude figure.

Bold marks the best value in a column. Two EN-FR entries are bold (41.29 and 41.8) and the running text quotes 41.0 rather than 41.8 for the big model — leftovers from a revision of that number. The comparison is across papers, so data pipelines and tokenization differ; the paper reports only what previous work published.

The claim the table supports: a single Transformer beats prior ensembles on EN-DE while costing about an order of magnitude less to train than the ensembles it beats.
