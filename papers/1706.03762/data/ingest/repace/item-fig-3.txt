ITEM: fig-3 (figure) - Heads tracking "making … more difficult"

TAKEAWAY:
This is one query word's attention pattern in a single encoder self-attention layer, drawn as lines from the word to the positions it attends to, colored by head. Several of the eight heads put most of their weight on "more" and "difficult", six and seven tokens away from "making" — the paper's illustration that self-attention can link distant, grammatically related words in a single step.

WALKTHROUGH:
### What you are looking at

One query position's attention, taken from a trained Transformer's [[encoder-self-attention|encoder self-attention]] at layer 5 of [[n-layers|six]]. The input sentence — *"It is in this spirit that a majority of American governments have passed new laws since 2009 making the registration or voting process more difficult."* — is written out twice: once along the top, once along the bottom. Both copies are the same sentence in the same order; the words are rotated vertically only to fit. `<EOS>` and six `<pad>` slots pad the sequence out to 33 positions.

### How to read it

The top copy holds the *querying* positions; the bottom copy holds the positions being *attended to*. Only one query is drawn here — "making", marked by the gray box in the top row. Each line leaving "making" ends on a bottom-row word that "making" assigned weight to. Line opacity encodes that weight: it is a [[softmax]] output, so within each head the weights across all 33 positions sum to 1. Faint line, small weight; solid line, large weight. Line color identifies which [[attention-head|head]] produced it. The model runs [[num-heads-h|eight]] heads in parallel on separately projected queries, keys and values ([[multi-head-attention]]), so each head has its own distribution and its own color. The colored cells stacked under the bottom sentence carry the same numbers as a heat strip: one row per head, one column per word, shade = weight.

### What it shows

Most of the visible weight lands on two words. Blue, orange, green and gray heads concentrate on "more"; blue, green, red and brown heads concentrate on "difficult". Both sit six and seven tokens after "making", with "the registration or voting process" in between. A purple head attends back to "making" itself and a pink head to "2009" and "laws".

The paper reads this as heads following the verb to its complement, completing "making … more difficult" across the intervening phrase — a [[long-range-dependencies|long-distance dependency]] resolved in one attention hop rather than six sequential steps, which is the [[maximum-path-length]] argument made concrete. Treat it as an example, not a measurement: the paper only says the distributions [[attention-interpretability|appear to relate]] to sentence structure.
