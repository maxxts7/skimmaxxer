ITEM: fig-5 (figure) - Two heads, one sentence

TAKEAWAY:
Two attention heads from the same encoder self-attention layer, run on the same sentence, produce visibly different attention patterns: one fans many positions into a few words, the other makes sparse, short, word-to-word links. The paper offers this as a qualitative illustration that heads specialize, and says only that the patterns "seem related" to sentence structure — nothing here is measured.

WALKTHROUGH:
### What you are looking at

Two pictures of the same thing. Each panel is the full attention map of one [[attention-head|head]] inside the [[encoder-self-attention]] at layer 5 of the encoder's 6 layers, computed on one input sentence: *"The Law will never be perfect, but its application should be just - this is what we are missing, in my opinion."* Top panel is one head (green), bottom panel is a different head from the same layer (red). Everything else — sentence, layer, model — is held fixed, so any difference between the panels is the difference between the two heads.

### How to read a panel

Each panel prints the sentence twice, once along the top and once along the bottom. The top copy is the *querying* positions; the bottom copy is the positions being attended to. Both copies are the same sentence because this is [[self-attention|self-attention]] ([[queries-keys-values|queries, keys, and values]] all come from the previous encoder layer). A line from a top token to a bottom token is one attention weight; the darker and more opaque the line, the larger the weight. Opacity is the only quantitative channel — there are no axes and no printed numbers. Each top token's outgoing lines are a [[softmax]] distribution, so they sum to 1 across all bottom positions.

### What it shows

The green head spreads: a faint haze of lines covers almost every pair, with heavy fans converging on a handful of words — *Law*, *application*, *missing*, *opinion*. Many positions route mass into the same few tokens. The red head is sparse: mostly short, near-vertical links, each query picking out one or two specific nearby tokens, with little background haze. Two different jobs, in the same layer, at the same time — which is what [[multi-head-attention]] is meant to allow.

The caption is deliberately uneven. That the heads "learned to perform different tasks" is stated flatly, and you can see it. That the behaviour relates to sentence structure only "seems" so; Section 4 likewise says heads "appear to exhibit" behaviour related to syntactic and semantic structure, and calls [[attention-interpretability|interpretability]] a side benefit. This is two hand-picked heads on one sentence, with no syntactic annotation and no score. Note also the dark self-link on `<pad>` in both panels: not every strong line is linguistic.
