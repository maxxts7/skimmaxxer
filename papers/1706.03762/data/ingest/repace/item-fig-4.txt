ITEM: fig-4 (figure) - Two heads that resolve "its"

TAKEAWAY:
Two different attention heads in the same encoder layer put almost all of the word "its" attention on two different words — head 5 on "Law", head 6 on "application". The paper offers this as a sign that individual heads pick up distinct, sentence-structure-related jobs (here, apparently anaphora resolution) that nobody supervised or programmed.

WALKTHROUGH:
### What you are looking at

Two views of the attention weights inside one trained [[transformer|Transformer]], for the English sentence *"The Law will never be perfect, but its application should be just — this is what we are missing, in my opinion."* Both come from [[encoder-self-attention|encoder self-attention]] at layer 5 of the encoder's 6 layers, so every token is attending to tokens of the same sentence.

*Anaphora resolution* is the job of working out what a pronoun points back to. Here: whose application? The Law's. Nothing in the training objective asks the model to do this — it was trained only to translate.

### How to read the panels

Each panel prints the sentence twice. The **top row is the querying positions**, the **bottom row is the attended positions**. A line from a top token to a bottom token means that token's [[queries-keys-values|query]] put weight on that [[queries-keys-values|key]]. Weight is drawn as line darkness and thickness; the weights come out of a [[softmax]], so the lines leaving any one top token sum to 1.

**Top panel:** all of head 5's attention, every query at once. Most lines are faint — weight spread thinly — with a handful of dark lines standing out.

**Bottom panel:** the same picture with everything removed except the lines leaving the single word "its" (shaded grey), now for two heads at once. Purple is head 5, tan is head 6. Head 5 sends nearly all of "its" weight to **Law**; head 6 sends nearly all of it to **application**. The stacked bars under those two words show each head's share. The caption notes the attentions are "very sharp" for this word — concentrated on one place rather than smeared.

### What it shows

The two [[attention-head|heads]] behave differently on the same input at the same depth. The paper's claim is hedged — "apparently involved in anaphora resolution" — and it is one example, not a measurement. It supports the broader point in Section 4 that [[attention-interpretability|inspecting attention distributions]] suggests heads learn separate tasks, which is part of the argument for [[multi-head-attention]] over a single averaged head.
