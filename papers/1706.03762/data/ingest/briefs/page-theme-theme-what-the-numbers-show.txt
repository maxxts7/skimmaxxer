NAME: What the numbers show

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
How outputs are produced at test time and what they score. Decoding uses beam search with a length penalty; quality is reported as BLEU, with perplexity and an estimated training FLOP count alongside it so quality can be read against cost. The ablations change one design choice at a time to see which parts matter, and a constituency-parsing experiment tests whether the architecture carries to a different task. This comes last because it is the evidence for the claims made earlier.

EVIDENCE ITEMS RELATED TO THIS THEME (link as [[item-id]]):
  - table-2 | Translation quality against training cost: On the WMT 2014 newstest2014 benchmarks, the big Transformer reports the best English-to-German BLEU in the table (28.4, more than 2.0 BLEU above every earlier model listed, ensembles included) and a competitive English-to-French score, while its estimated training cost is at least a few times lower than any competitive baseline. The base Transformer, at roughly 3.3e18 FLOPs, already scores above every prior row on English-to-German.
  - table-3 | One-knob-at-a-time ablations: Table 3 changes one design choice of the base Transformer at a time and reports the effect on English-to-German development-set quality. It shows a middling head count (around 8-16) is best, shrinking the attention key dimension $d_k$ hurts, bigger models are better, dropout matters a lot, and learned positional embeddings match sinusoids almost exactly.
  - table-4 | The parsing leaderboard: Trained on the ~40K-sentence WSJ treebank with almost no task-specific tuning, a 4-layer Transformer reaches 91.3 F1 on WSJ Section 23 — above every listed discriminative parser except Dyer et al.'s RNN Grammar (91.7) — and 92.7 F1 when the extra ~17M auto-parsed sentences are added, the best semi-supervised number in the table. It also beats the RNN sequence-to-sequence parser (Vinyals & Kaiser et al.) whose experimental setup it borrows, in both regimes.
  - eq-posenc | Position as a bank of sinusoids: A model with no recurrence and no convolution has no idea what order its tokens are in, so the paper adds a fixed vector to each input embedding whose entries are sines and cosines of the position at geometrically spaced wavelengths. The paper says it picked this over learned position vectors because relative offsets become linear functions of the encoding, and because it may extrapolate to sequences longer than those seen in training.
