NAME: English constituency parsing

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
The task of producing a sentence's syntactic tree — nested phrases like noun phrases and verb phrases — used as a generalization test for the Transformer beyond translation.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
Constituency parsing maps a sentence to a tree that groups its words into nested labeled phrases (e.g. a noun phrase inside a verb phrase inside a sentence). Framed as sequence-to-sequence, the output is the tree written out as a bracketed sequence, which is significantly longer than the input and must obey strong structural constraints — brackets have to nest and close correctly. The paper picks this task precisely because it stresses the model differently from translation, and because RNN sequence-to-sequence models had not reached state-of-the-art results in small-data regimes. A 4-layer Transformer with $d_{model}=1024$, with only dropout, learning rate, and beam size re-tuned (on the WSJ Section 22 development set), outperforms all previously reported models except the Recurrent Neural Network Grammar, and beats the BerkeleyParser even when trained only on the 40K-sentence WSJ set — a regime where RNN seq2seq models had struggled.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-what-the-numbers-show | What the numbers show - How outputs are produced at test time and what they score. Decoding uses beam search with a length penalty; quality is reported as BLEU, with perplexity and an estimated training FLOP count alongside it so quality can be read against cost. The ablations change one design choice at a time to see which parts matter, and a constituency-parsing experiment tests whether the architecture carries to a different task. This comes last because it is the evidence for the claims made earlier.

PREREQUISITES:
  - sequence-transduction | Sequence transduction: The general task of converting one sequence of symbols into another sequence, such as translating a German sentence into English.
  - transformer | The Transformer: The architecture this paper proposes: an encoder-decoder built entirely from stacked self-attention and position-wise feed-forward layers, with no recurrence and no convolutions.
  - beam-search | Beam search: A decoding procedure that keeps the k best partial output sequences at each step instead of committing to a single best token; the paper uses beam size 4.
  - residual-dropout | Residual dropout: Dropout applied to each sub-layer's output before the residual addition and layer normalization, and to the embedding-plus-positional-encoding sums, at rate P_drop = 0.1 for the base model.
  - penn-treebank-wsj | Penn Treebank (WSJ portion): The standard constituency-parsing dataset: about 40K hand-parsed sentences from the Wall Street Journal.
