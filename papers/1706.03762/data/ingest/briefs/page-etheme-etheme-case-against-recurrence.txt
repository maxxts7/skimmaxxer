NAME: The case against recurrence and convolution

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
Section 4 sets up three criteria — computational complexity per layer, minimum sequential operations, and maximum path length between positions — and Table 1 turns them into numbers for self-attention, recurrent, and convolutional layers. The argument is that self-attention needs O(1) sequential operations where an RNN needs O(n), and connects any two positions in one step where a convolutional stack needs several layers, ByteNet growing logarithmically with distance and ConvS2S linearly. Short paths are wanted because long-range dependencies are the stated difficulty in sequence transduction, and Figure 3 shows one such dependency drawn out. The criteria do not agree with each other: cheap layers and parallel layers pull apart, and the restricted r-neighborhood variant buys a cheaper layer by lengthening the path. The paper also concedes that separable convolutions can match the layer cost, and that the O(n^2 · d) claim rests on n staying smaller than d, which depends on how the subword vocabulary is set.

  e089 [load-bearing] self-attention (Self-attention (intra-attention)) --contrasts-with--> recurrent-neural-network (Recurrent neural network (RNN)) | constant vs O(n) sequential steps: Section 4 sets these two layer types against each other on the same job: mapping one length-n sequence to another. A self-attention layer connects all positions with a constant number of sequentially executed operations, while a recurrent layer needs O(n) steps that cannot be parallelized. This is the paper's main reason for dropping recurrence.
  e090 [load-bearing] self-attention (Self-attention (intra-attention)) --contrasts-with--> convolutional-sequence-models (Convolutional sequence models) | one layer vs stacked layers: A single convolutional layer with kernel width k < n does not connect all pairs of positions; reaching all pairs needs O(n/k) layers, or O(log_k n) with dilated convolutions. Self-attention connects every pair in one layer, so the path between distant positions stays constant instead of growing with distance.
  e091 [supporting] convolutional-sequence-models (Convolutional sequence models) --contrasts-with--> recurrent-neural-network (Recurrent neural network (RNN)) | parallel positions, longer paths: Convolutions were the earlier answer to the same problem: they compute all positions in parallel, unlike recurrence, but the number of operations relating two positions grows with the distance between them. The paper also notes convolutional layers are generally more expensive than recurrent ones, by a factor of the kernel width k.
  e092 [supporting] bytenet (ByteNet) --contrasts-with--> convs2s (ConvS2S) | logarithmic vs linear distance growth: Both replace recurrence with convolutions, but the cost of relating two positions grows logarithmically with distance in ByteNet and linearly in ConvS2S. Neither reaches the constant cost the Transformer claims, which is the point of listing them together in the background section.
  e093 [supporting] separable-convolutions (Separable convolutions) --contrasts-with--> self-attention (Self-attention (intra-attention)) | the concession on layer cost: The paper concedes that separable convolutions cut complexity to O(k*n*d + n*d^2), and that even at k = n this equals the cost of a self-attention layer plus a point-wise feed-forward layer -- the exact combination the Transformer uses. So the cost argument against convolutions is not what carries the case; the path-length and parallelism arguments are.
  e094 [supporting] complexity-per-layer (Computational complexity per layer) --contrasts-with--> maximum-path-length (Maximum path length) | two criteria that pull apart: Two of Section 4's three criteria trade against each other. Buying a shorter path between distant positions means each layer looks at more pairs and costs more; making a layer cheaper (a narrow convolution kernel, a restricted attention window) lengthens the path. Table 1 is a comparison across that trade-off, not a single ranking.
  e095 [load-bearing] complexity-per-layer (Computational complexity per layer) --contrasts-with--> minimum-sequential-operations (Minimum sequential operations) | cheap layer vs parallel layer: Self-attention costs O(n^2 * d) per layer against a recurrent layer's O(n * d^2), so on per-layer cost recurrence wins whenever n is larger than d. The paper argues n < d holds for the sentence-length word-piece and byte-pair representations used in machine translation, and that self-attention wins outright on the sequential-operations criterion regardless.
  e096 [supporting] restricted-self-attention (Restricted (local) self-attention) --contrasts-with--> self-attention (Self-attention (intra-attention)) | cheaper window, longer path: For very long sequences the paper proposes limiting each position to a neighborhood of size r, which cuts per-layer cost but raises the maximum path length from constant to O(n/r). It is offered as future work, not something the paper tested.
  e101 [load-bearing] attention-averaging-resolution-cost (Averaging cost of single-head attention) --contrasts-with--> maximum-path-length (Maximum path length) | the price of constant-length paths: The background section states the trade plainly: the Transformer reduces relating two positions to a constant number of operations, albeit at the cost of reduced effective resolution from averaging attention-weighted positions. Multi-head attention is introduced as the counteraction, so this cost is what the head split is paying for.
  e116 [minor] vocabulary-vs-sequence-length-tradeoff (Vocabulary Size vs. Sequence Length Trade-off) --contrasts-with--> complexity-per-layer (Computational complexity per layer) | finer subwords lengthen n: The BPE paper's trade-off is vocabulary size against tokenized sequence length: smaller vocabularies mean longer sequences. That pushes against self-attention's O(n^2 * d) cost and against the n < d condition the Transformer paper leans on when it argues self-attention is faster than recurrence.
