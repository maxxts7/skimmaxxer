NAME: BLEU score

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
The standard automatic metric for translation quality: n-gram overlap precision against reference translations with a penalty for overly short outputs. Higher is better.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
BLEU compares machine output to one or more human reference translations. It computes, for n-gram sizes 1 through 4, what fraction of the n-grams in the machine output also appear in the references (clipped so repeating a word cannot inflate the count), combines the four precisions by geometric mean, and multiplies by a brevity penalty that punishes outputs shorter than the references. Scores are usually reported on a 0-100 scale; differences of 1-2 BLEU on standard test sets are considered meaningful. It is a proxy: it measures overlap of the produced text, not fluency, meaning, or the model's probabilities — the reason label smoothing can help BLEU while hurting perplexity. All of the paper's translation claims — 28.4 on WMT 2014 English-German (more than 2.0 above prior best, including ensembles) and 41.8 on English-French for the final big model — are stated in BLEU.
