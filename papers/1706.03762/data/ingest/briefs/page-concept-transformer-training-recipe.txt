NAME: Transformer training recipe

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
The complete set of choices the paper used to train its models: data, batching, hardware, optimizer schedule, and regularization.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
Section 5 is the paper's reproducibility record. It specifies the datasets and tokenization (section 5.1), the hardware and number of training steps (5.2), the Adam optimizer with a warmup-then-decay learning rate (5.3), and two regularizers: residual dropout and label smoothing (5.4). The results in section 6 depend on this exact recipe, and the ablations in section 6.2 vary pieces of it. Each piece is a child concept.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-training-the-thing | Training the thing - Everything needed to reproduce a trained model: the translation datasets, subword vocabularies built with byte-pair encoding or word pieces, batching by sequence length, and eight GPUs on one machine. The learning rate is not fixed — it ramps up linearly for the first 4000 steps and then decays as the inverse square root of the step. Label smoothing, dropout, and averaging the last several checkpoints round out the recipe, and the code was released as tensor2tensor.

SUB-CONCEPTS (these render as expandable sections BELOW your page - reference them, do not duplicate them):
  - wmt-2014-en-de | WMT 2014 English-German dataset: The standard benchmark translation dataset of about 4.5 million English-German sentence pairs used as the main training and evaluation task.
  - wmt-2014-en-fr | WMT 2014 English-French dataset: A much larger benchmark translation dataset of 36 million sentence pairs, used to test the model at larger data scale.
  - length-based-batching | Batching by sequence length: Sentence pairs of similar length are grouped into the same batch, and batch size is measured in tokens rather than in sentences.
  - training-hardware-and-schedule | Hardware and training schedule: One machine with 8 NVIDIA P100 GPUs; base models train for 100,000 steps (12 hours), big models for 300,000 steps (3.5 days).
  - base-vs-big-transformer | Base vs big model configurations: The paper trains two main configurations: a 'base' Transformer and a scaled-up 'big' Transformer with larger dimensions, more dropout, and three times the training steps.
  - transformer-adam-settings | Adam settings (beta2 = 0.98): The paper runs Adam with beta1 = 0.9, beta2 = 0.98, and epsilon = 10^-9, a lower-than-default beta2.
  - learning-rate-warmup-schedule | Warmup learning-rate schedule: The learning rate rises linearly for the first warmup_steps steps, then decays as the inverse square root of the step number, all scaled by the model width.
    - warmup-steps-4000 | warmup_steps = 4000: The number of initial steps over which the learning rate ramps up linearly; the paper uses 4000.
  - residual-dropout | Residual dropout: Dropout applied to each sub-layer's output before the residual addition and layer normalization, and to the embedding-plus-positional-encoding sums, at rate P_drop = 0.1 for the base model.
  - attention-dropout | Attention dropout: Dropout applied inside the attention mechanism, mentioned as a separately tunable rate in the parsing experiments.
  - label-smoothing | Label smoothing: A training target modification: instead of putting probability 1 on the correct token, the target gives it 1 - eps and spreads eps = 0.1 over other tokens, penalizing overconfidence.

PREREQUISITES:
  - transformer | The Transformer: The architecture this paper proposes: an encoder-decoder built entirely from stacked self-attention and position-wise feed-forward layers, with no recurrence and no convolutions.
