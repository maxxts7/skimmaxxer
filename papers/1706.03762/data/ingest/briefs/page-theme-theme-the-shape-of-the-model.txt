NAME: The shape of the model

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
The outline of the Transformer before any of the math. It keeps the familiar encoder-decoder frame: an encoder turns the input into representations, a decoder emits output tokens one at a time. Both are stacks of six identical layers, and every sub-layer inside a layer is wrapped in a residual connection followed by layer normalization. Read this to have a map before the parts are filled in.

EVIDENCE ITEMS RELATED TO THIS THEME (link as [[item-id]]):
  - fig-1 | The two-stack data path: The figure shows the entire model in one picture: two towers of identical layers, each layer built only from attention and a position-wise feed-forward network, every sub-layer wrapped in a residual connection plus layer normalization. There is no recurrence and no convolution anywhere, so order information has to be injected once, at the bottom of each tower.
  - table-4 | The parsing leaderboard: Trained on the ~40K-sentence WSJ treebank with almost no task-specific tuning, a 4-layer Transformer reaches 91.3 F1 on WSJ Section 23 — above every listed discriminative parser except Dyer et al.'s RNN Grammar (91.7) — and 92.7 F1 when the extra ~17M auto-parsed sentences are added, the best semi-supervised number in the table. It also beats the RNN sequence-to-sequence parser (Vinyals & Kaiser et al.) whose experimental setup it borrows, in both regimes.
  - fig-5 | Two heads, one sentence: Two attention heads from the same encoder self-attention layer, run on the same sentence, produce visibly different attention patterns: one fans many positions into a few words, the other makes sparse, short, word-to-word links. The paper offers this as a qualitative illustration that heads specialize, and says only that the patterns "seem related" to sentence structure — nothing here is measured.
  - eq-ffn | The per-position feed-forward block: Equation (2) defines the second sub-layer in every encoder and decoder layer: a two-layer MLP with a ReLU in between, applied to each position's vector on its own. It widens each 512-dimensional vector to 2048, rectifies, and projects back to 512, with weights shared across positions but not across layers.
  - eq-posenc | Position as a bank of sinusoids: A model with no recurrence and no convolution has no idea what order its tokens are in, so the paper adds a fixed vector to each input embedding whose entries are sines and cosines of the position at geometrically spaced wavelengths. The paper says it picked this over learned position vectors because relative offsets become linear functions of the encoding, and because it may extrapolate to sequences longer than those seen in training.
