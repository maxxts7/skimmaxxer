NAME: Byte-pair encoding (BPE)

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
A subword tokenization scheme that builds a vocabulary by repeatedly merging the most frequent pair of symbols, so rare words split into known pieces.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
BPE starts from characters and greedily merges the most frequent adjacent symbol pairs until the vocabulary reaches a target size. Common words end up as single tokens; rare words break into subword units, so any text can be encoded without an out-of-vocabulary token. The paper uses BPE for the WMT 2014 English-German data with a single vocabulary of about 37000 tokens shared between source and target languages. BPE also appears in section 4's complexity argument: byte-pair sentence representations are cited as the kind state-of-the-art translation models use, supporting the claim that sequence length $n$ is usually smaller than representation dimension $d$.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-training-the-thing | Training the thing - Everything needed to reproduce a trained model: the translation datasets, subword vocabularies built with byte-pair encoding or word pieces, batching by sequence length, and eight GPUs on one machine. The learning rate is not fixed — it ramps up linearly for the first 4000 steps and then decays as the inverse square root of the step. Label smoothing, dropout, and averaging the last several checkpoints round out the recipe, and the code was released as tensor2tensor.
