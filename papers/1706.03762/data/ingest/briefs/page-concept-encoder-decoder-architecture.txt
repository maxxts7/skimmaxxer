NAME: Encoder-decoder architecture

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
A two-part model design: an encoder turns the input sequence into continuous representations, and a decoder generates the output sequence from them, one symbol at a time.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
The encoder maps an input sequence of symbols $(x_1, ..., x_n)$ to a sequence of continuous representations $z = (z_1, ..., z_n)$ — one vector per input position. Given $z$, the decoder generates the output sequence $(y_1, ..., y_m)$ one element at a time, consuming its previously generated symbols as extra input at each step. The abstract notes that the dominant transduction models of the time were recurrent or convolutional encoder-decoders, and that the best ones also connected encoder and decoder through an attention mechanism. The Transformer keeps this overall structure but changes what the encoder and decoder are made of.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-the-shape-of-the-model | The shape of the model - The outline of the Transformer before any of the math. It keeps the familiar encoder-decoder frame: an encoder turns the input into representations, a decoder emits output tokens one at a time. Both are stacks of six identical layers, and every sub-layer inside a layer is wrapped in a residual connection followed by layer normalization. Read this to have a map before the parts are filled in.
