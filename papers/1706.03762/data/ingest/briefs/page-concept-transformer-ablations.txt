NAME: Model variation ablations (Table 3)

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
Experiments that change one component of the base model at a time and measure the effect on English-to-German translation, to see which parts matter.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
Section 6.2 varies the base Transformer along several axes, holding the rest fixed, and reports perplexity and BLEU for each variant. The findings: the number of attention heads has a sweet spot (rows A); shrinking the attention key size $d_k$ hurts quality (rows B); bigger models are better and dropout is very helpful against over-fitting (rows C and D); and swapping the sinusoidal positional encoding for learned positional embeddings changes almost nothing (row E). Each finding is a child concept.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-what-the-numbers-show | What the numbers show - How outputs are produced at test time and what they score. Decoding uses beam search with a length penalty; quality is reported as BLEU, with perplexity and an estimated training FLOP count alongside it so quality can be read against cost. The ablations change one design choice at a time to see which parts matter, and a constituency-parsing experiment tests whether the architecture carries to a different task. This comes last because it is the evidence for the claims made earlier.

SUB-CONCEPTS (these render as expandable sections BELOW your page - reference them, do not duplicate them):
  - attention-heads-ablation | Number of heads sweet spot: Varying the head count with total computation held constant shows single-head attention is 0.9 BLEU worse than the best setting, but too many heads also degrades quality.
  - key-size-ablation | Reducing d_k hurts quality: Shrinking the attention key dimension d_k lowers translation quality, suggesting the dot product may be too simple a compatibility function.
  - size-and-dropout-ablation | Model size and dropout effects: Bigger models translate better, and dropout is very helpful in preventing over-fitting.

PREREQUISITES:
  - base-vs-big-transformer | Base vs big model configurations: The paper trains two main configurations: a 'base' Transformer and a scaled-up 'big' Transformer with larger dimensions, more dropout, and three times the training steps.
  - wmt-2014-en-de | WMT 2014 English-German dataset: The standard benchmark translation dataset of about 4.5 million English-German sentence pairs used as the main training and evaluation task.
  - bleu | BLEU score: The standard automatic metric for translation quality: n-gram overlap precision against reference translations with a penalty for overly short outputs. Higher is better.
  - perplexity | Perplexity: A measure of how well a model predicts the true next tokens: the exponentiated average negative log-probability. Lower is better.

EVIDENCE ITEMS THAT SHOW THIS (link them as [[item-id]]):
  - eq-posenc | Position as a bank of sinusoids: A model with no recurrence and no convolution has no idea what order its tokens are in, so the paper adds a fixed vector to each input embedding whose entries are sines and cosines of the position at geometrically spaced wavelengths. The paper says it picked this over learned position vectors because relative offsets become linear functions of the encoding, and because it may extrapolate to sequences longer than those seen in training.
