NAME: Multi-head attention

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
Running $h$ attention functions in parallel on different learned linear projections of the queries, keys, and values, then concatenating and projecting the results.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
Instead of one attention function over full $d_{model}$-dimensional vectors, the model linearly projects the queries, keys, and values $h$ times, with different learned projections, down to $d_k$, $d_k$, and $d_v$ dimensions. Attention runs in parallel on each projected set, giving $h$ outputs of dimension $d_v$; these are concatenated and projected once more: $\mathrm{MultiHead}(Q,K,V) = \mathrm{Concat}(\mathrm{head}_1, ..., \mathrm{head}_h)W^O$ with $\mathrm{head}_i = \mathrm{Attention}(QW^Q_i, KW^K_i, VW^V_i)$. The stated motivation: a single attention operation averages over attention-weighted positions, which reduces effective resolution; multiple heads let the model jointly attend to information from different representation subspaces at different positions. Because each head works in reduced dimensions ($d_k = d_v = d_{model}/h$), the total compute is similar to one full-dimensional head. Section 4 adds an observed side effect: individual heads appear to learn different tasks, some related to syntactic and semantic sentence structure.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-how-attention-is-computed | How attention is computed - The one operation the model is built from. Attention maps a query and a set of key-value pairs to a weighted sum of the values, with the weights coming from how well the query matches each key. The paper's version scores with a dot product, divides by the square root of the key dimension, and softmaxes; it then runs eight of these in parallel on lower-dimensional projections and concatenates the results. This is the mechanical core, so it comes before the places it gets used.

SUB-CONCEPTS (these render as expandable sections BELOW your page - reference them, do not duplicate them):
  - attention-head | Attention head: One of the parallel attention functions inside multi-head attention, operating on its own learned low-dimensional projection of the queries, keys, and values.
  - num-heads-h | h = 8 (number of heads): The number of parallel attention heads in the base model: eight.
  - attention-projection-matrices | Attention projection matrices (W^Q, W^K, W^V, W^O): The learned linear maps that create each head's queries, keys, and values, and the final matrix that mixes the concatenated head outputs back to $d_{model}$.
  - attention-averaging-resolution-cost | Averaging cost of single-head attention: A single attention operation blends many positions into one weighted average, which can blur position-specific information.
  - attention-interpretability | Interpretability of attention: The paper's observation that inspecting attention distributions suggests individual heads learn distinct, sometimes linguistically meaningful, roles.

PREREQUISITES:
  - scaled-dot-product-attention | Scaled dot-product attention: The Transformer's attention: dot the query with every key, divide by $\sqrt{d_k}$, softmax the scores, and use them to weight the values.
  - d-model | d_model = 512 (model dimension): The shared width of the model: every sub-layer and embedding layer outputs vectors of dimension $d_{model} = 512$.

  - fig-3 | Heads tracking "making … more difficult": This is one query word's attention pattern in a single encoder self-attention layer, drawn as lines from the word to the positions it attends to, colored by head. Several of the eight heads put most of their weight on "more" and "difficult", six and seven tokens away from "making" — the paper's illustration that self-attention can link distant, grammatically related words in a single step.
  - fig-4 | Two heads that resolve "its": Two different attention heads in the same encoder layer put almost all of the word "its" attention on two different words — head 5 on "Law", head 6 on "application". The paper offers this as a sign that individual heads pick up distinct, sentence-structure-related jobs (here, apparently anaphora resolution) that nobody supervised or programmed.
  - fig-5 | Two heads, one sentence: Two attention heads from the same encoder self-attention layer, run on the same sentence, produce visibly different attention patterns: one fans many positions into a few words, the other makes sparse, short, word-to-word links. The paper offers this as a qualitative illustration that heads specialize, and says only that the patterns "seem related" to sentence structure — nothing here is measured.
  - eq-multihead | Attention in h parallel subspaces: Instead of one attention function over full-width 512-dimensional vectors, the Transformer runs $h=8$ attention functions in parallel, each on its own learned 64-dimensional projection of the queries, keys, and values, then concatenates the results and projects them once more. Because each head is narrower by a factor of $h$, the paper says the total computational cost stays similar to one full-width head.
