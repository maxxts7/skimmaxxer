NAME: Sequential computation bottleneck of RNNs

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
Because an RNN's hidden state at position t depends on the hidden state at position t-1, positions within one training example cannot be computed in parallel.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
Recurrent models align positions with computation steps: they compute a hidden state $h_t$ as a function of the previous state $h_{t-1}$ and the input at position $t$. Each step must wait for the one before it, so the work inside a single example cannot be parallelized. This matters most for long sequences, where memory limits also restrict how many examples fit in a batch, cutting off the other route to parallelism. The paper notes that factorization tricks and conditional computation improved efficiency, but says the fundamental constraint of sequential computation remains. Removing this constraint is the Transformer's central motivation.
