NAME: How attention is computed

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
The one operation the model is built from. Attention maps a query and a set of key-value pairs to a weighted sum of the values, with the weights coming from how well the query matches each key. The paper's version scores with a dot product, divides by the square root of the key dimension, and softmaxes; it then runs eight of these in parallel on lower-dimensional projections and concatenates the results. This is the mechanical core, so it comes before the places it gets used.

EVIDENCE ITEMS RELATED TO THIS THEME (link as [[item-id]]):
  - fig-1 | The two-stack data path: The figure shows the entire model in one picture: two towers of identical layers, each layer built only from attention and a position-wise feed-forward network, every sub-layer wrapped in a residual connection plus layer normalization. There is no recurrence and no convolution anywhere, so order information has to be injected once, at the bottom of each tower.
  - fig-2 | Attention as a wiring diagram: The figure draws the paper's attention math as data flow: on the left, Equation (1) box by box (dot queries against keys, scale, optionally mask, softmax, weight the values); on the right, that whole block used as a reusable unit, run $h$ times in parallel on separately projected copies of Q, K and V, then concatenated and projected once more.
  - fig-3 | Heads tracking "making … more difficult": This is one query word's attention pattern in a single encoder self-attention layer, drawn as lines from the word to the positions it attends to, colored by head. Several of the eight heads put most of their weight on "more" and "difficult", six and seven tokens away from "making" — the paper's illustration that self-attention can link distant, grammatically related words in a single step.
  - fig-4 | Two heads that resolve "its": Two different attention heads in the same encoder layer put almost all of the word "its" attention on two different words — head 5 on "Law", head 6 on "application". The paper offers this as a sign that individual heads pick up distinct, sentence-structure-related jobs (here, apparently anaphora resolution) that nobody supervised or programmed.
  - fig-5 | Two heads, one sentence: Two attention heads from the same encoder self-attention layer, run on the same sentence, produce visibly different attention patterns: one fans many positions into a few words, the other makes sparse, short, word-to-word links. The paper offers this as a qualitative illustration that heads specialize, and says only that the patterns "seem related" to sentence structure — nothing here is measured.
  - eq-attention | The scaled dot-product attention formula: This is the paper's whole attention function in one line: score every query against every key with a dot product, shrink the scores by $\sqrt{d_k}$, softmax them into weights, and return the weighted sum of the values. The $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ factor is the only thing separating it from ordinary dot-product attention, and the paper adds it because it suspects large dot products push the softmax into regions with very small gradients.
  - eq-multihead | Attention in h parallel subspaces: Instead of one attention function over full-width 512-dimensional vectors, the Transformer runs $h=8$ attention functions in parallel, each on its own learned 64-dimensional projection of the queries, keys, and values, then concatenates the results and projects them once more. Because each head is narrower by a factor of $h$, the paper says the total computational cost stays similar to one full-width head.
