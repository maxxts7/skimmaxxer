NAME: Scaled dot-product attention

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
The Transformer's attention: dot the query with every key, divide by $\sqrt{d_k}$, softmax the scores, and use them to weight the values.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
Inputs are queries and keys of dimension $d_k$ and values of dimension $d_v$. For each query, the model computes its dot product with all keys, divides each score by $\sqrt{d_k}$, and applies a softmax to get weights on the values. With queries, keys, and values packed into matrices $Q$, $K$, $V$, the whole thing is $\mathrm{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \mathrm{softmax}(QK^T / \sqrt{d_k})\,V$. The paper picks this over additive attention because it runs as plain matrix multiplication, which is much faster and more memory-efficient in practice even though the two have similar theoretical complexity. The $\sqrt{d_k}$ divisor is the one change from standard dot-product attention.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-how-attention-is-computed | How attention is computed - The one operation the model is built from. Attention maps a query and a set of key-value pairs to a weighted sum of the values, with the weights coming from how well the query matches each key. The paper's version scores with a dot product, divides by the square root of the key dimension, and softmaxes; it then runs eight of these in parallel on lower-dimensional projections and concatenates the results. This is the mechanical core, so it comes before the places it gets used.

  - sqrt-dk-scaling | The 1/sqrt(d_k) scaling factor: Dividing the query-key dot products by $\sqrt{d_k}$ so their magnitude stays controlled as the dimension grows, keeping the softmax out of its flat, tiny-gradient regions.

  - eq-attention | The scaled dot-product attention formula: This is the paper's whole attention function in one line: score every query against every key with a dot product, shrink the scores by $\sqrt{d_k}$, softmax them into weights, and return the weighted sum of the values. The $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ factor is the only thing separating it from ordinary dot-product attention, and the paper adds it because it suspects large dot products push the softmax into regions with very small gradients.
  - eq-multihead | Attention in h parallel subspaces: Instead of one attention function over full-width 512-dimensional vectors, the Transformer runs $h=8$ attention functions in parallel, each on its own learned 64-dimensional projection of the queries, keys, and values, then concatenates the results and projects them once more. Because each head is narrower by a factor of $h$, the paper says the total computational cost stays similar to one full-width head.
