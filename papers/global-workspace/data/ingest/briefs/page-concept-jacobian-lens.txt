PAGE FOR CONCEPT: jacobian-lens
NAME: Jacobian lens (J-lens)

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
The paper's new interpretability technique: for each vocabulary token, it identifies the vector direction that encodes the model's potential to verbalize that token, by averaging the linearized effect of activations on the token's likelihood over a large corpus.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
The Jacobian lens computes, for each layer and each token in the model's vocabulary, the average linearized effect of an activation on the model's likelihood of producing that token now or in the future, averaged over a large corpus of contexts. The result is one vector per token per layer, and projecting an activation onto these vectors yields a ranked list of tokens the model is poised to verbalize. The averaging is the key step: it separates verbalizable content from content merely verbalized in one context. The lens is described as a principled refinement of the logit lens: where the logit lens assumes representations use the same coordinates at every layer, the J-lens corrects for representational changes across depth, recovering meaningful readouts at earlier layers where the logit lens is uninterpretable. Practically, applying it costs a single precomputed matrix multiply per layer, with the matrix computed once per model, and it applies uniformly to activations, weights, and feature directions. The paper describes it as an imperfect tool that only approximately and incompletely captures the underlying workspace structure.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-the-jacobian-lens | The Jacobian lens - The paper's instrument is one matrix per layer, built by averaging the Jacobian from that layer to the model's output over a corpus of prompts. It yields one vector per token in the vocabulary: the direction that, on average, disposes the model to say that token. This chapter covers how the matrix is built, how a readout is taken from it, and how a concept is steered, ablated or swapped along its vectors, together with the earlier tools the lens is measured against — the logit lens, the tuned lens, linear probes and sparse dictionaries. The instrument comes before the findings because every finding is a lens measurement.

SUB-CONCEPTS (these render as expandable sections BELOW your page - reference them, do not duplicate them):
  - averaged-jacobian | Corpus-averaged Jacobian: The single $d_{\text{model}} \times d_{\text{model}}$ matrix per layer obtained by averaging the Jacobian from layer $\ell$ to the final layer over positions and a corpus of a thousand prompts.
    - jacobian-computation-procedure | Computing the Jacobian in practice: The pseudocode the paper supplies for estimating $J_\ell$: one forward pass per prompt, then one backward pass per output dimension, with every layer's Jacobian falling out of the same backward pass.
  - bag-of-concepts-limitation | Bag-of-concepts limitation: The J-lens treats the workspace as a flat collection of independently active concept vectors, blind to how concepts are bound together or structured.
  - j-lens-interventions | Writing with the J-lens: The family of causal interventions the lens supports: steering concepts in, ablating them out, and swapping one concept for another in lens coordinates.
    - j-lens-ablation | J-lens ablation: Suppressing a concept by projecting out an activation's component along its J-lens vector, or suppressing the top-$k$ J-space contents entirely.
    - j-lens-steering | Steering along a J-lens vector: The intervention $h \leftarrow h + \alpha\,v_t$: adding a scaled J-lens vector to the residual stream to inject a concept.
  - j-lens-probe | J-lens probe form: Using a single J-lens vector as a per-token probe: reading the score or cosine similarity of an activation against one chosen $v_t$, without ranking the full vocabulary.
  - j-lens-readout | J-lens readout: The basic read operation of the lens: $\text{lens}(h_\ell) = \text{softmax}(W_U\,\text{norm}(J_\ell h_\ell))$, giving a score for every vocabulary token.
    - lens-readout-normalization | Normalization inside the readout: The readout passes $J_\ell h_\ell$ through the model's own pre-unembedding normalization, which is why a lens score is only approximately a linear function of the activation.
  - j-lens-recipe-variants | J-lens recipe variants and ablations: The design space of the lens — which gradient is computed, how per-prompt Jacobians are aggregated, and what data the expectation is over — and the finding that results are fairly consistent across it.
    - frozen-qk-variant | Frozen-QK variant: A lens variant that zeroes gradients through the query and key projections, holding attention patterns fixed so only the value pathway contributes.
    - jacobian-aggregation | Jacobian aggregation choices: How per-sample Jacobians are combined: averaging over positions within a prompt, then per-element mean or median across prompts, with outlier filtering at both stages.
    - lens-corpus | Lens corpus and convergence: The data the Jacobian expectation is averaged over: one thousand 128-token pretraining-like sequences by default, with the J-lens beating baselines from as few as 10 prompts.
    - target-position-variants | Target-position variants: Choices of which output positions the Jacobian targets: all $t' \geq t$ (default), self-only ($t' = t$), or future-only ($t' > t$).
    - target-layer-choice | Target layer for the backward pass: Which layer's residual stream sits at the output end of the derivative: the final layer, or the penultimate one, with the last transformer block left out of the backward pass.
  - j-lens-vector | J-lens vector: One vector per vocabulary token per layer: the direction in residual-stream space that, on average across contexts, disposes the model to say that token.
  - j-lens-visualization | Interactive J-lens visualization: The interactive tool the authors used throughout their research: a grid of top lens tokens over (position, layer) cells, with pinned-token heatmaps and rank trajectories.
  - lens-evaluation | Lens comparison evaluations: The paper's qualitative and quantitative framework for comparing the J-lens, logit lens, and tuned lens: benchmarks of known intermediates, readout metrics, and causal intervention metrics.
    - ablation-kl-effect | Ablation KL effect: A causal metric: the KL divergence induced on the model's output by projecting out a lens's intermediate direction at the readout position.
    - inter-lens-agreement | Inter-lens agreement by layer: Pairwise comparison of the three lenses — vector cosines, symmetric KL between readouts, and top-1 agreement — which independently picks out the workspace layer boundaries.
      - tuned-lens-bias-term | The tuned lens's bias term: The tuned lens is an affine map, and the paper finds that in early layers its whole advantage over the logit lens comes from the learned bias rather than from its linear part.
    - intermediate-concept-benchmarks | Intermediate-concept benchmarks: Six prompt distributions in which the model must compute a known single-token intermediate concept that is neither in the input nor identical to the predicted output.
      - association-benchmark | Association benchmark: 50 short evocative passages that depict a concept through behavioral cues without naming it, checking whether the implied concept surfaces in the lens.
      - multihop-benchmark | Multihop benchmark: 50 two-hop factual questions requiring a bridging entity — for example, computing Mars to answer that the color of the fourth planet is red.
      - multilingual-benchmark | Multilingual benchmark: 54 prompts written entirely in a non-English language, testing whether the model routes through English-aligned representations.
      - order-of-operations-benchmark | Order-of-operations benchmark: 55 arithmetic expressions like (2 + 3) × 4, where a partial result must be computed before the remaining operation is applied.
      - poetry-benchmark | Poetry benchmark: 52 rhyming couplets where the lens is read at the newline between lines, looking for the second line's planned final word.
      - typo-benchmark | Typo benchmark: 96 sentences ending on a common misspelling, checking whether the correctly spelled word appears in the lens at the misspelled token.
    - pass-at-k-auc | Pass@k AUC: The summary metric for intermediate recovery: pass@k computed over a range of k, plotted against $\log k$, with the area under the curve normalized so a method that always ranks the intermediate first scores 1.
    - swap-success-rate | Swap success rate: A causal metric: the fraction of items whose top-1 output flips to the implied answer when one intermediate is exchanged for another via a lens-coordinate swap.
  - multi-token-lens-extensions | Multi-token concept extensions: Two methods — the template lens and the oracle lens — for extending lens readouts beyond concepts that happen to be single vocabulary tokens.
    - oracle-lens | Oracle lens: A method that decodes arbitrary-length phrases from an activation: a fine-tuned oracle model proposes phrases whose template vectors, combined sparsely, reconstruct the activation.
      - delimiter-commentary | Continuations versus commentary: The observation that oracle readouts at most positions predict upcoming text, but at delimiter tokens they often read as the model's running commentary on the situation, sometimes in the first person.
      - oracle-training | Oracle training pipeline: The four-stage recipe: train the reconstructor, build a 3.4-million-phrase dictionary, label activations with a matching-pursuit teacher, then train the oracle by supervision and RL on reconstruction reward.
      - reconstructor-model | Reconstructor model: A fine-tuned copy of the subject model that maps a short phrase to the residual-stream activation that would immediately precede it — a learned template generator.
    - template-lens | Template lens: A method that derives a vector for any pre-enumerated word or phrase from averaged activations preceding it, usable for readout and intervention like a J-lens vector.
      - template-construction | Template construction and whitening: The recipe $t_w(\ell) = (\Sigma_\ell + \lambda I)^{-1}(\mu_w(\ell) - \mu(\ell))$: a centered, whitened mean activation, which is the linear discriminant for contexts about to say $w$.
  - single-token-limitation | Single-token limitation: The J-lens can only directly name concepts that have single-token names in the tokenizer's vocabulary; multi-token and diffusely tokenized concepts are read out imperfectly or not at all.
  - uninterpretable-readouts | Inconsistent interpretability of readouts: At some positions and layers the top lens tokens resist interpretation, and the paper has not characterized when or why.
  - lens-readout-reading-heuristics | Reading a lens readout: The rules the paper gives for interpreting a list of top lens tokens: treat it as a bag of ideas, look for families of related words, and trust an idea more when it recurs across layers or neighbouring positions.
  - readout-method-tradeoffs | Where the J-lens sits among readout methods: The paper places the J-lens on a spectrum of activation-readout tools that trade expressivity against cost and grounding, and puts it at the cheap and grounded end.

PREREQUISITES:
  - j-lens-vector | J-lens vector: One vector per vocabulary token per layer: the direction in residual-stream space that, on average across contexts, disposes the model to say that token.
  - jacobian-matrix | Jacobian matrix: The matrix of partial derivatives of one vector-valued quantity with respect to another; the linear map that best approximates the effect of a small perturbation.
  - logit-lens | Logit lens: An earlier interpretability technique that decodes intermediate residual-stream states into the model's output vocabulary by applying the final unembedding matrix at every layer.
  - residual-stream | Residual stream: The running vector at each token position that a transformer's layers read from and write to; the activations that all the paper's readouts and interventions act on.
  - unembedding-matrix | Unembedding matrix: The final linear map from the residual stream to scores over the vocabulary, from which the model's next-token distribution is computed.
  - verbalizable-representation | Verbalizable representations: Representations poised to be put into words should the occasion arise, as distinct from representations that merely happen to be verbalized in one particular context.

  [load-bearing] -> eq-jlens-matrix (The averaged Jacobian, one matrix per layer (eq-jlens-matrix)) | supported-by | defines the averaged lens matrix: The equation averages the Jacobian from layer $\ell$ to the final layer over the source position, over every later position in the context, and over one thousand pretraining-like prompts, leaving a single matrix per layer. The averaging is what separates a general disposition to say a token from the particular use one context puts that token to.
  [load-bearing] <- tuned-lens (Tuned lens) | contrasts-with | output match versus intermediate recovery: The tuned lens is trained to match the model's own next-token distribution, and it is the best match at every depth; the J-lens is the worst of the three, with higher KL than even the uncorrected logit lens through most of the network. The paper reads that loss as a feature, because on the same prompts the tuned lens skips ahead to the answer while the J-lens surfaces the unspoken intermediate. The two metrics disagree, and the paper chooses the causal one.
  [supporting] <- logit-lens (Logit lens) | contrasts-with | identity shortcut versus averaged Jacobian: The logit lens is what the J-lens becomes when the per-layer matrix is set to the identity, which is a fair approximation late in the network and degrades earlier. The two agree closely in the final layers and diverge before them. The paper reports that the logit lens still captures much of the workspace structure at somewhat lower reliability, so the correction buys accuracy in early layers rather than a different picture.
  [supporting] <- template-lens (Template lens) | contrasts-with | multi-token coverage bought with cost: The template lens names whole words such as blackmail, photosynthesis and Tchaikovsky where the J-lens prints only the fragments black, phot and tch, and its readout and swap performance stay roughly flat as the intermediate grows from one to four tokens. The coverage is paid for: a template needs a few hundred forward passes per word against one set of backward passes per layer, its vocabulary must be fixed in advance, and it inherits the tuned lens's habit of skipping to the answer.
  [supporting] <- sparse-autoencoder (Sparse autoencoder (SAE)) | contrasts-with | trained dictionary versus one matrix: A sparse autoencoder must be trained, and its features carry no names until someone labels them. The J-lens is a single matrix multiplication per layer, with the matrix computed once per model, and every direction already carries a vocabulary token. The paper treats them as complements rather than rivals, and reads SAE decoder directions back through the lens to sort them by kurtosis.
  [supporting] <- linear-probe (Linear probe) | contrasts-with | one supervised concept versus whole vocabulary: A linear probe is trained for one researcher-specified concept at a time, and what it reads is correlational. The J-lens supplies a direction for every token in the vocabulary without supervision, and those directions are derived from their average effect on the output. That is why the paper tests them by ablation and swap rather than by classification accuracy.

Steering the feature down on the blackmail evaluation cuts the rate at which the model says out loud that the scenario is a test from 28 of 50 rollouts to 10 of 50, though the causal test covers one feature on one prompt.
  - fig-91 | Both ends of three translation features: Projecting a transcoder feature's encoder and decoder directions separately through the J-lens makes the feature's input-to-output map readable without running the model on any data. In all three cases shown, the encoder side reads a concept expressed across many languages together with common French words, and the decoder side writes the opening token of the French word for that concept.
  - fig-92 | Arithmetic transcoder features under the J-lens: Projecting a transcoder feature's encoder and decoder directions separately through the J-lens recovers what the feature reads and what it writes, without running any prompts. For three two-digit-addition features, those weight-only readouts match the firing patterns measured earlier on 10,000 addition prompts.
  - fig-93 | The British-Spelling Nationality Head: A single attention head is read two ways at once: by its attention pattern on a passage, and by pushing each of its four weight matrices through the J-lens. The two readings agree — the head fires on British spellings and writes Commonwealth nationality — which is the paper's demonstration that composing the lens with a component's weights can name what that component reads and what it writes, using no prompt data at all.
  - fig-94 | Three more heads read through their weights: Three further attention heads are shown in the format of Figure 93: five attention snippets on the left, and on the right the top six vocabulary tokens for each of the head's four weight matrices projected through the J-lens. The point the section draws from them is a structural one — heads whose weights project interpretably tend to give similar token rankings across all four matrices — and no function is proposed for any individual head here.
  - eq-lens-swap | Swapping two concepts in lens coordinates: These three expressions define the paper's concept-exchange surgery: read how much of a source concept and a target concept an activation carries along their two J-lens directions, exchange those two numbers, and write the difference back. Everything the activation carries outside the plane spanned by the two lens vectors is left exactly as it was, so the edit swaps one concept for another rather than replacing the activation.
