NAME: Swap-success chart

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
A pass/fail scoreboard for the lens-coordinate swap: the fraction of trials on which exchanging two concepts' coordinates along lens directions moved the swapped-in concept's answer to the model's top-1 output. It appears as bars across models, templates or lenses, and as a per-trial grid that prints what the model actually said.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
### Why this chart and not another

A lens readout that surfaces the right word has shown only that a direction displays a concept. The [[lens-coordinate-swap]] tests something stronger: it reads an activation's coordinates along two [[j-lens-vector|lens vectors]], exchanges them, and leaves the rest of the activation alone. An average shift in log-probability would let many small effects that never dislodge the original answer sum into an apparent result. The [[swap-success-rate]] instead puts a yes-or-no question to each trial — whether the top-1 output token became the answer implied by the swapped-in concept — and plots the fraction that passed.

That criterion places the baseline near zero, not at one half. Without the intervention the model answers for the argument in the prompt, and the swapped-in answer must climb the whole vocabulary to reach rank 1. Fifty percent is therefore a large effect, not a coin flip.

Spread is the second reason for a chart, not a number. An aggregate hides that success runs from near-total in one argument category to zero in another, and hides whether a failure was a near miss. The per-trial grid exists for that: it prints the model's actual output in every cell, so a near miss reads differently from a total failure.

### How to read it, step by step

In the summary form the horizontal axis is categorical — models of the [[claude-model-family]], the sixteen frames of the [[function-template-protocol]], or the lens compared. The vertical axis is a fraction from 0 to 1, and one bar or point is a whole set of trials: its height is the share whose top-1 token matched the target-appropriate answer. Where three lenses are compared, colour carries the method — black for the [[jacobian-lens]], red for the [[logit-lens]], blue for the [[tuned-lens]] — and position carries the model. In Figure 19 marker shape carries [[swap-strength-alpha|swap strength]] instead: a filled circle at $\alpha = 1$, a cross at $\alpha = 2$. Whiskers, where captioned, are 95% [[wilson-interval|Wilson intervals]]. The height reads against zero rather than against one half; the next question is whether the intervals overlap, and at $n = 50$ many do.

The grid form holds the same trials one at a time. A row fixes the argument written in the prompt, a column the argument swapped in, and each column's sub-heading gives the answer the template implies for the swapped-in argument. A cell prints the model's top-1 token after the swap. The four diagonal cells exchange a coordinate with itself, so they are the no-op control and should reproduce the clean answer. The twelve off-diagonal cells are the trials. A small grey number beside a token gives the rank of the target-appropriate answer when that rank is not 1 — the near-misses that the summary bar counts as failures.

### What a bad result would look like

Bars flat against zero: the swap changes coordinates and the output never moves. Or J-lens bars level with the logit- and tuned-lens bars, which would say the intervention works on any direction assigned to that token and the J-lens found nothing particular. In the grid, off-diagonal cells printing the row's own answer with grey ranks in the hundreds, or none at all, would mean the swap did not push toward the target, and a diagonal cell departing from the clean answer would indict the harness. In the depth control of Figure 15, two curves rising at the same depth would leave the intermediate swap indistinguishable from one that smuggled the answer in ([[swap-depth-control]]).

  - j-lens-vector | J-lens vector: One vector per vocabulary token per layer: the direction in residual-stream space that, on average across contexts, disposes the model to say that token.
  - lens-coordinate-swap | Lens-coordinate swap: An intervention that exchanges one concept for another: it reads an activation's coordinates along two J-lens vectors, permutes them, and writes the result back, leaving everything orthogonal to those vectors unchanged.
  - residual-stream | Residual stream: The running vector at each token position that a transformer's layers read from and write to; the activations that all the paper's readouts and interventions act on.
  - j-space | J-space: The set of activation-space points expressible as a sparse nonnegative combination of J-lens vectors — the region of representation space the paper argues functions as a global workspace.
