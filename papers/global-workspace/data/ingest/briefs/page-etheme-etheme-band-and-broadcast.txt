NAME: Where the workspace sits in depth, and how its contents reach the rest of the model

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
Four statistics derived from the lens — next-token accuracy, readout kurtosis, top-1 autocorrelation and effective dimensionality — change sharply at the same two depths, and that is what fixes the workspace band (fig-28). Below the band the readouts carry essentially nothing, which the paper flags may be a degeneracy of the lens at those depths rather than a fact about the model; above it the readout tips over into the tokens the model is about to say. Inside the band the model's weights treat J-space directions differently from others: MLP blocks amplify them roughly ten times more than random directions, and the top 1% of heads, selected by OV gain and by whether they copy directions without scrambling them, relay those directions between positions. Zeroing those broadcast heads degrades what the J-space holds while leaving next-token prediction largely unchanged (fig-34). At the same onset depth the model stops tracking a blended input smoothly and commits to one reading of it, which the paper offers as an analogue of ignition and quantifies as the width of the interval over which projection share moves from 10% to 90%.

  e015 [supporting] j-rotated-control (J-rotated control population) --depends-on--> sparse-subframe (Sparse subframe) | control isolates token alignment alone: Applying one fixed random orthogonal rotation to the J-lens vectors keeps their spectrum and pairwise geometry while destroying their alignment with particular vocabulary tokens.   e017 [supporting] j-space-broadcast (Broadcast privilege of J-lens vectors) --depends-on--> two-time-dimensions (Two time dimensions) | two axes force two measurements: In a transformer, information travels across layers within a position and across the sequence via attention.   e028 [load-bearing] transition-width (Transition width) --depends-on--> projection-share (Projection share) | width defined on the share curve: Transition width is the change in the mixture weight $\alpha$ needed for the projection share to move from 10% to 90%.   e051 [supporting] ignition (Ignition) --supported-by--> fig-29 (The Ambiguity Sweep (fig-29)) | commitment sharpens at workspace onset: A concept token's input embedding is replaced by a weighted mixture of two country embeddings, over sixteen pairs and forty carrier sentences.   e075 [load-bearing] j-space-broadcast (Broadcast privilege of J-lens vectors) --instance-of--> global-broadcast (Global broadcast) | broadcast, in the model's weights: Global broadcast is the workspace's defining act in the neuroscience theory: making one representation available to many downstream processes at once.   e077 [supporting] bimodal-commitment (Bimodal responses to maximal ambiguity) --instance-of--> ignition (Ignition) | ignition, seen per trial: Ignition is the theory's name for a late, all-or-none commitment to one reading of an ambiguous input.   e078 [supporting] broadcast-heads (Broadcast heads) --instance-of--> attention-head (Attention head) | a selected subset of heads: Broadcast heads are ordinary attention heads, not a separate component.   e110 [supporting] excess-variance-explained (Excess variance explained) --contrasts-with--> j-space-broadcast (Broadcast privilege of J-lens vectors) | small variance share, outsized reach: The top J-lens vectors explain no more than 10% of activation variance beyond a same-size random control, so the model's activations are dominated by content outside the J-space. Across the workspace band, MLP blocks nonetheless amplify J-space-aligned directions roughly ten times more than random ones. A small share of the representation is wired for a disproportionate share of the distribution.
  e113 [supporting] two-time-dimensions (Two time dimensions) --contrasts-with--> cortical-recurrent-broadcast (Recurrent cortical broadcast) | two separate axes versus recurrent loops: A transformer moves information along two axes: across layers within a position, and across the sequence through attention. The brain does not separate the two, and broadcast there is held to run through recurrent loops and long-range cortical connections. This is where the paper says its analogy is weakest, matching global workspace theory at the level of function rather than implementation.
  e114 [supporting] emergent-rather-than-designed-workspace (Emerged, not built in) --contrasts-with--> workspace-inspired-architectures (Workspace-inspired architectures) | emerged in training versus built in: Earlier proposals install a workspace in a network on purpose, as an architectural bottleneck. Nothing in the transformer supplies such a module. The paper's claim is that the organization it finds arose during training, which is what lets it read the result as evidence about what learning systems converge on.
  e115 [load-bearing] motor-layers (Motor layers) --contrasts-with--> workspace-layers (Workspace layer range) | imminent output versus intermediate computation: The paper splits depth into a middle band whose J-space carries intermediate computations and late layers tied to the model's imminent output, where the Jacobian approaches the identity and the readout collapses toward the plain unembedding. It states that this late boundary was fixed empirically and somewhat post hoc, with no principled definition separating a workspace representation from a motor one. Next-token predictions do sometimes appear in the middle range as well.
  e116 [supporting] early-layer-lens-degeneracy (Lens degeneracy in early layers) --contrasts-with--> sensory-layers (Sensory layers) | empty band or unreadable band: The J-space carries essentially no content through roughly the first third of depth. Two readings compete: those layers may compute content that genuinely does not belong to a workspace, or they may carry verbalizable content in a geometry the lens cannot read, since the averaged Jacobian relates each layer to the final one and the early layers sit far from it. The CKA block structure marks the early band as a distinct regime but does not decide between the two.
  e118 [supporting] broadcast-head-ablation (Broadcast-head ablation) --contrasts-with--> next-token-prediction (Next-token prediction) | contents disrupted, output largely unchanged: Zeroing the top 1% of workspace-layer heads identified as broadcast heads drops J-lens recall@25 to 0.67 at the most affected layers, against 0.86 for equal-sized random head sets. The model's top-1 next token changes at only 5% of positions, against 2% for the control. The paper reads the gap as the heads acting on the workspace's contents rather than on the output, with reporting of an injected thought falling from 0.54 to 0.09.
