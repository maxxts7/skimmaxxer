SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
The handful of chart shapes this paper argues with, each explained once: why that shape was chosen, how to read it, and what it would look like if the claim it supports were false.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
This paper makes the same kinds of measurement over and over - on the Arabic feature, then the DNA one, then base64, then Hebrew - and plots each the same way. That repetition is the point: once you can read one activation spectrum you can read all of them, and the comparison across cases is where the argument actually lives. Each page below takes one of those shapes and says what question forced it, how to get information out of it, and what a negative result would look like on it.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-reading-the-charts | How to read this paper's charts - The paper argues with a small number of chart shapes, each repeated across many figures. This chapter explains each shape once: what it plots, why that shape was chosen, how to read it, and what it would look like if the paper's claim were false. It teaches how to read the evidence rather than what the evidence says, so it stands apart from the argument and sits at the end, where a reader can return to it while working through the figures.

SUB-CONCEPTS (these render as expandable sections BELOW your page - reference them, do not duplicate them):
  - ambiguity-commitment-plot | Ambiguity commitment plot: A readout of how far an activation has moved between two blended concepts as the input blend is swept, plotted against depth. It is the paper's test of whether the model commits to one interpretation of a deliberately ambiguous input, and at which layer that commitment appears.
  - concept-token-detector-plot | Concept-token detector plot: A fixed list of words is looked for in the top-k J-lens readout at every workspace layer, and the hits for one prompt are summed into a single count; dots are prompts, bars are means, and a clean baseline sitting at zero is what makes the count readable. It is the paper's screening evidence that a trained-in disposition leaves a trace on ordinary prompts.
  - intervention-vs-control-bars | Score-under-intervention bar chart: Bars giving a rate or score measured while an intervention is applied to the model, printed beside a control perturbation matched in size, layer and position, with per-item dots and error whiskers. It exists so the reader can judge whether an effect belongs to what was removed or merely to how much was removed.
  - layer-position-readout-trace | Layer-by-layer lens readout trace: A grid of (token position × layer) cells taken from one instrumented forward pass, each cell printing the top-ranked J-lens token for that activation, with hover for the top ten. It shows what an activation disposes the model to say, and at what depth, rather than what the model is about to say next.
  - lens-attribution-graph | J-lens attribution graph: A wiring diagram of a single forward pass whose nodes are J-lens vectors picked out by sparse decomposition and whose edges are one-layer gradient attributions, paired with a bar panel of coordinate swaps that tests whether the drawn wiring is causally real. It is for showing how a computation is organized, not how strong any one quantity is.
  - method-schematic | Method and convention schematic: A drawing that defines rather than measures: how the Jacobian lens is built, read and written, which layers the paper calls the workspace, and how strong each ablation dose is. Its job is to fix the meaning of terms and axes that every later measurement depends on.
  - ranked-readout-panel | Ranked lens-readout panel: A column of the top few vocabulary tokens read off one object — an activation at a marked position, or a static weight direction — repeated under two or three conditions so the contents can be compared. It is how the paper displays what the workspace is carrying, and it reads by the identity and printed rank of the tokens, never by the length of the bars across columns.
  - swap-success-chart | Swap-success chart: A pass/fail scoreboard for the lens-coordinate swap: the fraction of trials on which exchanging two concepts' coordinates along lens directions moved the swapped-in concept's answer to the model's top-1 output. It appears as bars across models, templates or lenses, and as a per-trial grid that prints what the model actually said.
  - three-lens-comparison | Three-lens benchmark comparison: A fixed three-way comparison — Jacobian lens in black, logit lens in red, tuned lens in blue — repeated across benchmarks, metrics and swept variables. It exists to test whether one lens leads consistently rather than at one convenient setting.
  - token-shaded-transcript | Token-shaded transcript: A passage or a set of model responses printed in full, with every unit shaded by a measured scalar and hoverable for the number behind it, so a reader can see where in the text the quantity was large. Each panel's ramp saturates at its own maximum, so the shading ranks positions inside a panel and does not compare across panels.
  - turn-paired-rank-plot | User-turn vs Assistant-turn rank plot: Four bars in two pairs: how highly a chosen concept word ranks in the J-lens readout while the model is reading the user's message, and while it is writing its own response, for a base model and its post-trained counterpart. It is the shape section 6 uses to argue that post-training moves the Assistant's reaction forward, into the user's turn.
  - weights-only-population-comparison | Weights-only population comparison: A curve per population of candidate directions, plotted against source layer, showing how strongly the model's weights amplify, connect to or compose with each population — computed from weights alone and normalized so a random direction scores 1, or read against an explicit chance line. It is how the paper argues the J-space is wired for broadcast, and the argument lives in the gap between populations rather than in any curve's height.
  - workspace-band-profile | Workspace-band profile: A statistic of the J-space's contents plotted against layer depth — or, in the word-list case, against position in a running list — with percentile bands and a random or shuffled control that fixes the zero level. Its job is to locate the two depths where the curve changes slope, the start and end of the workspace band, rather than to report any absolute value.
