NAME: The five workspace-like properties

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
The paper's operational definition: a set of vector representations counts as workspace-like if it supports verbal report, directed modulation, internal reasoning, flexible generalization, and selectivity.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
The paper defines what it would mean for a language model to have a global workspace by five testable properties, each mirroring a functional signature of conscious access in humans: information that is reportable, subject to top-down control, the medium of deliberate reasoning, flexibly generalizable, and selective. Each property is stated as a claim about a candidate subset of vector representations and is tested by measurement and intervention experiments. The paper reports identifying the candidate set through the first property, [[verbal-report]], and then finding, described as rather surprising, that the same set satisfies the other four.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-the-workspace-claim | The workspace claim - The paper starts from a neuroscience theory: information becomes consciously accessible when it is posted to a shared, capacity-limited workspace and broadcast to many processes at once. It then makes its central claim, that a language model maintains a comparable privileged subset of vector representations. This chapter sets out the operational definition the rest of the paper is scored against — five functional properties and three structural signatures — and the paper's insistence that nothing in the transformer supplies such a workspace by design. It comes first because every later result is measured against this definition.

SUB-CONCEPTS (these render as expandable sections BELOW your page - reference them, do not duplicate them):
  - directed-modulation | Directed modulation: The property that the model can deliberately activate and compute with workspace vectors when instructed, independent of its outputs, and can pull normally absent information into the workspace when a task requires it.
    - character-counting-task | Character-counting task: A task in which the model silently tracks the number of characters in each line of a passage, used to probe covert numeric content in the J-space.
    - hold-in-mind-copy-task | Hold-in-mind copying task: A protocol in which the model copies an unrelated sentence while an instruction tells it what to hold in mind; the lens is read at the copied tokens.
    - instruction-conditions | Instruction conditions (focus, ignore, mention, don't-think): The four instruction types compared in the modulation experiments, which produce different levels of target-concept activation in the J-space.
      - instruction-phrasing-variation | Phrasing spread within a condition: Each instruction condition is instantiated with five to eight different wordings, and the per-wording rates are plotted individually rather than only as a condition mean.
    - j-orthogonalized-property-probe | J-orthogonalized property probe: A linear probe for a stimulus property with its J-space component projected out, used to show that instructions move the J-space but not the underlying representation.
      - reverse-dissociation | Reverse dissociation (probe up, label absent): The second half of the dissociation: for some categories a real stimulus drives the property probe strongly while the property's name stays out of the J-lens entirely.
    - paired-question-protocol | Paired-question protocol: Presenting the same stimulus under two different questions to show that the question alone determines whether a property's label enters the J-space.
    - white-bear-effect | White bear effect: The human finding that instructions to suppress a thought increase its occurrence relative to no instruction at all; the paper observes a parallel in the J-space.
    - workspace-competition | Workspace competition under concurrent covert tasks: When the model holds two covert tasks at once, static concepts share workspace tokens freely, but an ongoing computation excludes other content from the tokens it occupies.
      - co-occupancy | Co-occupancy: A named quantity: at tokens where one covert task's content is in the lens top-10, the probability that the other task's content is too.
        - shuffled-occupancy-control | Shuffled partner control: The baseline against which co-occupancy is read: the partner task's presence at a token is taken from a different prompt, one where that task was given on its own.
      - dual-task-cost | Cost of holding two covert tasks: A separate measurement from token-level sharing: how often each task's content still reaches the top of the lens anywhere in the response when the two are given together rather than alone.
    - imagine-instruction-paradigm | Imagine-instruction paradigm: A three-way comparison in which a stimulus with one property is presented neutrally, presented under an instruction to imagine it has a different property, and set against a matched stimulus that genuinely has that other property.
    - modulation-trial-scoring | Scoring a modulation trial: The rule that decides whether a single modulation trial counts as a hit, which differs between the two concept-style task families and the line-width task.
    - no-instruction-baseline | No-instruction baseline: A control condition in which the target concept is never mentioned in the prompt, whose rate is approximately zero across all task families.
    - modulation-model-size-trend | Modulation and model size: Under a focus instruction, the rate at which the target reaches the J-lens tends to rise with model size; the paper states this as a tendency rather than a measured scaling law.
  - flexible-generalization | Flexible generalization (broadcast): The property that the same workspace vector serves as a valid argument to many different downstream computations: lifted from one context and placed in another, it is correctly operated on by whatever function the new context supplies.
    - function-template-protocol | Function-template protocol: The design of the flexible-generalization experiment: four argument categories, four functions each, and twelve source-target swap pairs per function.
    - workspace-loading | Workspace loading: A named quantity: the cosine similarity between the residual stream and a concept's lens vector, averaged over the relevant positions of an unmodified forward pass.
    - prompt-independent-swap | Prompt-independent swap: The design choice that turns the swap experiment into a test of broadcast: the same lens-coordinate swap is applied unchanged in every prompt, and each downstream function is left to interpret it.
    - swap-grid-diagonal-baseline | Swap grid and its diagonal baseline: The 4x4 grid used to report every individual swap trial for a function template: rows are the argument written in the prompt, columns the argument whose lens coordinates were swapped in, so the diagonal is the unmodified run.
      - partial-swap-rank-evidence | Partial swaps read off rank: The paper's reading of its own failures: in most failed trials the target answer is not absent but ranked below the original answer, which it takes as a swap that moved the activation in the right direction but not far enough.
    - number-word-failure-interpretation | Two readings of the number-word failure: Number-word arguments have the lowest workspace loading and swap worst, and the paper offers two explanations it cannot separate.
    - swap-effect-magnitude | Magnitude of swap effect: The graded measure plotted against workspace loading - how far the swap shifts the model's output towards the target's answer - as distinct from the binary top-1 criterion behind the headline counts.
  - flexible-vs-automatic-dissociation | Flexible versus automatic cognition: The paper's proposed criterion for when a computation engages the J-space: it does when an intermediate result must be handed to an arbitrary, context-specified downstream circuit, and does not when the computation is automatic.
    - language-passage-tasks | Language-passage task battery: Four tasks over the same foreign-language passage, chosen so that all depend on the passage's language but only some should require the workspace.
      - anomaly-detection-condition | Anomaly detection under a swapped language: One of the four passage conditions splices a sentence of another language into the passage and asks whether anything is out of place. The judgment survives a swap that overwrites the surrounding language.
    - linewrap-task | Linewrap task (information pulled into the J-space on demand): A character-count task where the count is absent from the J-space during automatic line-wrapping but appears when a question requires reporting or computing with it.
    - naming-vs-avoidance | Naming versus avoiding a primed concept: An experiment showing early workspace layers are required to deliberately avoid a primed concept, while late layers are required to name it.
      - inclusion-exclusion-paradigm | Inclusion/exclusion paradigm: A paradigm from human memory research in which the ability to deliberately avoid a primed response, rather than produce it, is taken as a marker of conscious processing.
      - avoidance-item-filter | Item selection for the naming and avoidance test: The experiment keeps only the 63 items on which the unablated model both names the implied concept correctly and avoids it correctly.
    - presence-without-causal-role | Presence in the readout versus causal role: A J-lens readout can name a concept at a position where that concept is not driving the model's behaviour. The language-passage experiment separates the two.
    - operational-automaticity | Automaticity as an operational definition: The paper uses "automatic" as a label for computations it finds to be J-space-independent, and flags that this matches human automaticity only partially.
    - generic-prior-fallback | Fallback to a generic prior under ablation: On ordinary pretraining text, ablating the J-space leaves the model fluent but shifts its continuation from a context-specific inference to a generic one.
    - ablation-output-token-exemption | Exempting the model's own top tokens from ablation: The J-space ablation skips any token appearing in the top ten of a clean forward pass, so that it targets internal reasoning rather than the model's intention to report.
    - automatic-misalignment-blind-spot | Automatic behaviour as a limit on J-space monitoring: Because automatic computations do not route through the J-space, a misaligned behaviour that has become a fixed circuit could run without appearing in a J-lens readout.
    - automaticity-criterion-limits | How far the flexible/automatic criterion has been tested: The paper states that it cannot predict, for an arbitrary computation, whether that computation will engage the J-space.
    - attentional-selection-analog | The unidentified mechanism that admits content: The same information enters the J-space or not depending on what the model is asked to do, which the paper reads as some analog of attentional selection without identifying the mechanism.
  - internal-reasoning | Internal reasoning: The property that workspace vectors carry the values of intermediate computations when the model chains inferential steps, and that intervening on them redirects the conclusion.
    - arithmetic-intermediate-ordering | Arithmetic intermediates surface in computation order: In a two-step arithmetic problem, the intermediate values reach the top of the J-lens at successively later layers, in the order the computation requires.
    - bandit-choice-prompt | Bandit choice prompt: A reward-driven A/B choice task in which the lens surfaces the model's selected strategy (repeat or switch) and swapping it flips the choice.
    - intermediate-probe | Intermediate probe: A J-lens-free representation of an unspoken intermediate, built by averaging activations over prompts that imply the same intermediate through different surface cues.
      - complement-clamped-swap-control | Complement-clamped swap control: A control in which one half of the probe is swapped while the other half is held at its clean-pass value, testing whether the half being swapped acts on its own or only by disturbing the other.
    - rhyme-planning | Rhyme planning: When completing a rhyming couplet, the model selects the line-ending rhyme in advance, and this planned word is visible in the J-lens and steerable.
    - shared-multilingual-representation | Shared multilingual representation: The finding that multilingual models route some computation through an English-aligned intermediate representation, visible here as English tokens in the lens during a Chinese task.
      - target-language-representation | Explicit target-language token: In the Chinese antonym prompt the lens shows the token Chinese alongside the English intermediate, which the paper reads as the model representing which language to answer in.
    - swap-depth-control | Intermediate-versus-answer swap depth control: A control comparing at which layers swapping the intermediate versus swapping the answer takes effect, to rule out the intermediate's lens vector containing the answer.
    - same-category-swap-target | Same-category swap target: In the systematic two-hop experiment, the concept swapped in for the true intermediate is drawn at random from the same category as it - another animal, another country.
    - ablation-multihop-positive-control | Multi-hop positive control for ablation: Before the ablation is used to survey what the J-space is needed for, it is run on the two-hop reasoning prompts to confirm that it removes the content the earlier sections attributed to the J-space.
    - chaining-of-mental-operations | Chaining of mental operations: The human signature behind the paper's third property: conscious access is held to be the medium of effortful, step-by-step reasoning, where one operation is applied to the result of another.
  - selectivity | Selectivity: The property that the workspace is a small subset of the model's total representational content, required for only a fraction of its behavior and not for pervasive routine processing.
  - verbal-report | Verbal report from the J-space: The property that, when asked what it is thinking about, the model names concepts represented in the workspace, and swapping one active workspace vector for another changes its answer to match.
    - injected-thought-introspection | Injected-thought introspection protocol: A protocol in which the model is told a thought may have been implanted in its activations and is asked to report what, if anything, it detects.
      - user-turn-concept-injection | Injecting a concept across the user turn: A single J-lens vector is added at positions on the user prompt rather than in the model's own turn, and the model then reports that concept when asked to introspect.
        - report-time-selectivity | Selectivity of the injected concept to the moment of report: A concept injected on the user turn does not leak into earlier assistant text; it surfaces only where the introspective report is being given.
    - think-of-a-category-task | Think-of-a-category task: A prompt asking the model to think of an item from a named category and then name it in one word; the lens is read just before the name is produced.
      - candidate-ranking-agreement | Candidate ranking agreement between lens and logits: For each of fourteen categories, ten candidate answers are ranked twice - by their strength in the J-lens readout and by their next-token logits - and the two orderings are compared with a Spearman correlation.
      - out-of-top-10-swap-targets | Swap targets drawn from outside the top 10: The item swapped in is always one the model was not already likely to say - drawn from the same category but excluded if it sat in the top 10 of the output distribution.
    - report-relation-by-construction | Report relation expected by construction: The J-lens is built from the causal effect of activations on output tokens, so some agreement between its readouts and what the model says is guaranteed in advance rather than discovered.
    - j-space-privilege-for-report | Privilege of the J-space component for report: Both report experiments are rerun with a concept vector's J-space component and its non-J-space remainder substituted for the J-lens vectors, at equal magnitude; the small J-space part nearly matches the lens vectors while the large remainder barely works.
      - j-space-mediation-clamp | Clamping the J-space to test mediation: The non-J-space conditions are rerun with the relevant J-lens coordinates held at their clean-pass values, so the injected concept cannot be re-derived into the J-space; the remaining effect then vanishes.
