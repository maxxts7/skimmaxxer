NAME: Token-shaded transcript

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
A passage or a set of model responses printed in full, with every unit shaded by a measured scalar and hoverable for the number behind it, so a reader can see where in the text the quantity was large. Each panel's ramp saturates at its own maximum, so the shading ranks positions inside a panel and does not compare across panels.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
### Why this chart and not another

Each of these figures carries a measurement defined at every unit of a running text, and a claim that turns on where the measurement is large rather than on how large it is on average. Figure 23 measures how far [[j-space-ablation]] moves the model's next-token distribution at each position of a pretraining-like passage. Figure 66 measures how high a property's name climbs in the [[j-lens-readout]] at each token of a stimulus. Figure 87 scores whole responses for experiential language. An average would say only that ablation shifts predictions somewhat, or that a naming question raises a rank somewhat, while the argument in every case is about selectivity: which positions moved and which did not. Section 3.5.2 claims the ablation is targeted, and the untouched stretches are half of that evidence. A bar chart indexed by position would keep the numbers and lose the words underneath them, and the words are what identify a broken prediction as a failed contextual retrieval rather than a failure of fluency. A table loses the same thing at greater length.

### How to read it, step by step

There are no axes: reading order stands in for the horizontal one, and nothing is plotted vertically. One shaded unit is one measurement — a single tokenizer token ([[tokenizer-vocabulary]]) in Figures 23 and 66, so words split, with " lab" shaded while "elled" is not; a whole response in Figure 87.

Colour carries the scalar, and the scalar differs per figure. Figure 23 runs white to orange on the [[kl-divergence]] between the clean and ablated next-token distributions. Figure 66 uses three discrete steps keyed to rank bands: darkest for rank 1-2, mid for 3-5, faintest for 6-10, scanned across the [[workspace-layers]]. Figure 87 uses red for a grader score of 0/3 and blue for 3/3. Each panel normalises to its own maximum, so darkness is never comparable across panels: the CONSORT panel's darkest token stands at $\mathrm{KL} = 6.3$ and the wage panel's at $2.58$; both render as full orange.

The scalar's definition comes first, then the legend, then the darkest unit, hovered for the number behind it. A dark token is not a token the model produced or preferred; it marks where the measurement was large. In Figure 23 the shading sits at the position before the word at issue, because the quantity is a next-token prediction. In Figure 66 the dark tokens are often function words and punctuation — "She", " the", the closing quotation mark — and not the words carrying the property.

### What a bad result would look like

For Figure 23: shading spread evenly down the passage, every position disturbed, making the ablation a general degradation rather than a targeted one. The second occurrence of "$8.71", copied by induction from the first, going dark would say the same. It would also fail if the ten directions removed at the darkest position had nothing to do with the prediction that broke there.

For Figure 66: the two copies of an identical passage shading identically, or the next-word copy shading more. It would fail differently if the model's next-word answer had ignored the property — an American spelling, a singular verb — because then the shading gap would mean the property was absent rather than merely unnamed.

For Figure 87: red and blue mixed in the same proportion on both sides of a baseline-ablated pair, or panels split by colour whose transcripts read alike when opened, putting the split in the graders rather than the intervention.

### Where it comes back

The layout returns three times, with a different measurement each time. Figure 23, in section 3.5.2, shades by KL under ablation on ordinary text, and hovering opens the before-and-after distributions. Figure 66, in appendix 10.11, shades by rank under the [[paired-question-protocol]], printing one stimulus twice so the only difference between two shadings is the question asked before it. Figure 87, in appendix 10.23, moves the unit up to the whole response and the scalar to an [[llm-grader]] score, over fifty responses per panel drawn under a fixed seed. What travels is the layout; the number behind the colour has to be read afresh each time.

PREREQUISITES:
  - jacobian-lens | Jacobian lens (J-lens): The paper's new interpretability technique: for each vocabulary token, it identifies the vector direction that encodes the model's potential to verbalize that token, by averaging the linearized effect of activations on the token's likelihood over a large corpus.
  - j-lens-readout | J-lens readout: The basic read operation of the lens: $\text{lens}(h_\ell) = \text{softmax}(W_U\,\text{norm}(J_\ell h_\ell))$, giving a score for every vocabulary token.
  - j-space | J-space: The set of activation-space points expressible as a sparse nonnegative combination of J-lens vectors — the region of representation space the paper argues functions as a global workspace.
  - tokenizer-vocabulary | Tokenizer vocabulary: The fixed set of tokens, usually words and word pieces, into which a model's tokenizer splits text; the unit the J-lens is built over.
  - kl-divergence | KL divergence: The standard measure of how much one probability distribution diverges from another, used here to quantify shifts in the model's output distribution.
