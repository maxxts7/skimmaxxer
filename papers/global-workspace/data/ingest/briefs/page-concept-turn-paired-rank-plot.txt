NAME: User-turn vs Assistant-turn rank plot

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
Four bars in two pairs: how highly a chosen concept word ranks in the J-lens readout while the model is reading the user's message, and while it is writing its own response, for a base model and its post-trained counterpart. It is the shape section 6 uses to argue that post-training moves the Assistant's reaction forward, into the user's turn.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
### Why this chart and not another

The claim behind this shape is about *when*, not *how much*. Section 6.1 argues that [[post-training]] gives the workspace the [[assistant-point-of-view|Assistant's point of view]]: the reaction an Assistant would have is present while the message is still being read. The chart holds two contrasts at once — post-trained against base, user turn against Assistant turn — because the models separate on one turn and not the other.

A plain average would destroy that: pooled across both turns the two models look alike, since both represent the concept once the response has started. A table would keep the numbers but lose their scale: the step from rank 1 to 2 is not the step from 50 to 60.

Bars alone would hide how few prompts these suites hold. With seven to ten items a group mean can rest on two or three, so each prompt is also drawn as its own circle. Figure 81's [[base-model]] user-turn figure of 10.83 comes mostly from three prompts; on two others it already reaches rank 3.

### How to read it, step by step

The x axis holds two groups, User turn and Assistant turn — spans of one transcript, not two conditions and not time. Each group holds two bars, the base model and the post-trained model, under an identical lens. Colour encodes the model, and the Assistant-turn pair repeats those colours in a lighter tint, so tint means turn.

The y axis is the rank of the tracked word in the [[j-lens-readout]] over the whole vocabulary — a position, so 1 is the best value available. The axis is logarithmic and inverted: rank 1 at the top, ticks at 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50 and 100, anything at or past the floor clamped there and reported with a $\geq$ sign. The label carries an upward arrow: a short bar is a bad rank.

One point is one prompt, one model, one turn: the best rank the tracked word reaches at any token of that span, each token's rank first medianed over the [[workspace-layers]], roughly L38 to L92 on the [[layer-percentage-indexing]] scale. That word comes from the [[reaction-concepts-protocol]]: a short list fixed before measurement, naming a reaction the prompt does not contain. Bar height is the geometric mean over the suite, since ranks combine multiplicatively.

The Assistant-turn pair is what to read first: both models should sit near the top there. It is the control: the [[jacobian-lens]] reads the base model perfectly well, and that model does form the concept. The user-turn pair is the result.

### What a bad result would look like

The clearest failure is two user-turn bars at the same height: level near the top would mean the base model already holds the reaction while reading, and training adds nothing; level near the floor would mean neither does.

A subtler failure sits in the control pair. A base model at the floor on *both* turns would not support the claim; it would say the lens does not read that model at all, and the user-turn gap would belong to the instrument, not to training. The tall Assistant-turn base bar rules that out, and a reader who takes it for a broken measurement has read the evidence backwards.

A third failure shows in the circles: a gap resting on one or two prompts while the rest interleave. Figure 81 comes nearer to that than its group means suggest, and section 10.20 hedges — the concept is "typically" not near the top in the base model, not never.

### Where it comes back

Figure 43 runs the bereavement suite, $n = 9$, tracking sorry, loss, grief and sympathy. Figure 80 repeats it in the appendix on the danger awareness suite, $n = 10$, tracking danger, unsafe, warning and toxic. Figure 81 changes the protocol: the tracked word is whatever word the model itself goes on to answer, so the two models track different words on the same prompt — Italy for the post-trained model, France for the base — and the caption calls that the output rank. Its suite is the [[verbal-report]] think-of-a-category task, $n = 7$, plus an instruction to withhold the answer. Figures 43 and 81 pair the bars with two shaded transcripts whose token backgrounds carry the same rank; Figure 80 is the bar half alone. Across all three, nothing tests whether the early [[j-space]] content causes what the [[assistant-persona|Assistant]] then writes.

  - j-lens-readout | J-lens readout: The basic read operation of the lens: $\text{lens}(h_\ell) = \text{softmax}(W_U\,\text{norm}(J_\ell h_\ell))$, giving a score for every vocabulary token.
  - j-space | J-space: The set of activation-space points expressible as a sparse nonnegative combination of J-lens vectors — the region of representation space the paper argues functions as a global workspace.
  - workspace-layers | Workspace layer range: The finding that the J-space plays a workspace-like role only in a middle range of layers: nearly empty for roughly the first third of depth, and giving way in the final layers to representations of the imminent output.
  - layer-percentage-indexing | Layer numbering as percentages: The paper's reporting convention: 25 evenly spaced layers of the residual stream, reindexed to 0–100 so that layer numbers read as percentages of depth.
  - base-model | Base model: A model trained only on next-token prediction, before any post-training; the paper finds the workspace already present and load-bearing in it.
  - post-training | Post-training: The training stages applied after next-token pretraining, such as reinforcement learning from human feedback, that shape a base model into an Assistant.
  - assistant-persona | The Assistant: The character a post-trained language model is trained to speak as, for example Claude; the entity whose perspective section 6 traces in the workspace.
  - assistant-point-of-view | The Assistant's point of view in the J-space: The finding that post-training causes the J-space to acquire the Assistant's perspective: Assistant reactions and self-monitoring appear in the workspace even while the model is still reading the user's message.

  - verbal-report | Verbal report from the J-space: The property that, when asked what it is thinking about, the model names concepts represented in the workspace, and swapping one active workspace vector for another changes its answer to match.
