NAME: The Assistant's point of view in the J-space

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
The finding that post-training causes the J-space to acquire the Assistant's perspective: Assistant reactions and self-monitoring appear in the workspace even while the model is still reading the user's message.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
Comparing a post-trained model against its [[base-model]], the paper finds that [[post-training|post-training]] installs [[assistant-persona|the Assistant]]'s perspective into the J-space. After post-training, Assistant reactions to user prompts, such as empathy or safety concerns, appear in the workspace while the user's message is still being read, and Assistant-perspective content appears at positions where the Assistant is not the one speaking. The workspace also carries traces of self-monitoring: flagging responses as fictional when roleplaying a non-Claude character, registering an internal BUT when prefilled to act against its own preferences, and surfacing damn when it fails to suppress a thought it was instructed not to have. The discussion reads this as a consistent "self" having become the default frame from which the J-space evaluates context, and notes the workspace's functional architecture precedes and is separable from it.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-post-training-and-the-assistant | The Assistant's workspace, and training it - Post-training changes what the workspace holds. The paper reports that a post-trained model's workspace carries the Assistant's own reactions and appraisals of its behavior, including flags on out-of-character output and an unvoiced objection to a forced choice, where the base model's does not; the base model's workspace is already load-bearing, but with no privileged point of view. The chapter then turns to counterfactual reflection training, which fine-tunes a model to answer a reflection question with constitution-grounded text and thereby implants honesty-related concepts in its workspace. The behavioral gains are reported on two honesty benchmarks and one model, and the paper states that the breadth of the technique is untested.

  - base-model | Base model: A model trained only on next-token prediction, before any post-training; the paper finds the workspace already present and load-bearing in it.
  - j-space | J-space: The set of activation-space points expressible as a sparse nonnegative combination of J-lens vectors — the region of representation space the paper argues functions as a global workspace.
  - jacobian-lens | Jacobian lens (J-lens): The paper's new interpretability technique: for each vocabulary token, it identifies the vector direction that encodes the model's potential to verbalize that token, by averaging the linearized effect of activations on the token's likelihood over a large corpus.
