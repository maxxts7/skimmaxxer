NAME: Score-under-intervention bar chart

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
Bars giving a rate or score measured while an intervention is applied to the model, printed beside a control perturbation matched in size, layer and position, with per-item dots and error whiskers. It exists so the reader can judge whether an effect belongs to what was removed or merely to how much was removed.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
### Why this chart and not another

Every claim carried by this shape is causal and comparative. Something is done to a running model — directions projected out of the [[residual-stream]], attention heads zeroed, one lens coordinate exchanged for another — and a score is measured afterwards. The interventions are large: [[j-space-ablation]] zeroes the activation's projection onto the ten most active [[j-lens-vector]] directions at every position across dozens of layers, and a perturbation that size damages a model whatever it removes. A score reported alone settles nothing: it cannot separate damage from removing the [[j-space]] from damage of that magnitude anywhere.

The fix is to put the matched comparison in the same panel, on the same axis. [[matched-norm-controls]] rescale a different perturbation — a random direction, the non-J-space remainder, [[sparse-autoencoder]] decoder directions — so it carries the same norm at the same layers and positions. Other panels match differently: equally many random attention heads at the same depths, a smaller [[claude-model-family]] model as a yardstick for a tier's worth of damage, or the same ablation on an untrained model. A table would hold the same numbers a row apart; an average would fold the control into the mean. Adjacent bars make the distance between them the object being read.

The dots do a second job: a bar is a mean over a small, uneven item set, and printing the items separates a bar carried by two easy cases from one that moved everything.

### How to read it, step by step

The horizontal axis is categorical: conditions, grouped by task or model in the wider panels. The vertical axis is the experiment's score, and its scale says what kind of quantity that is. A 0–100% axis is a rate over trials — how often a swap target reaches top-1, how often an [[llm-grader]] scores a response positive — and a 0–10 axis is a judge's points. An axis whose interesting gridline is 1.0 has been normalised: each bar is divided by the unablated model's own score on that task, so 1.0 means the intervention cost nothing, and reading it as a perfect score is the error the normalisation invites.

Colour encodes condition, not magnitude: one shade for the unperturbed baseline, one for the intervention, one for the controls. Whiskers are [[wilson-interval]]s where the score is a proportion, standard errors where a bar averages case means. Dots are per-item rates, spread horizontally for legibility only.

Reading runs against the eye's habit: the control bar first, since it fixes what a null result looks like here, then the intervention bar's distance from it, then whether the whiskers overlap, then the dots.

### What a bad result would look like

Intervention and control level with each other, whiskers overlapping. That is the outcome in which the effect belongs to the perturbation's size rather than its target. In the fourteen-task battery, selectivity fails if the five shallow tasks fall alongside the seven generative ones, or if the random-direction control also knocks two-hop accuracy off its 98%. In the ethics-vector test, it fails if ablation drags the untrained model down too, leaving the recovery unspecific to what training implanted. It fails in the naming-versus-avoidance panels if both move together. A subtler failure: a bar well clear of its control but with dots split between 0 and 1 is a mean over items that individually did nothing or everything.

### Where it comes back

The control changes; the shape does not. fig-8 and fig-16 split a representation into its [[j-space-component]], found by [[gradient-pursuit]], and the remainder, then inject each at equal magnitude; the remainder, holding most of the variance, is the control. fig-24 sets three [[ablation-strengths]] against unablated Haiku across the [[ablation-task-battery]]. fig-26 places three matched-norm controls beside ablation on the [[experiential-language-score]]; fig-86 widens that set to ten across six panels. fig-34 controls broadcast-head ablation with five seeds of layer-matched random heads ([[broadcast-heads]]). fig-49 ablates the implanted ethics vectors in both the baseline model and the one given [[counterfactual-reflection-training]]; the baseline is the control. fig-69 plays two ablation depths against two instructions ([[inclusion-exclusion-paradigm]]). fig-10 and fig-65 use the no-instruction floor as the control for [[directed-modulation]]. fig-83 varies the tool an [[automated-auditing-agent]] is handed, not the intervention.

PREREQUISITES:
  - j-space | J-space: The set of activation-space points expressible as a sparse nonnegative combination of J-lens vectors — the region of representation space the paper argues functions as a global workspace.
  - jacobian-lens | Jacobian lens (J-lens): The paper's new interpretability technique: for each vocabulary token, it identifies the vector direction that encodes the model's potential to verbalize that token, by averaging the linearized effect of activations on the token's likelihood over a large corpus.
  - j-lens-vector | J-lens vector: One vector per vocabulary token per layer: the direction in residual-stream space that, on average across contexts, disposes the model to say that token.
  - residual-stream | Residual stream: The running vector at each token position that a transformer's layers read from and write to; the activations that all the paper's readouts and interventions act on.
  - j-space-ablation | J-space ablation: An intervention that suppresses the workspace wholesale: at each position, the residual stream's projections onto the top-k most active J-lens vectors are zeroed across a band of layers.
  - matched-norm-controls | Matched-norm control perturbations: Control interventions calibrated to perturb the activations by the same magnitude, at the same layers and positions, as J-space ablation, but along other directions.
  - wilson-interval | Wilson 95% interval: A confidence interval for a proportion, used for the paper's success-rate error bars.
  - llm-grader | LLM grader: A language model used to score responses against a written rubric, in place of human raters.
  - claude-model-family | Claude model family (Haiku, Sonnet, Opus): The production models the experiments run on; Haiku, Sonnet, and Opus are ascending size tiers, with version numbers like 4.5 and 4.6.
  - workspace-layers | Workspace layer range: The finding that the J-space plays a workspace-like role only in a middle range of layers: nearly empty for roughly the first third of depth, and giving way in the final layers to representations of the imminent output.
