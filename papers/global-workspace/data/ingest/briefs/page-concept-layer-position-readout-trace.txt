DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
### Why this chart and not another

The claims these figures carry are about order and location, not frequency. Figure 17 asserts that 21, 42 and 49 enter the [[j-space]] in the order the arithmetic requires; a bar chart of how often each appears would show all three present and say nothing about which arrived first. Figure 9 asserts that citrus content sits at the token "ook" inside "crookedly", where the surface text explains none of it; an average over positions would dissolve that. A table of top tokens per layer keeps depth but drops position, and position is what makes the content surprising.

The paper does run the aggregates: rates across [[instruction-conditions]], swap success over fifty two-hop prompts, layer-boundary statistics. The grid exists for what those cannot show, a serial trace through one pass. It also carries the lens comparison ([[lens-evaluation]]), legible only when three readout methods occupy the same rows.

### How to read it, step by step

One axis runs over token positions, in order, through prompt and response. The other runs over layers of the [[residual-stream]], reported under [[layer-percentage-indexing]]: 25 evenly spaced layers renumbered 0 to 100, so a label reads as a percentage of depth. A cell is one activation — one position, one layer — and prints the top-ranked token of the [[j-lens-readout]] there, $\text{softmax}(W_U\,\text{norm}(J_\ell h_\ell))$, which scores the entire vocabulary. Hovering opens the top ten.

A cell is not a prediction. The $J_\ell$ of the [[jacobian-lens]] is averaged over positions and a corpus of a thousand prompts, so a cell names what the activation disposes the model to say on average across contexts — a [[verbalizable-representation]] — not what it is about to emit. Where a panel is a heatmap or line chart instead of printed tokens, it encodes the rank of one selected token, rank 1 being the top of the readout, so lower is more prominent.

The depth bands come first. Through roughly the first third, the [[sensory-layers]], readouts are noisy and largely uninterpretable. Content is expected in the band from about layer 38 to 92, the [[workspace-layers]]. In the final few rows the readout flips to [[next-token-prediction]]: the [[motor-layers]]. That collapse is the workspace ending, not the lens working; taking it for success inverts the figure. The informative cells name a token that appears in neither prompt nor output.

### What a bad result would look like

For the arithmetic trace, the three values reaching rank 1 together, or in an order the computation does not require, would leave [[arithmetic-intermediate-ordering]] with nothing to report. So would readout depths that [[mean-difference-patching]] fails to confirm; the paper reports answer flips near layer 71 for 21, near 79 for 42, and only at the end for 49.

For the [[hold-in-mind-copy-task]], orange appearing at "ook" under no instruction would make [[directed-modulation]] vacuous. The systematic figure puts that baseline near zero, and the paper states plainly that the control is imperfect: "ignore X" still loads the target somewhat.

For the three-column comparison, a J-lens column as fragmentary as the [[logit-lens]] column below layer 58 would remove the case for the correction; the paper concedes the two look alike below the workspace onset.

The general failure is a grid whose every row prints the imminent output token, making the lens a delayed logit lens, or one whose cells never repeat, which is noise. The standing limitation is that one striking cell is an anecdote. All four are single forward passes on chosen prompts, and the aggregates have to hold them up.

### Where it comes back

Figure 5 is the full instrument ([[j-lens-visualization]]) on "Count to five and introspect deeply." given to Sonnet 4.5 ([[claude-model-family]]): the grid of top-1 tokens, a rank heatmap over pinned tokens, and per-position and per-layer columns with rank trajectories. Figure 9 drops to one boxed position per prompt and keeps only the layer axis, across three covert tasks. Figure 17 keeps both axes but replaces printed tokens with rank, adds a line chart at the final "=" token and sliders over the operands, on Opus 4.5. Figure 51 triples the columns — J-lens, logit lens, [[tuned-lens]] — over six prompts, where the variable is the instrument, not the model's content.

PREREQUISITES:
  - jacobian-lens | Jacobian lens (J-lens): The paper's new interpretability technique: for each vocabulary token, it identifies the vector direction that encodes the model's potential to verbalize that token, by averaging the linearized effect of activations on the token's likelihood over a large corpus.
  - j-lens-readout | J-lens readout: The basic read operation of the lens: $\text{lens}(h_\ell) = \text{softmax}(W_U\,\text{norm}(J_\ell h_\ell))$, giving a score for every vocabulary token.
  - j-lens-vector | J-lens vector: One vector per vocabulary token per layer: the direction in residual-stream space that, on average across contexts, disposes the model to say that token.
  - residual-stream | Residual stream: The running vector at each token position that a transformer's layers read from and write to; the activations that all the paper's readouts and interventions act on.
  - unembedding-matrix | Unembedding matrix: The final linear map from the residual stream to scores over the vocabulary, from which the model's next-token distribution is computed.
  - next-token-prediction | Next-token prediction: The pretraining objective: given a context, output a probability distribution over the vocabulary for the next token.
  - j-space | J-space: The set of activation-space points expressible as a sparse nonnegative combination of J-lens vectors — the region of representation space the paper argues functions as a global workspace.
  - verbalizable-representation | Verbalizable representations: Representations poised to be put into words should the occasion arise, as distinct from representations that merely happen to be verbalized in one particular context.
  - layer-percentage-indexing | Layer numbering as percentages: The paper's reporting convention: 25 evenly spaced layers of the residual stream, reindexed to 0–100 so that layer numbers read as percentages of depth.
  - layer-bands | Sensory, workspace, and motor layer bands: The paper's division of the model's depth into three functional regions: an early sensory band where the J-space is empty, a middle workspace band carrying persistent abstract content, and a late motor band aligned with the imminent output.
  - sensory-layers | Sensory layers: Roughly the first third of the model's layers, where J-lens readouts are noisy and the J-space carries essentially no meaningful content.
  - workspace-layers | Workspace layer range: The finding that the J-space plays a workspace-like role only in a middle range of layers: nearly empty for roughly the first third of depth, and giving way in the final layers to representations of the imminent output.
  - motor-layers | Motor layers: The paper's term for late-layer representations tied to the model's imminent output, as opposed to workspace representations of intermediate computations.
