NAME: Internal reasoning

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
The property that workspace vectors carry the values of intermediate computations when the model chains inferential steps, and that intervening on them redirects the conclusion.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
In prompts like "The number of legs on the animal that spins webs is", the model must infer spider before answering 8; the J-lens at intermediate layers shows spider even though the word appears nowhere in prompt or output, and swapping the spider lens vector for ant changes the answer from 8 to 6. On a systematic set of 50 two-hop factual prompts, lens-coordinate swaps of the intermediate redirect the answer on 54-70% of trials depending on model. A control shows the intermediate swap takes effect a median of roughly 17% earlier in depth than swapping the answer itself, ruling out the possibility that the intermediate's lens vector merely smuggles in the answer. A probe decomposition further shows that although most of the variance of the model's working representation of an intermediate lies outside the J-space, the J-space component carries the causal mediation. Case studies extend the picture to planning (rhymes), cross-lingual intermediates, reward-driven choices, and multi-step arithmetic whose intermediates surface in computation order.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-five-workspace-properties | Five things the workspace does - This chapter takes the five properties of the paper's definition in turn. The model names concepts its workspace holds, loads the workspace on instruction, and carries the intermediate values of chained reasoning in it; one workspace vector also serves as an argument to many different downstream computations. Only a fraction of the model's behavior depends on the workspace at all, which the paper shows by suppressing it and recording which tasks survive and which collapse. It follows the instrument because each of these tests is a lens reading followed by an intervention on the same directions.

PREREQUISITES:
  - j-space | J-space: The set of activation-space points expressible as a sparse nonnegative combination of J-lens vectors — the region of representation space the paper argues functions as a global workspace.
  - j-space-component-decomposition | J-space component decomposition: Splitting a concept's representation into a small J-space component and a large non-J-space remainder, to test which part carries causal effects.
  - jacobian-lens | Jacobian lens (J-lens): The paper's new interpretability technique: for each vocabulary token, it identifies the vector direction that encodes the model's potential to verbalize that token, by averaging the linearized effect of activations on the token's likelihood over a large corpus.
  - lens-coordinate-swap | Lens-coordinate swap: An intervention that exchanges one concept for another: it reads an activation's coordinates along two J-lens vectors, permutes them, and writes the result back, leaving everything orthogonal to those vectors unchanged.
  - workspace-like-properties | The five workspace-like properties: The paper's operational definition: a set of vector representations counts as workspace-like if it supports verbal report, directed modulation, internal reasoning, flexible generalization, and selectivity.

Across three worked examples — a two-hop fact, a planned rhyme, and a cross-language antonym — the output tracks the substituted intermediate, which the paper takes as evidence that these lens vectors are causally load-bearing for internal reasoning, not just readable.
  - fig-61 | Template lens versus J-lens on multi-token words: Three side-by-side comparisons show that the template lens names whole multi-token words — blackmail, photosynthesis, Tchaikovsky — where the J-lens returns only word-initial fragments, and that swapping one template for another at the workspace layers flips the model's answer in a way the corresponding fragment vectors cannot. The panels are hand-picked examples; the paper treats the template lens as a proof of concept rather than a finished method.
  - fig-63 | Template lens versus J-lens on single-token intermediates: Re-running the appendix's readout and swap evaluations on the paper's original 50-item multi-hop benchmark, where every unspoken intermediate is a single token, leaves the two lenses at rough parity: the template lens reads out slightly more intermediates (84% against 76%) and redirects slightly fewer answers (52% against 68%). Both gaps sit inside overlapping 95% Wilson intervals, so the figure supports only the claim that the template lens buys nothing here, not that either lens is better.
