NAME: Weights-only population comparison

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
A curve per population of candidate directions, plotted against source layer, showing how strongly the model's weights amplify, connect to or compose with each population — computed from weights alone and normalized so a random direction scores 1, or read against an explicit chance line. It is how the paper argues the J-space is wired for broadcast, and the argument lives in the gap between populations rather than in any curve's height.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
### Why this chart and not another

The claim under test is structural: the model's weights are built to spread [[j-space]] content across depth and across token positions, rather than that spreading being an accident of particular inputs ([[j-space-broadcast]]). Every number comes from weight matrices alone, so no dataset or sampling choice can be blamed — and equally, nothing here shows what the model does on any input.

A single average over candidate directions would delete the finding, a difference between populations at the same depth. A bar chart or a table would fix depth away, and depth is the variable: the paper claims the effect switches on at the workspace onset and fades near the output. Absolute values would be unreadable too: the raw output norm of an [[mlp-block]], or a raw [[cosine-similarity]], has no scale a reader can judge. So each statistic is divided by its value on isotropic random directions, or scored against a chance rate.

### How to read it, step by step

The horizontal axis is the source layer where the candidate direction lives, under the paper's convention of 25 evenly spaced residual layers renumbered 0-100 ([[layer-percentage-indexing]]). The shaded band marks the [[workspace-layers]], roughly L38 to L92, an overlay from section 4.1, not a result here.

The vertical axis changes between figures; the normalization does not. [[mlp-gain]], OV-circuit gain, the [[tail-energy]] ratio and the [[composition-score]] are each divided by their value on isotropic random directions, so a random direction scores exactly 1. Where the statistic is a share of neurons, the null is a chance rate instead: $1/6$ among six equal strata, $50\%$ between two equal pools.

One point is a median over a population of unit directions: 2,000 per population in the gain figures, 500 in the tail-energy figure. Colour encodes population, not condition — [[j-lens-vector]]s from the [[jacobian-lens]]; [[sparse-autoencoder]] decoder directions cut into strata by [[lens-kurtosis]] $\kappa$; MLP neuron directions as control; and, in Figure 33, a randomly rotated copy of the J-lens vectors.

Under [[superposition]] the [[residual-stream]] holds more feature directions than dimensions, so any weight vector has appreciable cosine with directions it has no relationship to. The breadth figures answer that by keeping only each neuron's strongest match, the strength figure by thresholding at the 99.9th percentile of chance cosines.

The gap to read first is the vertical distance between the J-aligned curves and the controls inside the band. Height alone says little, and is not an effect on behaviour: a gain of $10\times$ is a wiring statistic, and the paper calls MLP gain a first-pass approximation that ignores whatever else shares the stream. The causal link runs through Figure 34's broadcast-head ablation.

### What a bad result would look like

Every curve flat at 1, or flat at the chance line. Or a J-lens curve well above 1 with the MLP-neuron control just as high, meaning the statistic rewards any real model direction rather than a J-space one. Or SAE strata elevated but unordered in $\kappa$, breaking the dose-response the paper leans on. Or elevation spread evenly across depth, leaving the shaded band to do no work. The rotated J-lens vectors in Figure 33 are the sharpest control: had they scored as well as the originals, the effect would belong to the population's geometry, not its alignment with the weights.

### Where it comes back

Figure 32 is the base case: block-level gain across depth, J-lens vectors against neuron outputs on the left, six $\kappa$ strata on the right. Figure 33 keeps the populations, drops the layer axis, and plots each population's [[broadcast-heads]] as a marker with an interquartile bar on two [[ov-circuit]] metrics — gain and [[label-preservation]] — for [[attention-head]]s rather than MLPs. Figures 76 and 78 move from the block to the individual neuron ([[neuron-connectivity]]): 76 counts where each neuron's top match lands among six strata against the $1/6$ null, 78 measures how strongly directions overlap neuron weights, and both split read side from write side, where the early-late asymmetry appears. Figure 77 repeats 76 without the sparse-autoencoder dictionary, two equal pools against a $50\%$ line. Figure 79 returns to attention in aggregate, three panels by weight family, the claim living in the difference between panels.

PREREQUISITES:
  - jacobian-lens | Jacobian lens (J-lens): The paper's new interpretability technique: for each vocabulary token, it identifies the vector direction that encodes the model's potential to verbalize that token, by averaging the linearized effect of activations on the token's likelihood over a large corpus.
  - j-lens-vector | J-lens vector: One vector per vocabulary token per layer: the direction in residual-stream space that, on average across contexts, disposes the model to say that token.
  - j-space | J-space: The set of activation-space points expressible as a sparse nonnegative combination of J-lens vectors — the region of representation space the paper argues functions as a global workspace.
  - j-space-broadcast | Broadcast privilege of J-lens vectors: The structural claim that the model's weights are configured to distribute J-space content disproportionately widely — via MLPs across depth and attention heads across token positions.
  - workspace-layers | Workspace layer range: The finding that the J-space plays a workspace-like role only in a middle range of layers: nearly empty for roughly the first third of depth, and giving way in the final layers to representations of the imminent output.
  - lens-kurtosis | Lens kurtosis (κ): A statistic operationalizing whether a direction lies in the J-space: project it through the J-lens and measure how sharply peaked the resulting vocabulary distribution is.
  - sparse-autoencoder | Sparse autoencoder (SAE): An unsupervised dictionary-learning method that decomposes activations into sparsely active linear features, at the cost of expensive training and a per-feature interpretation step.
  - superposition | Superposition: The hypothesis that a model's activations decompose as sparse linear combinations drawn from an overcomplete set of feature directions — more features than dimensions.
  - residual-stream | Residual stream: The running vector at each token position that a transformer's layers read from and write to; the activations that all the paper's readouts and interventions act on.
  - mlp-block | MLP block: The feed-forward component in each transformer layer that performs nonlinear computation at a single token position.
  - attention-head | Attention head: The transformer operation that lets each position retrieve and transform information from any earlier position in the context.
  - layer-percentage-indexing | Layer numbering as percentages: The paper's reporting convention: 25 evenly spaced layers of the residual stream, reindexed to 0–100 so that layer numbers read as percentages of depth.
