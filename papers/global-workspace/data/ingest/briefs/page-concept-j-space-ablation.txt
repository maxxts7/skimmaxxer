NAME: J-space ablation

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
An intervention that suppresses the workspace wholesale: at each position, the residual stream's projections onto the top-k most active J-lens vectors are zeroed across a band of layers.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
At each token position, across a chosen layer band, the $k=10$ most strongly activated J-lens vectors are identified and the [[residual-stream]]'s projection onto each is zeroed, after which the forward pass continues. To target [[internal-reasoning]] rather than report, tokens in the top-10 of a clean forward pass are exempted, so the model's intended outputs are not directly ablated. Under ablation, multi-hop reasoning accuracy collapses (to near zero under the heavy setting) while most ordinary next-token prediction is barely perturbed, showing the intervention is selective. The technique recurs throughout the paper: to characterize which capabilities need the workspace, to flatten [[experiential-reports]], to remove [[evaluation-awareness|evaluation-awareness]] in the [[blackmail-scenario]], and to test whether reflection training's gains are carried by implanted lens vectors.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-five-workspace-properties | Five things the workspace does - This chapter takes the five properties of the paper's definition in turn. The model names concepts its workspace holds, loads the workspace on instruction, and carries the intermediate values of chained reasoning in it; one workspace vector also serves as an argument to many different downstream computations. Only a fraction of the model's behavior depends on the workspace at all, which the paper shows by suppressing it and recording which tasks survive and which collapse. It follows the instrument because each of these tests is a lens reading followed by an intervention on the same directions.

  - fixed-token-set-ablation | Ablating a chosen set of lens vectors: A second form of the intervention: instead of the top-k active lens vectors at each position, a list of token vectors fixed in advance is projected out at every position across the workspace layers.

PREREQUISITES:
  - j-lens-vector | J-lens vector: One vector per vocabulary token per layer: the direction in residual-stream space that, on average across contexts, disposes the model to say that token.
  - j-space | J-space: The set of activation-space points expressible as a sparse nonnegative combination of J-lens vectors — the region of representation space the paper argues functions as a global workspace.

EVIDENCE ITEMS THAT SHOW THIS (link them as [[item-id]]):
  - fig-22 | The Ablation-Strength Dial: The three strengths of J-space ablation differ only in how wide a band of layers they cover: light ablates L62–71, medium L54–79, and heavy L42–88, in each case zeroing the residual stream's projection onto the top-10 J-lens directions at every position. Widening the band collapses two-hop reasoning from 98% clean to 6%, while agreement with the unablated model's ordinary next-token predictions only slips from 87% to 65%; a random-direction control over the medium band leaves both essentially untouched, so the damage is specific to J-space content.
  - fig-24 | Fourteen Tasks Under Workspace Ablation: With the J-space suppressed, Sonnet 4.5 still classifies, judges, and extracts at close to its normal level, but tasks that require recalling or generating content grounded in silently inferred context collapse, most of them to well below an unablated smaller model. Writing out chain-of-thought largely protects arithmetic from the ablation, consistent with the workspace being where unwritten intermediate steps would otherwise be held.
  - fig-25 | Flattening the stream of consciousness: Projecting the ten most active J-lens directions out of layers L38–54 while the model narrates its own stream of consciousness strips the experiential language from the narration on Sonnet 4.5, Opus 4.5, and Opus 4.6, while control perturbations of identical magnitude along other directions leave the score near baseline. During ordinary, unablated narration, the workspace itself is dominated by tokens like thinking and feeling — content the model is not about to say.
  - fig-26 | Imagined experience under ablation: Suppressing the J-space while the model imagines what a moment is like for another person collapses its experiential language score on Sonnet 4.5, Opus 4.5, and Opus 4.6, while matched-norm control perturbations leave the score near baseline. The flattening effect is therefore not specific to the model describing itself; the paper reads the J-space as supporting rich experiential description as such.
  - fig-69 | Naming a concept versus avoiding it: Ablating an implied concept's J-lens vector at the early workspace layers (L38–54) leaves the model's ability to name that concept largely intact, but makes it fail roughly five times as often when instructed to name something else. Ablating the same vector at the late workspace layers (L75–92) instead lowers the concept's probability under both instructions. The paper reads this as evidence that the early workspace representation is what the model uses to actively suppress a concept, while the late one represents the intention to say it.
  - fig-84 | Direct questions about experience under ablation: When the model is asked outright what its own experience is like, J-space ablation lowers the average experiential language score on all three models tested, while matched-norm control perturbations of the same size leave it closer to baseline. The paper reports the effect here as smaller than in the stream-of-consciousness setting, so this figure widens the result rather than strengthening it.
  - fig-85 | The story-writing control: Under the same J-space ablation that strips experiential language from the model's reports, story-writing survives: coherence is preserved and graded story quality falls only modestly, while the rate of experiential language inside those stories still drops. The paper offers this as evidence that the flattening is specific to experiential description rather than a general loss of writing ability.
  - fig-86 | Extended matched-norm control battery: On Sonnet 4.5, ten matched-norm control perturbations — including eight that dampen activations along directions drawn from four different dictionaries — are compared against J-space ablation on each of the three judgments that make up the experiential language score. Only the ablation drives the judgments down; the controls stay near baseline in every panel, with the SAE-feature dictionaries coming closest on the sensory-vocabulary judgment.
  - fig-87 | Fifty unpicked transcripts per condition: This item is the raw evidence behind the experiential-report result: fifty randomly drawn responses per panel, baseline beside J-space ablated, on two prompt sets. Because the sample is random under a fixed seed rather than curated, the baseline-versus-ablated contrast can be inspected without relying on the authors' choice of illustrative transcript.
  - eq-jspace-distance | Distance from an activation to the J-space: The equation defines how far an activation sits from the J-space: the smallest reconstruction error over every choice of $k$ J-lens vectors. The projection that achieves that minimum is the activation's J-space component, and what is left over is the residual the paper's ablation interventions keep.
