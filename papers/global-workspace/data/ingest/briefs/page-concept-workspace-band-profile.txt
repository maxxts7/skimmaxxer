NAME: Workspace-band profile

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
A statistic of the J-space's contents plotted against layer depth — or, in the word-list case, against position in a running list — with percentile bands and a random or shuffled control that fixes the zero level. Its job is to locate the two depths where the curve changes slope, the start and end of the workspace band, rather than to report any absolute value.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
### Why this chart and not another

Every figure of this shape asks where along the model's depth something changes, not how large it gets. [[layer-bands]] splits depth into a sensory, a workspace and a motor region, and the split is claimed at particular depths — roughly $L38$ and $L92$. A claim about a location can only be read off a curve. A table of per-layer values would carry the same numbers and hide the elbows: a break in slope is something the eye finds and a column of figures does not. An average over layers would be worse. A statistic near zero across the [[sensory-layers]] and elevated across the [[workspace-layers]] averages to a middling value that describes no layer and erases the band being argued for.

Two further features follow. Percentile bands appear because the statistic varies across positions and contexts, and a lone mean line would present a plateau as a constant. A control curve appears because none of these statistics is zero by construction: random directions reconstruct activations to some degree, arbitrary readouts have some kurtosis, unrelated words land in a top-25 list by chance. The control defines the zero level; without it the vertical axis has no origin.

### How to read it, step by step

The horizontal axis is depth, under [[layer-percentage-indexing]]: 25 evenly spaced layers of the [[residual-stream]], reindexed 0–100, so a layer number reads as a percentage of depth, comparable across models. The paper notes that this subsampling exaggerates how sharp the transitions look. In the word-list case the axis is instead position in a running list, one tick per comma.

The vertical axis is one statistic of what the [[j-space]] holds there, computed from the [[jacobian-lens]]. Units differ from figure to figure — a fraction of positions, an excess kurtosis, a $\Delta$ log probability, a count of vectors — so heights are not comparable across panels. One point is that statistic pooled over many positions, not a single measurement.

Colour carries the comparison, not the quantity: grey is almost always the control, solid against dashed separates what the model has read from the full list, and shading is a percentile spread over positions or trials rather than a confidence interval on a mean.

The first thing to find is not the peak but the two places where the slope breaks, and whether the curve has left its control at all. Two misreadings recur. The control is the definition of no signal, not a baseline to beat. And [[j-space-occupancy]] of 25 counts active lens vectors, not 25 concepts: the [[word-list-experiment]] shows several related vectors carrying one category ([[category-loading]]), which is why [[j-space-capacity]] is stated at the order of tens.

### What a bad result would look like

A flat line from 0 to 100 would say there is no band at all. A smooth monotone ramp with no elbows would be almost as damaging: that is what a lens reading nothing but a gradually sharpening next-token prediction would produce, and the middle band would be an early view of the output rather than a workspace. Four panels whose rises fell at four different depths would locate nothing. A curve that never left its control would mean lens vectors reconstruct activations no better than random directions. In the list figures, an unrelated-word count climbing with list length would deny any capacity limit, and a related-word count matching it would deny category loading.

### Where it comes back

Figure 28 is the base case: four [[workspace-boundary-metrics]] in four panels, agreeing on two elbows. Figure 30 keeps the depth axis, changes the statistic to occupancy with percentiles over positions and a random-direction control, and adds a panel of [[excess-variance-explained]] at five workspace layers. Figure 31 swaps depth for list position, shades the interquartile range over trials, greys the control words, and adds [[category-displacement]]; its counts pool the whole band, and [[single-layer-capacity]] is smaller. Figure 75 changes the population to a million [[sparse-autoencoder]] features scored by [[lens-kurtosis]], against a covariance-matched baseline and with motor features excluded. Figure 88 replaces the readout statistic with a causal one — a logit difference under [[mean-difference-patching]] swept over the patching layer — so the shape carries the check on [[mechanistic-localization]] rather than the claim.

  - jacobian-lens | Jacobian lens (J-lens): The paper's new interpretability technique: for each vocabulary token, it identifies the vector direction that encodes the model's potential to verbalize that token, by averaging the linearized effect of activations on the token's likelihood over a large corpus.
  - j-space | J-space: The set of activation-space points expressible as a sparse nonnegative combination of J-lens vectors — the region of representation space the paper argues functions as a global workspace.
  - residual-stream | Residual stream: The running vector at each token position that a transformer's layers read from and write to; the activations that all the paper's readouts and interventions act on.
  - layer-percentage-indexing | Layer numbering as percentages: The paper's reporting convention: 25 evenly spaced layers of the residual stream, reindexed to 0–100 so that layer numbers read as percentages of depth.
  - layer-bands | Sensory, workspace, and motor layer bands: The paper's division of the model's depth into three functional regions: an early sensory band where the J-space is empty, a middle workspace band carrying persistent abstract content, and a late motor band aligned with the imminent output.
  - kurtosis | Kurtosis: A statistic measuring how heavy-tailed or sharply peaked a distribution is; excess kurtosis is measured relative to a Gaussian.
