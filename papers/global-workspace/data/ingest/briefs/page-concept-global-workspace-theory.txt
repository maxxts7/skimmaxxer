NAME: Global workspace theory

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
A neuroscience theory holding that information becomes consciously accessible when it is posted to a shared, capacity-limited workspace that broadcasts it to many specialized brain processes.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
Global workspace theory, due to Baars and developed by Dehaene and colleagues, grounds the functional properties of conscious access in an architectural picture of the brain. Many [[specialized-processors]] operate in parallel and in isolation, outside conscious access. A representation becomes consciously accessible when it enters a shared global workspace from which many downstream processes can read; the workspace integrates and broadcasts information, making it usable for flexible reasoning and report. The workspace is limited in capacity, so entry is competitive and modulated by attention, and its contents at any moment are a small selection of the brain's activity. The paper notes the theory is not universally accepted, and uses it as a comparison point rather than an established fact.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-the-workspace-claim | The workspace claim - The paper starts from a neuroscience theory: information becomes consciously accessible when it is posted to a shared, capacity-limited workspace and broadcast to many processes at once. It then makes its central claim, that a language model maintains a comparable privileged subset of vector representations. This chapter sets out the operational definition the rest of the paper is scored against — five functional properties and three structural signatures — and the paper's insistence that nothing in the transformer supplies such a workspace by design. It comes first because every later result is measured against this definition.

SUB-CONCEPTS (these render as expandable sections BELOW your page - reference them, do not duplicate them):
  - global-broadcast | Global broadcast: The workspace's defining act: making a representation available to many downstream processes at once, in a common format.
    - cortical-recurrent-broadcast | Recurrent cortical broadcast: In the brain, broadcast is held to be carried by recurrent loops and long-range cortical connections. This is the part of global workspace theory with no direct counterpart in a transformer's forward pass.
  - ignition | Ignition: In global workspace theory, the late, all-or-none amplification of one interpretation of an ambiguous input; the paper finds an analogous sharp commitment emerging at the workspace onset layer.
    - bimodal-commitment | Bimodal responses to maximal ambiguity: At the most ambiguous mixture value, per-trial responses in middle and late layers cluster near 0 or 1 rather than 0.5 — the model commits to one concept per trial.
    - embedding-mixture-experiment | Ambiguous-input embedding mixtures: The experimental construction: a concept token's input embedding is replaced by the weighted mixture $(1-\alpha)\,e_B + \alpha\,e_A$ inside ordinary sentences, sweeping $\alpha$ from 0 to 1.
      - projection-share | Projection share: Where an activation sits along the line connecting the same trial's pure-$B$ and pure-$A$ activations at the same position and layer — 0 at one end of the sweep, 1 at the other.
    - transition-width | Transition width: The change in $\alpha$ needed for the projection share to go from 10% to 90% — the quantified sharpness of the commitment to one interpretation.
  - specialized-processors | Specialized processors: In global workspace theory, the many parallel, largely isolated brain systems whose activity proceeds outside conscious access.
    - separable-input-processors | Separable input processors: The brain-side picture has distinct input systems whose outputs compete for the workspace. The paper notes that a transformer has no obviously separable processors of this kind.
  - workspace-capacity-limit | Workspace capacity limit: The claim that the workspace holds only a small selection of the brain's ongoing activity at any moment, making entry competitive.
  - functional-vs-architectural-analogy | Functional versus architectural analogy: The paper's stated limit on its own claim: the J-space matches many of the functional properties global workspace theory assigns to the workspace, while sharing only some of its architectural ones.
  - convergent-workspace-emergence | Convergence on a workspace: The paper's reading of its own result: nothing in the transformer imposes a workspace, so finding one suggests that learning systems converge on this organization under the right computational pressures.

PREREQUISITES:
  - access-consciousness | Access consciousness: The functional notion that, of everything the brain processes, only a subset is consciously accessible: poised for use in reasoning and in the direct control of action and speech.
