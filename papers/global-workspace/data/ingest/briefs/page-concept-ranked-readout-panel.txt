NAME: Ranked lens-readout panel

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
A column of the top few vocabulary tokens read off one object — an activation at a marked position, or a static weight direction — repeated under two or three conditions so the contents can be compared. It is how the paper displays what the workspace is carrying, and it reads by the identity and printed rank of the tokens, never by the length of the bars across columns.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
### Why this chart and not another

The [[jacobian-lens]] returns a score for every entry of the [[tokenizer-vocabulary]] at one layer and one position. Collapsing that to a single number is possible, and the paper does it elsewhere. The ranked panel exists because these claims are about *which* concepts are present, not how much of one named concept is there. A bar chart of one token's score across conditions would already assume which token mattered; the ranked list shows what else was in contention and what got displaced. A plain average over prompts destroys the object, since the contents differ trial by trial. A table would carry the same names but not the gaps between them.

A second reason applies to the weight-side cases. A [[sparse-autoencoder]] feature direction, a [[transcoder]] encoder, an [[attention-head]] matrix: each is one vector, read without running the model on any data. There is nothing to average and no distribution to plot, so a ranking is the whole result ([[weight-vocabulary-projection]], the use the paper calls [[component-interpretation]]).

Third, a readout alone proves nothing. Each panel sets it beside a second measurement taken at the same place: next-token logits, a property probe, activating excerpts, an attention pattern. The repeated-column layout is what lets an agreement or a dissociation be seen.

### How to read it, step by step

One column is one readout of one object under one condition. Rows are the top-$k$ tokens by lens score, best at the top. The printed number is a natural-log probability under that column's own softmax (figures 6, 67) or the token's rank in the full vocabulary ordering (figures 91, 94). Bar length is the token's probability relative to the top token of its own column, so the first row is always full width. Comparing bar lengths across columns therefore says nothing; the printed number is the comparable quantity. Where a target misses the visible top five, its full-vocabulary rank is printed separately — figure 67 prints the rank of *python* under every condition for that reason.

Colour is not a scale. In figure 6 orange marks the lens and grey the model's [[next-token-prediction]] logits; in figure 91 colour marks a hand-assigned category of token.

What to read first is the top two or three tokens in each column, then the target's rank as it moves column to column. Layers are percentages of depth ([[layer-percentage-indexing]]), and readouts are taken inside the band the paper calls the workspace range ([[workspace-layers]]).

The standing misreading is to treat the top token as the object's meaning. A [[j-lens-vector]] is the [[residual-stream]] direction that disposes the model to say a token, so a [[j-lens-readout]] names what the object makes the model disposed to say — the paper's sense of a [[verbalizable-representation]], not a definition.

### What a bad result would look like

In figure 6, the lens and logit columns would share no tokens, and after the [[lens-coordinate-swap]] *Rugby* would lead the lens while the model still answered *Soccer*: contents visible but not load-bearing. In figure 7, the injected concept would top the readout at the control positions too, making the [[j-lens-steering]] a blanket push to say a word rather than something reportable on demand. In figure 67, the imagine column and the real-Python column would move the [[j-orthogonalized-property-probe]] together, meaning the instruction re-encoded the stimulus rather than writing to the [[j-space]], and [[directed-modulation]] would lose its name. In figure 74 the three rows would look alike, and [[lens-kurtosis]] would sort nothing. In figures 91 and 94 the ranked tokens would be high-frequency filler, or a feature's two sides would name unrelated things.

### Where it comes back

Figures 6 and 7 read activations during verbal report ([[verbal-report]]), against logits and against position controls. Figure 67 reads an activation at a boxed token under three instruction conditions, a probe gauge beneath each column. Figure 74 reads nine SAE decoder directions and sorts them into [[workspace-features]], [[motor-features]] and [[bookkeeping-features]]. Figure 91 reads a transcoder's encoder and decoder separately, so the two columns together name a function. Figure 94 reads all four weight matrices of three heads, including the [[ov-circuit]] pair. What changes is whether the object is an activation or a weight, how many conditions sit side by side, and what the readout is paired with. What holds throughout is that ranks compare and bars do not.

PREREQUISITES:
  - jacobian-lens | Jacobian lens (J-lens): The paper's new interpretability technique: for each vocabulary token, it identifies the vector direction that encodes the model's potential to verbalize that token, by averaging the linearized effect of activations on the token's likelihood over a large corpus.
  - j-lens-vector | J-lens vector: One vector per vocabulary token per layer: the direction in residual-stream space that, on average across contexts, disposes the model to say that token.
  - j-lens-readout | J-lens readout: The basic read operation of the lens: $\text{lens}(h_\ell) = \text{softmax}(W_U\,\text{norm}(J_\ell h_\ell))$, giving a score for every vocabulary token.
  - j-space | J-space: The set of activation-space points expressible as a sparse nonnegative combination of J-lens vectors — the region of representation space the paper argues functions as a global workspace.
  - residual-stream | Residual stream: The running vector at each token position that a transformer's layers read from and write to; the activations that all the paper's readouts and interventions act on.
  - tokenizer-vocabulary | Tokenizer vocabulary: The fixed set of tokens, usually words and word pieces, into which a model's tokenizer splits text; the unit the J-lens is built over.
  - layer-percentage-indexing | Layer numbering as percentages: The paper's reporting convention: 25 evenly spaced layers of the residual stream, reindexed to 0–100 so that layer numbers read as percentages of depth.
  - workspace-layers | Workspace layer range: The finding that the J-space plays a workspace-like role only in a middle range of layers: nearly empty for roughly the first third of depth, and giving way in the final layers to representations of the imminent output.
  - verbalizable-representation | Verbalizable representations: Representations poised to be put into words should the occasion arise, as distinct from representations that merely happen to be verbalized in one particular context.
  - weight-vocabulary-projection | Vocabulary projection of weights: Applying lens-style vocabulary decoding to static weight matrices and learned directions rather than to activations.
