NAME: Building the lens, and reading and writing with it

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
The instrument is one matrix per layer: the Jacobian from that layer to the final layer, averaged over positions and over a corpus of pretraining-like sequences, which is what eq-jlens-matrix defines. Equation eq-lens-readout turns that matrix into a read operation, passing its output through the model's own unembedding matrix so a readout is a score for every vocabulary token. The same vectors support writing as well as reading: steering adds one in, ablation projects one out, and the lens-coordinate swap of eq-lens-swap exchanges two, solving for the coefficients with a pseudoinverse because the J-lens vectors are neither orthogonal nor unit-norm. Figure 4 draws the whole instrument in a single schematic, from how the Jacobian is averaged to how a swap is applied. The lens is then used as a general interpretability tool, locating where a computation happens by the layer at which a concept enters the readout, with mean-difference patching supplied as the causal check and attribution graphs rebuilt on lens vectors instead of trained features.

  e001 [load-bearing] j-lens-readout (J-lens readout) --depends-on--> unembedding-matrix (Unembedding matrix) | readout ends in the unembedding: The lens readout replaces every layer above $\ell$ with the single matrix $J_\ell$ and then runs the model's own normalization and unembedding. Without the unembedding matrix there is no map from the residual stream to vocabulary scores, and the readout has nothing to rank. The lens therefore reports in the model's own output vocabulary rather than in a learned code of its own.
  e002 [supporting] averaged-jacobian (Corpus-averaged Jacobian) --depends-on--> local-linearization (Local linearization) | one matrix stands for many layers: A Jacobian describes only the effect of a small perturbation, so collapsing all the layers above $\ell$ into a single matrix treats the network as locally linear. The paper takes that approximation as its working footing. Any reading of a J-lens score inherits the approximation.
  e006 [load-bearing] lens-coordinate-swap (Lens-coordinate swap) --depends-on--> pseudoinverse (Pseudoinverse) | oblique frame forces a pseudoinverse: J-lens vectors are neither orthogonal nor unit-norm, so plain inner products would not give an activation's coordinates along a chosen pair of them. The swap forms the matrix $V = [v_s\; v_t]$ and reads coordinates as $c = V^\dagger h$ using the pseudoinverse. This is what lets the component orthogonal to the two vectors stay untouched.
  e007 [supporting] lens-corpus (Lens corpus and convergence) --depends-on--> averaged-jacobian (Corpus-averaged Jacobian) | averaging needs a prompt corpus: A Jacobian from one prompt mixes the model's general disposition to verbalize a concept with the particular use that concept is being put to in that context. The paper separates the two by averaging over positions and over a corpus of a thousand pretraining-like prompts. The corpus is the operation that turns a per-input derivative into a fixed per-layer matrix.
  e025 [load-bearing] mechanistic-localization (Mechanistic localization) --depends-on--> mean-difference-patching (Mean-difference patching) | layer claim validated by patching: Reading the layer at which a concept enters the lens gives a candidate location for a computation, but that is a correlational observation. The localization claim depends on the patch, not on the readout alone.
