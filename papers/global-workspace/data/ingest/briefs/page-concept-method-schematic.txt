DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
### Why this chart and not another

Three of these four figures report nothing; they fix what a later measurement will mean: how the [[jacobian-lens]] is built, read and written (Figure 4); which layers count as the workspace (Figure 27); what the three structural claims about the [[j-space]] amount to before section 4 tests them (Figure 2). A bar chart, an average or a table each need an axis with units and a population to aggregate over, and a definition has neither; a table of the same recipe would keep its parts and lose their sequence. Only a drawing of the order of operations — backpropagate, average, unembed — shows where the averaging step sits, and that step is what separates a general disposition to verbalize from use in one context.

Figure 22 is the mixed case, and the one most easily misread: a four-row widget defining three doses of [[j-space-ablation]] plus a control, carrying accuracy numbers. Those numbers are not the section's finding but the side effects that calibrate each dose: a positive control that the intervention removes what earlier sections attributed to the J-space, and a measure of its cost to ordinary prediction.

### How to read it, step by step

None of the four has quantitative axes. The grid under Figures 2 and 4 is the paper's usual one: [[residual-stream]] layers bottom to top, token positions left to right. The vertical scale is not layer indices: the paper samples 25 evenly spaced layers and reindexes them 0 to 100, so L38 means about 38 percent of the model's depth ([[layer-percentage-indexing]]).

Figure 4 is read panel by panel. Panel A builds: red arrows mark one activation's influence on the final layer at the present position and every later one — the [[jacobian-matrix]] $\partial h_{\text{final},t'}/\partial h_{\ell,t}$ — and the stacked copies labelled aggregate are the average over positions and one thousand prompts giving one matrix $J_\ell$ per layer ([[averaged-jacobian]]). Panel B reads: $J_\ell$ replaces every layer above $\ell$, the [[unembedding-matrix]] follows, and the orange box is the ranked [[j-lens-readout]]. Panel C writes: its two axes are deliberately not perpendicular, because [[j-lens-vector]]s are not orthogonal, which is why a [[pseudoinverse]] reads the coordinates before they are permuted ([[lens-coordinate-swap]]).

Colour holds across the set: red for the gradient taken, blue for the lens object, orange for output or intervention result; Figure 22's orange deepens as the dose widens, and its control row is grey. Band widths, stripe positions and the spacing of drawn layers carry no information; only labelled numbers do.

### What a bad result would look like

A schematic cannot fail on its own terms; its failure belongs to the figures it points at. Panel A of Figure 4 would be decoration if the averaging made no difference — if a single-prompt Jacobian, or the plain [[logit-lens]], gave the same readouts; the appendix's recipe variants test exactly that. The bands of Figures 27 and 2 fail if their boundaries do not converge: a [[cka]] similarity matrix with no block structure, or four [[workspace-boundary-metrics]] placing the transition in four places. The paper concedes part of this — transitions are gradual in some models, sampling 25 layers exaggerates their sharpness, and every boundary metric derives from the lens itself, so a workspace the lens cannot resolve remains possible.

Figure 22 would fail if its two columns moved together, or if the control moved at all. Accuracy on the [[multihop-benchmark]] falls from 98 percent clean to 68, 26 and 6 across the three doses, while [[pretraining-top-1-match]] slips only from 87 to 75 to 65 percent; the random control leaves both at 98 and 96.

### Where it comes back

The four differ in distance from evidence. Figure 4 is pure method, and its panel C machinery sits behind every intervention figure. Figure 2 is a promise made in the introduction: [[layer-bands]], [[j-space-capacity]] and [[j-space-broadcast]] as a picture, matching the three properties [[global-workspace-theory]] asks for. Figure 27 returns to the first of them in section 4.1, naming the regions used from then on — [[sensory-layers]], [[workspace-layers]], [[motor-layers]] — after the measurement, not before. Figure 22 turns that band into a dial: L62–71, then L54–79, then L42–88, nested inside the roughly L38–L92 workspace range.

PREREQUISITES:
  - residual-stream | Residual stream: The running vector at each token position that a transformer's layers read from and write to; the activations that all the paper's readouts and interventions act on.
  - jacobian-matrix | Jacobian matrix: The matrix of partial derivatives of one vector-valued quantity with respect to another; the linear map that best approximates the effect of a small perturbation.
  - unembedding-matrix | Unembedding matrix: The final linear map from the residual stream to scores over the vocabulary, from which the model's next-token distribution is computed.
  - jacobian-lens | Jacobian lens (J-lens): The paper's new interpretability technique: for each vocabulary token, it identifies the vector direction that encodes the model's potential to verbalize that token, by averaging the linearized effect of activations on the token's likelihood over a large corpus.
  - j-space | J-space: The set of activation-space points expressible as a sparse nonnegative combination of J-lens vectors — the region of representation space the paper argues functions as a global workspace.
  - layer-percentage-indexing | Layer numbering as percentages: The paper's reporting convention: 25 evenly spaced layers of the residual stream, reindexed to 0–100 so that layer numbers read as percentages of depth.
  - global-workspace-theory | Global workspace theory: A neuroscience theory holding that information becomes consciously accessible when it is posted to a shared, capacity-limited workspace that broadcasts it to many specialized brain processes.
