NAME: How the lens is measured against the alternatives, and where it fails

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
The J-lens is set against the logit lens, which applies the unembedding directly and skips the intervening layers, and against the tuned lens, which trains an affine map per layer to match the model's output distribution. On six benchmarks where a known intermediate concept must be computed, the J-lens surfaces that intermediate in its top-k readout more often than either (fig-52); on agreement with the model's actual next token it comes last of the three (fig-55), which the paper reads as the difference between recovering an intermediate and predicting an output. Sparse autoencoders and linear probes are placed on the same spectrum, a trained dictionary or one supervised concept set against a single matrix covering the whole vocabulary. The lens can only name concepts whose names are single tokens, and its accuracy falls as the target word runs longer (fig-62); the template lens and the oracle lens, the second built on a fine-tuned reconstructor model, exist to cover multi-token names, at added cost. An auditing agent given J-lens readouts scores close to the same agent given natural-language autoencoder explanations, and well below it on two of six transcripts (fig-83).

  e008 [load-bearing] multi-token-lens-extensions (Multi-token concept extensions) --depends-on--> single-token-limitation (Single-token limitation) | extensions exist to cover multi-token names: One J-lens vector is defined per vocabulary token, so the lens can only name concepts whose names are single tokens. The template lens and the oracle lens are built to answer that limit. The extensions are a design choice forced by how the lens was constructed.
  e009 [load-bearing] oracle-lens (Oracle lens) --depends-on--> template-lens (Template lens) | phrases decoded as template vectors: The oracle lens returns free-form phrases, but what it is really doing is proposing phrases whose template vectors, combined sparsely, reconstruct the activation. Its training loss is a cosine error in the whitened metric of the template lens, and every later stage works in that whitened space. The template construction is the currency the oracle trades in.
  e010 [load-bearing] oracle-training (Oracle training pipeline) --depends-on--> reconstructor-model (Reconstructor model) | every stage runs through the reconstructor: The reconstructor is trained first, and it maps a short phrase to the activation that would precede it. The 3.4-million-phrase dictionary is built by passing phrases through it, the teacher labels activations against that dictionary, and at inference each sampled phrase is mapped back to a direction by it. Holding the reconstructor frozen is also the paper's stated reason for expecting fewer confabulations than a natural-language autoencoder produces.
  e062 [load-bearing] lens-evaluation (Lens comparison evaluations) --supported-by--> fig-52 (Intermediate recovery across three lenses (fig-52)) | J-lens leads on six benchmarks: On normalized pass@k AUC the J-lens recovers the known intermediate better than the logit lens and the tuned lens on all six prompt distributions. The margin over the logit lens is modest on multihop and association and substantial on multilingual, order-of-operations, poetry and typo. Every intermediate in these benchmarks was chosen to be a single token, which is the condition the lens is built for.
  e063 [supporting] tuned-lens (Tuned lens) --supported-by--> fig-55 (Next-token agreement by lens and layer (fig-55)) | J-lens worst at predicting output: Measured against the model's own next-token distribution, the tuned lens is the closest match at every depth and the J-lens is the furthest, with higher KL than even the uncorrected logit lens through most of the network. The paper treats this as a property rather than a defect, on the argument that the directions best at anticipating the output are not the ones that expose the computation producing it.
  e064 [supporting] single-token-limitation (Single-token limitation) --supported-by--> fig-62 (Lens accuracy versus word length (fig-62)) | accuracy falls with word length: Over 126 multi-hop prompts and 112 swap pairs, of which 59 have multi-token intermediates, the J-lens decodes and swaps single-token concepts well and degrades sharply as the intermediate grows longer, while the template lens stays roughly flat. This bounds how much weight the readouts elsewhere in the paper can carry for concepts without single-token names.
  e065 [supporting] automated-auditing-agent (J-lens-equipped automated auditing agent) --supported-by--> fig-83 (Auditing Scores With a J-Lens Tool (fig-83)) | auditor scored against NLA baseline: An investigator agent given a J-lens tool is scored by an LLM judge on eighteen claims across six transcripts, and matches the NLA-equipped agent on four of the six cases while falling behind on reward hacking, 4.5 against 10.0, and language switching, 6.0 against 9.9. The ground truth was established from the NLA study's own findings, so the comparison is set on the other method's terms.
  e092 [supporting] fig-52 (Intermediate recovery across three lenses (fig-52)) --instance-of--> three-lens-comparison (Three-lens benchmark comparison) | the three-way comparison, on benchmarks: The paper repeats one fixed comparison of the Jacobian, logit and tuned lenses across benchmarks and metrics. Figure 52 is that comparison applied to intermediate recovery over all six prompt distributions.
  e099 [load-bearing] tuned-lens (Tuned lens) --contrasts-with--> jacobian-lens (Jacobian lens (J-lens)) | output match versus intermediate recovery: The tuned lens is trained to match the model's own next-token distribution, and it is the best match at every depth; the J-lens is the worst of the three, with higher KL than even the uncorrected logit lens through most of the network. The paper reads that loss as a feature, because on the same prompts the tuned lens skips ahead to the answer while the J-lens surfaces the unspoken intermediate. The two metrics disagree, and the paper chooses the causal one.
  e100 [supporting] logit-lens (Logit lens) --contrasts-with--> jacobian-lens (Jacobian lens (J-lens)) | identity shortcut versus averaged Jacobian: The logit lens is what the J-lens becomes when the per-layer matrix is set to the identity, which is a fair approximation late in the network and degrades earlier. The two agree closely in the final layers and diverge before them. The paper reports that the logit lens still captures much of the workspace structure at somewhat lower reliability, so the correction buys accuracy in early layers rather than a different picture.
  e101 [supporting] template-lens (Template lens) --contrasts-with--> jacobian-lens (Jacobian lens (J-lens)) | multi-token coverage bought with cost: The template lens names whole words such as blackmail, photosynthesis and Tchaikovsky where the J-lens prints only the fragments black, phot and tch, and its readout and swap performance stay roughly flat as the intermediate grows from one to four tokens. The coverage is paid for: a template needs a few hundred forward passes per word against one set of backward passes per layer, its vocabulary must be fixed in advance, and it inherits the tuned lens's habit of skipping to the answer.
  e102 [supporting] sparse-autoencoder (Sparse autoencoder (SAE)) --contrasts-with--> jacobian-lens (Jacobian lens (J-lens)) | trained dictionary versus one matrix: A sparse autoencoder must be trained, and its features carry no names until someone labels them. The J-lens is a single matrix multiplication per layer, with the matrix computed once per model, and every direction already carries a vocabulary token. The paper treats them as complements rather than rivals, and reads SAE decoder directions back through the lens to sort them by kurtosis.
  e103 [supporting] linear-probe (Linear probe) --contrasts-with--> jacobian-lens (Jacobian lens (J-lens)) | one supervised concept versus whole vocabulary: A linear probe is trained for one researcher-specified concept at a time, and what it reads is correlational. The J-lens supplies a direction for every token in the vocabulary without supervision, and those directions are derived from their average effect on the output. That is why the paper tests them by ablation and swap rather than by classification accuracy.
  e104 [supporting] j-lens-auditing-tool (The J-lens readout tool given to the auditor) --contrasts-with--> natural-language-autoencoder (Natural-language autoencoder (NLA)) | cheap readout, two failed cases: Two investigator agents were run on the same eighteen claims across six transcripts, differing only in what the readout tool returned. The J-lens condition slightly outperformed the natural-language autoencoder condition on four of the six cases and fell well short on two, scoring 4.5 against 10.0 on reward hacking and 6.0 against 9.9 on language switching. The ground truth was itself established from the NLA results, which the paper flags as a limit on the comparison.
