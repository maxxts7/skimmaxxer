NAME: What the J-space is, and how little it holds

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
The J-space is defined rather than assumed: if activations decompose into sparsely active features, those features form a frame, and restricting that frame to k-sparse nonnegative combinations gives a union of cones (eq-jspace-cones), with a distance from any activation to the nearest cone (eq-jspace-distance). The sparsity level k is not derived from the theory; it is fixed by hand from measured occupancy, and the decomposition itself is solved by gradient pursuit. Membership is tested by lens kurtosis, which projects a direction through the lens and measures how sharply peaked the resulting readout is. Capacity is then measured: around 25 active lens vectors per position in the median case, under 10% of activation variance beyond a same-size random control, and only about six unrelated words held at any point in an 80-word list (fig-31). Related words behave differently, loading as a whole category, and the limit is per token position rather than global, since attention still retrieves content from earlier positions; writing steps out as chain-of-thought relieves the same limit.

  e003 [load-bearing] sparse-frame (Sparse frame and subframe) --depends-on--> superposition (Superposition) | frame footing borrowed from superposition: The paper's formal footing is that activations decompose into sparsely active linear features, which is the superposition hypothesis rather than a result of this paper. Given that hypothesis, the feature directions form a sparse frame, and the J-lens vectors are a token-indexed subframe of it. The J-space is then a subset of possible activations rather than a subspace.
  e004 [load-bearing] sparsity-level-k (Choosing the sparsity level k) --depends-on--> j-space-occupancy (J-space occupancy) | k set by measured occupancy: The J-space is not defined until an allowable sparsity level $k$ is fixed, and the paper concedes the choice is somewhat arbitrary. It settles on values no greater than 25 because sparse reconstruction found roughly that many J-lens vectors meaningfully active at a time. The definition's free parameter is therefore pinned by a measurement made with the same machinery.
  e005 [supporting] sparse-decomposition (Sparse decomposition) --depends-on--> gradient-pursuit (Gradient pursuit) | decomposition solved by gradient pursuit: Because the J-lens vectors are overcomplete, an activation has many decompositions rather than one, so a discrete inventory of active concepts requires a search. The paper uses gradient pursuit, a greedy sparse-approximation algorithm, to find a sparse nonnegative combination that reconstructs the activation. The occupancy and variance-share numbers rest on that solver.
  e016 [load-bearing] lens-kurtosis (Lens kurtosis (κ)) --depends-on--> sparse-subframe (Sparse subframe) | peakedness is the membership test: To ask whether a sparse-autoencoder feature is in the J-space, the paper projects its decoder direction through the lens and measures how sharply peaked the resulting vocabulary distribution is. A subspace test would ask about projection magnitude instead. Peakedness is the right statistic precisely because the J-space is a sparse subframe, so what matters is loading onto a few J-lens vectors.
  e018 [supporting] per-position-capacity (A limit per token position) --depends-on--> attention-head (Attention head) | attention makes the limit per position: The occupancy result counts what the J-space holds at one token position, on the order of tens of vectors. Attention can still retrieve representations from any earlier position, so the model is not confined to that many concepts overall. The scope of the capacity claim depends on how attention moves information between positions.
  e037 [supporting] j-space-formalization (Formalization of the J-space) --supported-by--> eq-jspace-cones (The J-space as a union of k-sparse cones (eq-jspace-cones)) | J-space as a union of cones: The equation defines the J-space as the union, over every size-$k$ subset of J-lens vectors, of the cone of their nonnegative combinations. It makes explicit that the J-space is not a subspace: the vectors span the whole residual stream, and it is the sparsity limit, not the vectors, that does the restricting.
  e052 [load-bearing] j-space-capacity (J-space capacity) --supported-by--> fig-30 (Workspace occupancy and variance share (fig-30)) | occupancy and variance share measured: Sparse reconstruction puts occupancy near zero through the first third of the layers and at a plateau of about 25 active lens vectors per position across the workspace band, in the median case.   e076 [load-bearing] j-space-capacity (J-space capacity) --instance-of--> workspace-capacity-limit (Workspace capacity limit) | the capacity limit, measured here: Global workspace theory holds that the workspace carries only a small selection of ongoing activity, which is what makes entry to it competitive.   e107 [supporting] chain-of-thought (Chain-of-thought) --contrasts-with--> j-space-capacity (J-space capacity) | written steps relieve internal capacity: The J-space holds on the order of tens of concepts at one position, so intermediates carried internally compete for room.   e111 [supporting] category-loading (Category loading) --contrasts-with--> unrelated-list-capacity (Capacity for unrelated words) | category loads wide, unrelated words cap: With lists of unrelated words, only about six of the words read so far are present at any comma, and the count stays flat as the list runs on.   e112 [supporting] working-memory (Human working memory limits) --contrasts-with--> attention-head (Attention head) | decaying human store versus lossless retrieval: Human working memory is sharply limited in capacity and degrades within seconds, so the contents of the workspace a moment ago are already partly gone. Attention lets the model retrieve any representation computed at an earlier position, which relieves its workspace of maintaining state. The per-position limit of tens of concepts is comparable across the two; the access to the past is not.
  e124 [supporting] co-occupancy (Co-occupancy) --contrasts-with--> dual-task-cost (Cost of holding two covert tasks) | token exclusion, little overall cost: Two held concepts share a token at the control rate, 0.46 against 0.53, so at the token level they ignore each other, while a concept and an ongoing calculation almost never share one, 0.09 against 0.29. That exclusion costs little in total: each task's content still reaches the top of the lens somewhere in the response, and the largest decline is the computed answer's fall from 95% to 72%. The two measurements disagree about how much competition there is.
