NAME: Sensory, workspace, and motor layer bands

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
The paper's division of the model's depth into three functional regions: an early sensory band where the J-space is empty, a middle workspace band carrying persistent abstract content, and a late motor band aligned with the imminent output.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
The J-space is defined at every layer, but its content has workspace-like properties only in a band of intermediate layers. Comparing the geometry of J-lens vectors across layer pairs with CKA reveals a block structure: an early block spanning roughly the first third of the model, a long middle block, and a small late block. Since final-layer J-lens vectors must encode next-token predictions and first-layer vectors can only reflect the current token's identity, the workspace-like properties reside in the middle block — roughly layers 38 to 92 in the paper's percentage indexing. The paper notes that in some models the transition is more gradual, sometimes with sub-blocks, and that the observed sharpness is exaggerated by layer subsampling. Because these boundary metrics are derived from the J-lens itself, the paper flags that the apparent emptiness of early layers could be an artifact of the lens degenerating at those depths, leaving open that parts of the model's true workspace operate earlier.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-shape-of-the-workspace | Where the workspace sits and what it holds - Where the previous chapter asks what the workspace does, this one asks where in the model it sits and how much it can hold. The J-space carries workspace-like content only in a middle band of layers: it is nearly empty for roughly the first third of depth, and gives way to output-bound representations near the end. Sparse reconstruction puts its occupancy at tens of concepts per position and under a tenth of activation variance, a limit the paper compares with human working memory. The model's weights amplify and relay J-space directions more than comparable directions, which the paper reads as broadcast, and the commitment to one reading of an ambiguous input sharpens across the same band.

SUB-CONCEPTS (these render as expandable sections BELOW your page - reference them, do not duplicate them):
  - cka | CKA block structure: A method for comparing the geometry of two sets of vectors by comparing their matrices of pairwise similarities, used to compare J-lens vectors across layers.
  - motor-layers | Motor layers: The paper's term for late-layer representations tied to the model's imminent output, as opposed to workspace representations of intermediate computations.
    - jacobian-approaches-identity | The Jacobian approaching the identity in late layers: In the final layers the detached Jacobian $J_\ell$ approaches the identity, so the J-lens readout $W_U J_\ell$ collapses toward the plain unembedding projection.
  - sensory-layers | Sensory layers: Roughly the first third of the model's layers, where J-lens readouts are noisy and the J-space carries essentially no meaningful content.
    - early-layer-lens-degeneracy | Lens degeneracy in early layers: The paper's caveat that the empty early band may be a failure of the J-lens at those depths rather than a fact about the model.
  - workspace-boundary-metrics | Workspace boundary metrics: Four lens-derived statistics — next-token accuracy, readout kurtosis, top-1 autocorrelation, and effective dimensionality — that jointly locate the workspace's start and end.
    - j-space-effective-dimensionality | Effective dimensionality of the J-space: The fraction of residual-stream dimensions needed to capture a given share of variance across the J-lens vectors $W_U J_\ell$.
    - lens-next-token-accuracy | Lens next-token accuracy: The fraction of positions at which any top-k J-lens token matches the model's top-1 prediction — near zero early, rising slowly, and jumping steeply in the final layers.
    - readout-excess-kurtosis | Excess kurtosis of readouts: A measure of how nonrandom a J-lens readout is: high kurtosis means the logit distribution is sharply peaked on a few tokens.
    - top1-autocorrelation | Top-1 token autocorrelation: The rate at which the same token stays at the top of the lens readout across nearby positions, relative to a position-shuffled null.
      - position-shuffled-null | Position-shuffled null: The baseline the autocorrelation measurement is reported against: the same readouts with their positions shuffled, so persistence is scored as an excess over chance repetition.
  - workspace-layers | Workspace layer range: The finding that the J-space plays a workspace-like role only in a middle range of layers: nearly empty for roughly the first third of depth, and giving way in the final layers to representations of the imminent output.
  - layer-subsampling | Layer subsampling: The analyses are computed at a subset of the model's layers, which makes the block boundaries in the CKA matrix look sharper than they are.

PREREQUISITES:
  - j-lens-vector | J-lens vector: One vector per vocabulary token per layer: the direction in residual-stream space that, on average across contexts, disposes the model to say that token.
  - j-space | J-space: The set of activation-space points expressible as a sparse nonnegative combination of J-lens vectors — the region of representation space the paper argues functions as a global workspace.
  - layer-percentage-indexing | Layer numbering as percentages: The paper's reporting convention: 25 evenly spaced layers of the residual stream, reindexed to 0–100 so that layer numbers read as percentages of depth.

EVIDENCE ITEMS THAT SHOW THIS (link them as [[item-id]]):
  - fig-27 | The three layer bands: The paper divides the model's depth into three functional regions: an early sensory band where the J-space carries essentially no meaningful content, a middle workspace band (roughly L38 to L92 in Sonnet 4.5) where it holds persistent abstract content, and a late motor band where its vectors align with the imminent output token.
  - fig-56 | Where the three lenses agree, layer by layer: Comparing the logit, tuned, and Jacobian lenses against each other rather than against an external target, the three agreement measures pick out four ranges of depth whose boundaries line up with the workspace band identified independently in section 4.1. Direction agreement and readout agreement come apart in the early workspace: the J-lens and logit lens vectors swing into rough alignment while their actual readouts still disagree.
  - fig-64 | Oracle-lens phrase readouts on nine prompts: The oracle lens decodes a single activation into ten free-form phrases rather than a ranked list of single tokens, and across nine prompts those phrases name latent content that the surface text does not contain: that a stated drug dose is toxic, that an email is an attempt at blackmail, that a loop will raise a TypeError. It is a demonstration on selected examples, not a benchmark.
  - fig-67 | Imagining Python versus reading Python: An instruction to imagine that a JavaScript snippet is Python pushes the token python high in the J-lens readout at the snippet's own tokens, while a probe for Python-ness with its J-space part removed stays at its no-instruction baseline. Real Python code moves that probe by three to six standard deviations, and only sometimes moves the lens. The paper reads the pair of results as evidence that the instruction writes to the workspace rather than re-encoding the stimulus.
  - fig-69 | Naming a concept versus avoiding it: Ablating an implied concept's J-lens vector at the early workspace layers (L38–54) leaves the model's ability to name that concept largely intact, but makes it fail roughly five times as often when instructed to name something else. Ablating the same vector at the late workspace layers (L75–92) instead lowers the concept's probability under both instructions. The paper reads this as evidence that the early workspace representation is what the model uses to actively suppress a concept, while the late one represents the intention to say it.
  - fig-73 | Two covert tasks competing for the same tokens: When the model is told to hold two things in mind while copying an unrelated sentence, both reach the workspace, but they share it differently: two concepts occupy the same tokens at about the rate a shuffled control predicts, while a concept and an ongoing arithmetic computation almost never occupy the same token. Splitting the workspace this way costs each task little overall reach, with the arithmetic answer paying the largest price.
