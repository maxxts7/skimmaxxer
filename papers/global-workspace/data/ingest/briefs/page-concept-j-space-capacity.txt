NAME: J-space capacity

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
The finding that the J-space is limited: it holds on the order of tens of concepts at a time, carries under 10% of activation variance, and excludes most of the model's representational features.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
[[global-workspace-theory]] predicts a workspace of limited capacity, and the paper documents this for the J-space in three ways. Occupancy — the number of J-lens vectors meaningfully active at once — plateaus around 25 in the workspace band. The variance the J-space explains in excess of random controls never exceeds 10%, indicating that activations are dominated by information outside it. And only a small fraction of [[sparse-autoencoder|SAE]] features align with the J-space, with the non-aligned majority dominated by low-level [[bookkeeping-features|syntactic and bookkeeping features]]. Word-list experiments refine the picture: the J-space holds many related words at once by representing their shared category, but only around six unrelated recent words pooled across layers — one to two at any single layer — and a task-switching experiment suggests cognitive load influences whether concepts can co-occupy it.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-shape-of-the-workspace | Where the workspace sits and what it holds - Where the previous chapter asks what the workspace does, this one asks where in the model it sits and how much it can hold. The J-space carries workspace-like content only in a middle band of layers: it is nearly empty for roughly the first third of depth, and gives way to output-bound representations near the end. Sparse reconstruction puts its occupancy at tens of concepts per position and under a tenth of activation variance, a limit the paper compares with human working memory. The model's weights amplify and relay J-space directions more than comparable directions, which the paper reads as broadcast, and the commitment to one reading of an ambiguous input sharpens across the same band.
