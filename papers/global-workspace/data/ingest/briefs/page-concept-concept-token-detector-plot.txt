DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
### Why this chart and not another

Section 5 asks whether the [[jacobian-lens]] can screen a model for a disposition its behavior does not reveal. The qualitative form of that evidence is one readout on one prompt: figures 38 and 40 print the top lens tokens for a single request with deception words in the list. One prompt settles nothing about a screening method, so the reading must run over a prompt set and come out as a number.

The lens supplies no number. A [[j-lens-readout]] at one position and one layer is a ranked list over the whole vocabulary, read at every one of the [[workspace-layers]]. The [[deception-token-readout]] manufactures the scalar: a word list fixed in advance, a rank cutoff, one point each time a listed word falls inside the cutoff, summed across the layers.

That construction is why the chart carries dots as well as bars. The measure is zero-inflated and capped, and a mean alone would not separate a signal on nearly every prompt from one large count on two — and the claim is about screening prompt by prompt. The control column carries the rest: a count of 3 has no reading except beside a model scanned with the same list that returns nothing.

### How to read it, step by step

The horizontal axis is categorical, holding whatever is under comparison: the four points of the [[checkpoint-lineage]] in figure 39, model crossed with reading method in figure 41, prompt category in its right panel. The vertical axis is a count of appearances. Its floor is a hard zero, and its ceiling comes from the method rather than the model — a word scores at most once per layer, so the number of workspace layers scanned bounds the axis. Figure 41 tops out at 5.

One dot is one prompt; the bar is the mean over that group's prompts. Colour encodes either the counted word — figure 41 draws *reward* in orange and *bias* in red — or the prompt category. The rank cutoff differs by figure: top-10 in figure 39, top-25 in figure 41. The control column is where to look first, then the spread of dots within each bar.

A count is not a probability and not a measure of intent. A prompt scoring 5 rather than 1 had a listed word surface in five workspace layers rather than one. The word lists were also chosen with the implanted objective already known, so these charts show a known signature separating cleanly, not blind discovery.

### What a bad result would look like

The baseline column off the floor. If the clean checkpoint, scanned with the same list, returned counts of two or three, the measure would be reading the lens or the prompts rather than anything training put there.

Or the dots pile at zero under a mean dragged up by two prompts: a screen that works only on prompts already suspected.

Or the signal appears only where the prompt text supplies the word. Figure 41 shows that failure inside itself: the [[logit-lens]] on the same model scores 2, both hits on the one prompt whose text says "reward models". Counts concentrated on the goal-probing prompts would be the same failure in another form: a model asked about its hidden goal has been handed the concept.

### Where it comes back

Figure 39 (§5.4) is the plain case: four checkpoints of the [[reward-hacking-organism]], a deception word list, top-10, and three panels for the three [[readout-positions]], the middle one a [[prefill]]ed " I".

Figure 41 (§5.5) counts two words separately by colour, raises the cutoff to top-25, and puts a second reading method on the same axis, so the logit-lens comparison sits inside the chart. Its right panel re-splits those counts by prompt category, which carries the claim that the signature is not elicited by the question.

Figure 82 (§10.21) keeps the shape and changes the reduction. Rather than counting rank hits, the [[eval-awareness-score]] averages the lens log-probability of two words and subtracts the mean over a hundred control words. It is continuous, has no fixed ceiling, and rests on no clean zero: deployment traffic centres near 1.0 and the evaluation suites overlap it.

PREREQUISITES:
  - jacobian-lens | Jacobian lens (J-lens): The paper's new interpretability technique: for each vocabulary token, it identifies the vector direction that encodes the model's potential to verbalize that token, by averaging the linearized effect of activations on the token's likelihood over a large corpus.
  - j-lens-readout | J-lens readout: The basic read operation of the lens: $\text{lens}(h_\ell) = \text{softmax}(W_U\,\text{norm}(J_\ell h_\ell))$, giving a score for every vocabulary token.
  - workspace-layers | Workspace layer range: The finding that the J-space plays a workspace-like role only in a middle range of layers: nearly empty for roughly the first third of depth, and giving way in the final layers to representations of the imminent output.
  - deception-token-readout | Deception-token readout: A screening method: counting how often a fixed vocabulary of deception-related tokens appears in the J-lens top-10 at the start of the model's response.
  - model-organism | Model organism: Models deliberately trained to have misaligned behaviors, used as testbeds; the J-lens uncovers internal signatures of their implanted intentions.
  - alignment-auditing | Alignment auditing with the J-lens: Using J-lens readouts to inspect a model's silent strategic and situational cognition in safety evaluations, where the relevant reasoning does not appear in the output.
  - logit-lens | Logit lens: An earlier interpretability technique that decodes intermediate residual-stream states into the model's output vocabulary by applying the final unembedding matrix at every layer.
