NAME: Ambiguity commitment plot

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
A readout of how far an activation has moved between two blended concepts as the input blend is swept, plotted against depth. It is the paper's test of whether the model commits to one interpretation of a deliberately ambiguous input, and at which layer that commitment appears.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
### Why this chart and not another

[[global-workspace-theory]] predicts [[ignition]]: a late, all-or-none amplification of one reading of an ambiguous input, with bimodal outcomes when the evidence sits at threshold. Ordinary sentences offer no dial for holding an input at threshold, so the [[embedding-mixture-experiment]] manufactures one: a concept token's input embedding is replaced by $(1-\alpha)\,e_B + \alpha\,e_A$, a blend of two single-token country embeddings, inside a carrier sentence such as "My sister has always wanted to visit ___".

That dial forces the rest of the shape. The claim concerns where in depth a smooth input turns into a discrete choice, so $\alpha$ and layer both have to be on the page at once; a bar chart would fix one of them, and a table of final-layer outputs would delete the depth at which the switch arrives — which is the test, since the paper is checking whether that depth matches the workspace onset at L38 located independently in section 4.1 ([[workspace-layers]]). Each pair's activations differ, so the quantity is per-trial too: the [[projection-share]] places an activation on the line joining that trial's own pure-$B$ and pure-$A$ [[activations]] at the same position and layer, 0 and 1 at the ends by construction. And an average cannot tell an activation that represents ambiguity from one that flips a coin, since both give 0.5, so the third figure drops the average and plots the spread.

### How to read it, step by step

In Figure 29B the horizontal axis is $\alpha$, swept 0 to 1 and shifted so each trial's own switching threshold lands in the same place; the vertical axis is layer, on the paper's 0–100 [[layer-percentage-indexing]] over 25 sampled [[residual-stream]] layers. Colour is the projection share, one country through a white midpoint at 0.5 to the other, aggregated over the 640 trials (sixteen pairs by forty sentences). Reading upward, early rows shade smoothly across $\alpha$; from about layer 38 a row becomes two flat fields split by an abrupt boundary, white only at the threshold. Panel C repeats this through the [[jacobian-lens]]: the rank of whichever country ranks higher, and the same share restricted to the two countries' [[j-lens-vector]] directions.

Figure 70 collapses each row to one number. Layer runs horizontally, [[transition-width]] vertically: the change in $\alpha$ needed for the share to move from 10% to 90%. A proportional response gives 0.8 and a step gives near 0, so lower is sharper. A point is a median over trials; the band is the interquartile range across prompts, not a confidence interval. Two series are drawn: the [[j-space-component]] along the two lens vectors, and the complement left after the top sixteen lens vectors are projected out.

Figure 71 fixes $\alpha$ at each pair's most ambiguous value and puts the projection share on the horizontal axis, one distribution per component at four layers. Mass at 0.5 means no commitment; mass at both ends is [[bimodal-commitment]].

$\alpha$ is an input the model never receives in use, so a sharp curve is a fact about how a layer resolves a fabricated blend, not about the sentence.

### What a bad result would look like

Panel B would keep the same smooth gradient at every depth, colour tracking $\alpha$ to the top row. Figure 70's curves would stay flat near 0.8 instead of falling over layers 21 to 42. Figure 71 would keep one hump at 0.5 in every panel — which is also what a faithful representation of ambiguity looks like, so that panel is the one to check first. The alignment claim fails separately: sharpening at layer 10, or only at layer 70, would say nothing about the workspace onset. The [[j-space]] claim fails if Figure 70's two series coincide, or if the non-J-space complement narrows first.

### Where it comes back

The three figures put $\alpha$, layer and trial on different axes. Figure 29 spreads $\alpha$ and layer and pools trials; Figure 70 keeps layer, collapses $\alpha$ into a width, and splits J-space from non-J-space; Figure 71 fixes $\alpha$ at threshold and opens the trials the others pool. The paper hedges what the last two show: the J-space transition is "consistently somewhat narrower" and reaches its floor earlier — a difference of degree, since the non-J-space activation sharpens too, only later.

  - ignition | Ignition: In global workspace theory, the late, all-or-none amplification of one interpretation of an ambiguous input; the paper finds an analogous sharp commitment emerging at the workspace onset layer.
  - residual-stream | Residual stream: The running vector at each token position that a transformer's layers read from and write to; the activations that all the paper's readouts and interventions act on.
  - activations | Activations: The internal vector states a network computes at each layer and position during a forward pass.
  - jacobian-lens | Jacobian lens (J-lens): The paper's new interpretability technique: for each vocabulary token, it identifies the vector direction that encodes the model's potential to verbalize that token, by averaging the linearized effect of activations on the token's likelihood over a large corpus.
  - j-lens-vector | J-lens vector: One vector per vocabulary token per layer: the direction in residual-stream space that, on average across contexts, disposes the model to say that token.
  - j-space | J-space: The set of activation-space points expressible as a sparse nonnegative combination of J-lens vectors — the region of representation space the paper argues functions as a global workspace.
  - j-space-component | J-space and non-J-space components: For any activation, its J-space component is the nearest point in the J-space; the non-J-space component is the leftover difference.
  - workspace-layers | Workspace layer range: The finding that the J-space plays a workspace-like role only in a middle range of layers: nearly empty for roughly the first third of depth, and giving way in the final layers to representations of the imminent output.
  - layer-percentage-indexing | Layer numbering as percentages: The paper's reporting convention: 25 evenly spaced layers of the residual stream, reindexed to 0–100 so that layer numbers read as percentages of depth.
  - embedding-mixture-experiment | Ambiguous-input embedding mixtures: The experimental construction: a concept token's input embedding is replaced by the weighted mixture $(1-\alpha)\,e_B + \alpha\,e_A$ inside ordinary sentences, sweeping $\alpha$ from 0 to 1.
  - projection-share | Projection share: Where an activation sits along the line connecting the same trial's pure-$B$ and pure-$A$ activations at the same position and layer — 0 at one end of the sweep, 1 at the other.
