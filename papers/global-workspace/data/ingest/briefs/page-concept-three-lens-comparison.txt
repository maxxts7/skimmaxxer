DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
### Why this chart and not another

The paper introduces a readout method and must place it against two that already exist, so the appendix builds a fixed three-way comparison and reruns it. One number could not carry the argument: a lens can be judged on whether it names a concept the model never says, on whether erasing its direction changes the answer, and on whether it anticipates the output, and the [[lens-evaluation|comparison suite]] finds these come apart.

A single bar per lens would also fix everything the comparison is arguing about: one $k$, one corpus size, one recipe. Fixing $k$ hides that the [[tuned-lens]] climbs past the [[logit-lens]] beyond $k=20$ on three of the six distributions, so its deficit there is one of ranking rather than of the concept being absent. Averaging the distributions into one score hides that the [[jacobian-lens]] margin over the logit lens is 0.04 on multihop and 0.31 on typo. A table would carry those numbers and lose the shapes the sweeps exist to show: curves that flatten, lines that cross, orderings that hold across a whole axis.

### How to read it, step by step

The colour key is fixed across every figure: black is the J-lens, red the logit lens, blue the tuned lens. Everything else moves.

Panels are conditions: usually the six prompt distributions of the [[intermediate-concept-benchmarks]] — multihop, multilingual, poetry, order_ops, association, typo — or three of them, or the two settings of [[jacobian-aggregation]].

The x-axis changes between figures: $k$, log-scaled 1 to 100; the [[lens-corpus|number of prompts averaged into the lens]], log-scaled 1 to 1000; depth, under [[layer-percentage-indexing]], with 25 sampled layers relabelled 0 to 100; or the token length of the concept, bucketed 1, 2, 3, 4+.

The y-axis is the metric, and the direction of good is not constant. Higher is better for [[pass-at-k-auc|normalized pass@k AUC]], a recovery rate, and for the [[ablation-kl-effect|ablation KL]] induced by projecting out a lens direction; agreement with the model's own output, measured as [[kl-divergence]], runs the other way.

One point is one lens on one benchmark at one setting of the swept variable, averaged over that benchmark's 50 to 96 items. In the corpus sweeps the baselines are flat lines, since neither is built from the corpus being varied.

The first thing to read is the ordering of the colours at the right edge, then whether it survives the whole axis. Y-ranges are per panel, so heights compare within a panel and not across.

### What a bad result would look like

Black interleaving with red — ahead on two distributions, behind on two — would make the J-lens a different readout rather than a better one. In the corpus sweeps, a black curve clearing the red line only after several hundred prompts would attribute the gain to averaging rather than to the Jacobian; the crossings in fact happen at two to ten prompts. In the recipe heatmap, an avg column swinging widely down the rows would locate the effect in the recipe, not the method.

Figure 55 is the case where black loses, and the paper reports it deliberately: it is the metric the tuned lens was fitted on. The J-lens carries higher KL against the model's [[next-token-prediction|output distribution]] than even the uncorrected logit lens through most of the network. The paper calls that a feature, arguing that the directions best at anticipating the output are not the ones that best expose or drive the computation — a reading that rests on the causal comparisons before it rather than on this figure.

### Where it comes back

Figure 58 drops the line chart for a heatmap of five [[j-lens-recipe-variants|recipe variants]] under two aggregations, replacing the three-lens key with a within-method one. PREREQUISITES:
  - jacobian-lens | Jacobian lens (J-lens): The paper's new interpretability technique: for each vocabulary token, it identifies the vector direction that encodes the model's potential to verbalize that token, by averaging the linearized effect of activations on the token's likelihood over a large corpus.
  - logit-lens | Logit lens: An earlier interpretability technique that decodes intermediate residual-stream states into the model's output vocabulary by applying the final unembedding matrix at every layer.
  - tuned-lens | Tuned lens: A related technique that trains per-layer affine maps to match the model's output distribution — an objective the paper argues is correlational rather than causal.
  - j-lens-readout | J-lens readout: The basic read operation of the lens: $\text{lens}(h_\ell) = \text{softmax}(W_U\,\text{norm}(J_\ell h_\ell))$, giving a score for every vocabulary token.
  - intermediate-concept-benchmarks | Intermediate-concept benchmarks: Six prompt distributions in which the model must compute a known single-token intermediate concept that is neither in the input nor identical to the predicted output.
  - pass-at-k-auc | Pass@k AUC: The summary metric for intermediate recovery: pass@k computed over a range of k, plotted against $\log k$, with the area under the curve normalized so a method that always ranks the intermediate first scores 1.
  - kl-divergence | KL divergence: The standard measure of how much one probability distribution diverges from another, used here to quantify shifts in the model's output distribution.
  - residual-stream | Residual stream: The running vector at each token position that a transformer's layers read from and write to; the activations that all the paper's readouts and interventions act on.
  - tokenizer-vocabulary | Tokenizer vocabulary: The fixed set of tokens, usually words and word pieces, into which a model's tokenizer splits text; the unit the J-lens is built over.
