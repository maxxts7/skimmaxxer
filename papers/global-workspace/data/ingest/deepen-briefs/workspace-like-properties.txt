CONCEPT TO DEEPEN: workspace-like-properties
name: The five workspace-like properties
summary: The paper's operational definition: a set of vector representations counts as workspace-like if it supports verbal report, directed modulation, internal reasoning, flexible generalization, and selectivity.

Its explanation as it stands:
The paper defines what it would mean for a language model to have a global workspace by five testable properties, each mirroring a functional signature of conscious access in humans: information that is reportable, subject to top-down control, the medium of deliberate reasoning, flexibly generalizable, and selective. Each property is stated as a claim about a candidate subset of vector representations and is tested by measurement and intervention experiments. The paper reports identifying the candidate set through the first property, [[verbal-report]], and then finding, described as rather surprising, that the same set satisfies the other four.

WHAT ALREADY EXISTS UNDER IT (28 concepts). Do not restate any of these; they are here so you can see what is left.
  - directed-modulation (Directed modulation): The property that the model can deliberately activate and compute with workspace vectors when instructed, independent of its outputs, and can pull normally absent information into the workspace when a task requires it.
    - character-counting-task (Character-counting task): A task in which the model silently tracks the number of characters in each line of a passage, used to probe covert numeric content in the J-space.
    - hold-in-mind-copy-task (Hold-in-mind copying task): A protocol in which the model copies an unrelated sentence while an instruction tells it what to hold in mind; the lens is read at the copied tokens.
    - instruction-conditions (Instruction conditions (focus, ignore, mention, don't-think)): The four instruction types compared in the modulation experiments, which produce different levels of target-concept activation in the J-space.
    - j-orthogonalized-property-probe (J-orthogonalized property probe): A linear probe for a stimulus property with its J-space component projected out, used to show that instructions move the J-space but not the underlying representation.
    - paired-question-protocol (Paired-question protocol): Presenting the same stimulus under two different questions to show that the question alone determines whether a property's label enters the J-space.
    - white-bear-effect (White bear effect): The human finding that instructions to suppress a thought increase its occurrence relative to no instruction at all; the paper observes a parallel in the J-space.
    - workspace-competition (Workspace competition under concurrent covert tasks): When the model holds two covert tasks at once, static concepts share workspace tokens freely, but an ongoing computation excludes other content from the tokens it occupies.
      - co-occupancy (Co-occupancy): A named quantity: at tokens where one covert task's content is in the lens top-10, the probability that the other task's content is too.
  - flexible-generalization (Flexible generalization (broadcast)): The property that the same workspace vector serves as a valid argument to many different downstream computations: lifted from one context and placed in another, it is correctly operated on by whatever function the new context supplies.
    - function-template-protocol (Function-template protocol): The design of the flexible-generalization experiment: four argument categories, four functions each, and twelve source-target swap pairs per function.
    - workspace-loading (Workspace loading): A named quantity: the cosine similarity between the residual stream and a concept's lens vector, averaged over the relevant positions of an unmodified forward pass.
  - flexible-vs-automatic-dissociation (Flexible versus automatic cognition): The paper's proposed criterion for when a computation engages the J-space: it does when an intermediate result must be handed to an arbitrary, context-specified downstream circuit, and does not when the computation is automatic.
    - language-passage-tasks (Language-passage task battery): Four tasks over the same foreign-language passage, chosen so that all depend on the passage's language but only some should require the workspace.
    - linewrap-task (Linewrap task (information pulled into the J-space on demand)): A character-count task where the count is absent from the J-space during automatic line-wrapping but appears when a question requires reporting or computing with it.
    - naming-vs-avoidance (Naming versus avoiding a primed concept): An experiment showing early workspace layers are required to deliberately avoid a primed concept, while late layers are required to name it.
      - inclusion-exclusion-paradigm (Inclusion/exclusion paradigm): A paradigm from human memory research in which the ability to deliberately avoid a primed response, rather than produce it, is taken as a marker of conscious processing.
  - internal-reasoning (Internal reasoning): The property that workspace vectors carry the values of intermediate computations when the model chains inferential steps, and that intervening on them redirects the conclusion.
    - arithmetic-intermediate-ordering (Arithmetic intermediates surface in computation order): In a two-step arithmetic problem, the intermediate values reach the top of the J-lens at successively later layers, in the order the computation requires.
    - bandit-choice-prompt (Bandit choice prompt): A reward-driven A/B choice task in which the lens surfaces the model's selected strategy (repeat or switch) and swapping it flips the choice.
    - intermediate-probe (Intermediate probe): A J-lens-free representation of an unspoken intermediate, built by averaging activations over prompts that imply the same intermediate through different surface cues.
    - rhyme-planning (Rhyme planning): When completing a rhyming couplet, the model selects the line-ending rhyme in advance, and this planned word is visible in the J-lens and steerable.
    - shared-multilingual-representation (Shared multilingual representation): The finding that multilingual models route some computation through an English-aligned intermediate representation, visible here as English tokens in the lens during a Chinese task.
    - swap-depth-control (Intermediate-versus-answer swap depth control): A control comparing at which layers swapping the intermediate versus swapping the answer takes effect, to rule out the intermediate's lens vector containing the answer.
  - selectivity (Selectivity): The property that the workspace is a small subset of the model's total representational content, required for only a fraction of its behavior and not for pervasive routine processing.
  - verbal-report (Verbal report from the J-space): The property that, when asked what it is thinking about, the model names concepts represented in the workspace, and swapping one active workspace vector for another changes its answer to match.
    - injected-thought-introspection (Injected-thought introspection protocol): A protocol in which the model is told a thought may have been implanted in its activations and is asked to report what, if anything, it detects.
    - think-of-a-category-task (Think-of-a-category task): A prompt asking the model to think of an item from a named category and then name it in one word; the lens is read just before the name is produced.
