CONCEPT TO DEEPEN: j-space-capacity
name: J-space capacity
summary: The finding that the J-space is limited: it holds on the order of tens of concepts at a time, carries under 10% of activation variance, and excludes most of the model's representational features.

Its explanation as it stands:
[[global-workspace-theory]] predicts a workspace of limited capacity, and the paper documents this for the J-space in three ways. Occupancy — the number of J-lens vectors meaningfully active at once — plateaus around 25 in the workspace band. The variance the J-space explains in excess of random controls never exceeds 10%, indicating that activations are dominated by information outside it. And only a small fraction of [[sparse-autoencoder|SAE]] features align with the J-space, with the non-aligned majority dominated by low-level [[bookkeeping-features|syntactic and bookkeeping features]]. Word-list experiments refine the picture: the J-space holds many related words at once by representing their shared category, but only around six unrelated recent words pooled across layers — one to two at any single layer — and a task-switching experiment suggests cognitive load influences whether concepts can co-occupy it.
