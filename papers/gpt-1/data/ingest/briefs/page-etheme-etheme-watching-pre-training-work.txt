NAME: Watching Pre-Training Do the Work

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
Two diagnostics in Figure 2 try to say why the recipe works, rather than that it works. The first varies how many pre-trained layers are carried into fine-tuning, a sweep that only exists because the stack is twelve blocks deep, and finds each transferred layer adding performance, up to 9 percent on MultiNLI for the full transfer — which is the paper's case that moving embeddings alone leaves value in the upper layers. The second runs the pre-trained model on the tasks with no labelled training at all, using heuristics built on one device: score a candidate string by the mean log-probability the language model assigns its tokens, which is the training quantity read back out. That device carries CoLA by thresholding a sentence's mean score, RACE by ranking candidate answers, and the Winograd-style DPRD set by substituting each referent for the pronoun and keeping the higher-scoring resolution, a coreference decision made by scoring; SST-2 works differently, appending 'very' and restricting the output distribution to two words. These heuristic scores rise steadily over pre-training, which the paper reads as task ability accumulating without supervision, and it adds the hypothesis that the transformer's attention gives a more structured memory than an LSTM, pointing at the LSTM's higher zero-shot variance as support. Two claims run ahead of the diagnostic: the inductive-bias hypothesis rests on a variance comparison, and the conclusion's talk of world knowledge acquired from predicting text is broader than the curves shown.
