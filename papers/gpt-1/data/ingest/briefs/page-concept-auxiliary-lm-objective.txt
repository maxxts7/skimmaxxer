NAME: Auxiliary Language Modelling Objective

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
During fine-tuning the paper keeps optimizing the language modelling loss alongside the task loss: $L_3(C) = L_2(C) + \lambda\, L_1(C)$.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
The next-token loss does not disappear when stage two begins. It is recomputed on the fine-tuning data $C$ — the same task inputs, scored simply as text — and added to the classification loss with weight $\lambda$.

The paper reports two effects from including it: better generalization of the supervised model, and faster convergence.

Both are stated as empirical findings. No mechanism is offered, and the paper notes prior work observed similar gains from adding a language modelling term to a supervised objective.

Section 5 puts a limit on the claim. Ablating the term shows it helps on the natural language inference tasks and on QQP, while smaller datasets do not benefit.

So the reported trend is that larger fine-tuning sets gain from it and smaller ones do not.

The language modelling loss is not dropped: it keeps being optimized alongside the task loss, scaled by a weight the paper sets to 0.5. Tasks whose inputs have several parts are handled by writing those parts into one ordered sequence with a start token, an end token and a delimiter between parts, rather than by building an architecture that mirrors the structure. The chapter ends with the settings actually used, which are the pre-training settings with a smaller learning rate and three passes over the task data.
