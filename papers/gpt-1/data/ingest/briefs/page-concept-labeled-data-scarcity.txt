DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
Deep models for language tasks are normally fitted on manually annotated examples. Producing those annotations costs people, time and money, so the datasets stay small relative to what a large model could absorb.

Raw text costs nothing by comparison. It exists in enormous quantity and needs no annotator at all.

The abstract states the gap plainly: large unlabeled corpora are plentiful while labelled data for specific tasks is scarce, which makes it hard for discriminatively trained models to perform adequately.

The paper's whole design follows from trying to spend the abundant resource to relieve the scarce one. It adds a second motivation as well: even where supervision is plentiful, learning representations without labels can still give a boost.
