A single architecture, pre-trained once, applied to every task without being redesigned for any of them.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
The abstract's central claim is comparative: the general task-agnostic model outperforms discriminatively trained models that use architectures specifically crafted for each task.

Task-agnostic here means the network's structure carries no assumptions about entailment, or multiple-choice answers, or sentence pairs. Those assumptions live in the input format and in a small output layer instead.

The introduction frames the goal as learning a universal representation that transfers with little adaptation to a wide range of tasks. Universal does two jobs in that sentence: one representation rather than one per task, and a low adaptation cost.

The evidence offered is breadth. Four task types, twelve datasets, one pre-training run, with improvement on the previous best result for nine of the twelve.

Nothing is frozen and no task-specific network is built on top, so the same architecture is what gets fine-tuned every time. The paper also states that the unlabelled corpus need not come from the target task's domain. This chapter is the hinge between the problem and the machinery, and the two stages that follow are its halves.
