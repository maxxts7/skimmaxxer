NAME: Impact of Number of Layers Transferred

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
An analysis varying how many pre-trained layers are carried into fine-tuning, showing each transformer layer adds performance, up to 9% on MultiNLI for full transfer.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
After pre-training, the paper can choose how much of the model to carry into the supervised task. Transfer zero layers and only the embeddings come across; transfer all twelve and the whole stack does.

The analysis sweeps that count on MultiNLI and RACE and plots performance against it.

Two things come out. Transferring embeddings helps, which the paper calls the standard result and which matches years of word-vector practice.

Beyond that, each additional transformer layer keeps adding, up to 9% for full transfer on MultiNLI.

The paper's reading is that each layer of the pre-trained model contains useful functionality for solving target tasks, not just the bottom embedding layer. That is the argument against treating pre-training as a source of word vectors only.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-taking-the-recipe-apart | Taking the Recipe Apart - Three diagnostics ask which parts of the recipe are doing the work. Transferring more pre-trained layers keeps helping rather than saturating early, and hand-built heuristics show the pre-trained model already doing several tasks at some level with no supervised training at all, improving steadily as pre-training runs. The removals are blunter: no pre-training costs the most, swapping the transformer for an LSTM costs less, and dropping the auxiliary language modelling loss helps on some datasets and hurts on others. The paper's reading of why attention transfers better than recurrence is stated as a hypothesis, and the zero-shot heuristics are hand-designed, so both carry less weight than the ablation numbers.
