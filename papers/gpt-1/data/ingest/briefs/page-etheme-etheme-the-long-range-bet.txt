NAME: The Bet on Long Stretches of Text

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
The corpus is chosen as an argument, not a convenience. BooksCorpus is over 7,000 unpublished books, and what the paper wants from it is long stretches of contiguous text — sentences left in their original order across paragraphs — which it sets against the 1B Word Benchmark, a corpus of roughly the same size but shuffled at the sentence level. The context window follows: $k$ is fixed at 512 tokens, which only means something if the training text actually runs that long without breaking, and 512 tokens is the counter to the paper's complaint that recurrent pre-training keeps its predictions local and so transfers less structure. The dependencies this is meant to capture are named concretely — a pronoun and the antecedent it refers to several sentences back, or a RACE exam question and the passage detail that answers it, RACE being chosen over CNN and SQuAD on the grounds that it asks for reasoning rather than extraction. The evidence for the corpus choice stays indirect: the paper reports a token-level perplexity of 18.4 to show the model fit BooksCorpus, and points at the RACE and Story Cloze results as a proxy, but it never runs the comparison against a shuffled corpus that would test the claim directly.
