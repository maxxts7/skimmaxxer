NAME: One Stack, Any Input Shape

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
A pre-trained decoder reads one ordered sequence of tokens, but the evaluation tasks hand over premise-hypothesis pairs and document-question-answer triplets. The paper's move is to change the input rather than the model: each structured input is walked into a single sequence, bracketed by a start and an end token and split by a `$` delimiter, so entailment becomes premise-delimiter-hypothesis, similarity is built in both orderings and processed twice, and multiple choice becomes one sequence per candidate answer. What sits on top is Equation 3 and nothing else — take the final block's activation at the last token and multiply it by one matrix $W_y$ to get label scores, with Equation 4 stating the summed log-probability of the correct labels as the objective. Two properties follow from this and are the point of the design: the same architecture serves every task, against the prevailing practice of hand-designing a network around each task's structure, and the per-task parameter cost is one matrix plus embeddings for the delimiter tokens, against transfer styles that initialize a fresh task model each time. The last-token readout is also why the similarity transformation exists at all — the readout is order-sensitive, so both orderings are summed element-wise before the head, where entailment simply keeps its one order.
