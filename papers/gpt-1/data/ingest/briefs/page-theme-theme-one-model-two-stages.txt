NAME: One Model, Two Stages

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
The recipe in outline: train a network to predict next tokens on a large unlabelled corpus, then take those same weights as the starting point for supervised training on a target task. Nothing is frozen and no task-specific network is built on top, so the same architecture is what gets fine-tuned every time. The paper also states that the unlabelled corpus need not come from the target task's domain. This chapter is the hinge between the problem and the machinery, and the two stages that follow are its halves.

  - table-2 | NLI Benchmark Scoreboard: Table 2 compares one fine-tuned general model against task-built baselines on five natural language inference datasets, and reports it ahead on four of them — by 1.5 points on MNLI, 5 on SciTail, 5.8 on QNLI and 0.6 on SNLI — while losing on RTE, the smallest dataset, where a multi-task biLSTM scores 61.7 against this model's 56.0.
  - table-3 | RACE and Story Cloze Results: On the paper's two reading-and-reasoning benchmarks, a single fine-tuned transformer language model beats every previously published system, by 8.9 accuracy points on the Story Cloze Test and 5.7 points on RACE overall. The RACE baselines it passes are 9-model ensembles, while the paper's entry is one model.
  - table-4 | Classification and Similarity Results: On the five GLUE tasks outside natural language inference, one fine-tuned transformer beats the previous best published number on CoLA, STS-B and QQP, and raises the overall GLUE score from 68.9 to 72.8.   - table-5 | Three Ablations Against the Full Model: Removing pre-training costs the most by far (14.8 average points), swapping the transformer for an LSTM costs 5.6, and dropping the auxiliary language modelling loss during fine-tuning actually gains 0.3 on average — helping only on the larger datasets (QQP and the two NLI sets) and hurting on the smaller ones.
