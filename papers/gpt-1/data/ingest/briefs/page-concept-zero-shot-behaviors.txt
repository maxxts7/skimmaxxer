NAME: Zero-Shot Behaviours

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
Heuristics that make the pre-trained generative model perform the evaluation tasks with no supervised fine-tuning at all, tracked across pre-training.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
The paper wants to know why language model pre-training of transformers works, and puts forward a hypothesis: the generative model learns to perform many of the evaluation tasks in the course of improving its language modelling.

To test this it strips out the supervised part entirely. It designs one heuristic per task that reads an answer directly off the generative model, with no fine-tuning and no trained classifier head.

It then runs those heuristics at checkpoints across pre-training and plots how they do as a function of updates.

The reported pattern is that performance is stable and steadily increases, which the paper takes as evidence that pre-training builds task-relevant functionality rather than fine-tuning acquiring it.

The same heuristics are also run against an LSTM, which the paper reports shows higher variance in zero-shot performance.

These are heuristics the authors designed, so the absolute numbers say as much about the heuristic as about the model. The trend across updates is what the paper actually leans on.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-taking-the-recipe-apart | Taking the Recipe Apart - Three diagnostics ask which parts of the recipe are doing the work. Transferring more pre-trained layers keeps helping rather than saturating early, and hand-built heuristics show the pre-trained model already doing several tasks at some level with no supervised training at all, improving steadily as pre-training runs. The removals are blunter: no pre-training costs the most, swapping the transformer for an LSTM costs less, and dropping the auxiliary language modelling loss helps on some datasets and hurts on others. The paper's reading of why attention transfers better than recurrence is stated as a hypothesis, and the zero-shot heuristics are hand-designed, so both carry less weight than the ablation numbers.

SUB-CONCEPTS (these render as expandable sections BELOW your page - reference them, do not duplicate them):
  - average-token-log-probability-scoring | Average Token Log-Probability Scoring: The common device behind three of the four heuristics: score a candidate string by the mean log-probability the language model assigns its tokens.
  - cola-zero-shot-heuristic | CoLA Zero-Shot Heuristic: Score each sentence by the generative model's average token log-probability and predict acceptability by thresholding that score.
  - sst2-zero-shot-heuristic | SST-2 Zero-Shot Heuristic: Append the token 'very' to the example, restrict the output distribution to 'positive' and 'negative', and take whichever scores higher.
  - race-zero-shot-heuristic | RACE Zero-Shot Heuristic: Condition the generative model on the document and question, then pick the answer with the highest average token log-probability.
  - dprd-winograd-heuristic | DPRD Winograd-Schema Zero-Shot Heuristic: Substitute each possible referent for the definite pronoun and choose the resolution under which the rest of the sentence scores higher.
  - transformer-inductive-bias | Structured Attentional Memory and Inductive Bias: The paper's hypothesis that the transformer's attention gives it a more structured memory than an LSTM, which helps transfer.

PREREQUISITES:
  - generative-pretraining | Generative Pre-Training (Stage One): Train a multi-layer transformer decoder to predict each next token on a large unlabelled corpus, and keep the weights rather than the outputs.
  - language-modeling-objective | Language Modelling Objective $L_1$: The pre-training loss $L_1(U)=\sum_i \log P(u_i \mid u_{i-k},\dots,u_{i-1};\Theta)$: maximize the log-probability the model gives each token given the tokens before it.
  - labeled-dataset-c | Labelled Dataset $C$: $C$ denotes a target task's labelled data: input token sequences each paired with one label.
