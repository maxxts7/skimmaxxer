NAME: Four Task Families, Twelve Datasets

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
The paper evaluates on natural language inference, question answering and commonsense reasoning, semantic similarity, and text classification, across twelve datasets.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
Rather than tuning one benchmark, the paper spreads evaluation across four families of supervised task.

Twelve datasets sit under them, some drawn from the GLUE multi-task benchmark.

The spread is the argument. A single model with one architecture and one pre-training run is fine-tuned on each, with only the input transformation and the output head changing.

The headline is new state-of-the-art results on 9 of the 12 datasets, in many cases beating ensembles.

The paper also notes the datasets span sizes from roughly 5.7k training examples on STS-B up to roughly 550k on SNLI, and uses that range to claim the method is not just a large-data technique.

Gains are reported as differences in percentage points against the previous best published number, mostly task-built recurrent systems, some of them ensembles or using ELMo features. The headline is new best results on nine of the twelve; this chapter also names the three where that does not happen, including the smallest inference dataset. SUB-CONCEPTS (these render as expandable sections BELOW your page - reference them, do not duplicate them):
  - absolute-improvement | Absolute Improvement: The paper reports its gains as differences in percentage points against the previous best, not as relative reductions in error.
  - state-of-the-art-9-of-12 | 9 of 12 Datasets: The paper's headline count: new best published results on nine of the twelve datasets, in many cases beating ensembles.
  - glue-benchmark | GLUE Benchmark: A recently released multi-task benchmark bundling several language understanding datasets, from which the paper draws some tasks and reports an overall score of 72.8.
  - baseline-comparisons | Baselines and Ensembles: The prior systems measured against: mostly task-specific biLSTM architectures, sometimes with ELMo features, attention, or ensembling.
    - lstm-gpt1 | LSTM: A recurrent architecture that processes tokens in sequence through a gated hidden state. Assumed known; used here as the comparison architecture.
  - natural-language-inference | Natural Language Inference (Textual Entailment): Given a premise and a hypothesis, decide whether the premise entails the hypothesis, contradicts it, or leaves it open.
    - coreference | Coreference: Two expressions in a text referring to the same entity, for instance a pronoun and the name it stands for.
    - snli | SNLI: A large NLI dataset built from image captions; the biggest in the evaluation at roughly 550k training examples.
    - multinli | MultiNLI (MNLI): A multi-genre NLI dataset covering transcribed speech, popular fiction and government reports, used as a benchmark and in the layer-transfer analysis.
    - qnli | QNLI: An NLI dataset built over Wikipedia articles, where the paper reports its largest NLI gain at 5.8%.
    - scitail | SciTail: An entailment dataset built from science exam questions, on which the paper reports a 5% absolute improvement.
    - rte | RTE: A small NLI dataset over news articles, with 2490 training examples, and the one NLI dataset where the paper does not beat the previous best.
  - question-answering-and-commonsense-reasoning | Question Answering and Commonsense Reasoning: Tasks requiring single- and multi-sentence reasoning over a passage: the paper evaluates on RACE and the Story Cloze Test.
    - race-dataset | RACE: A reading comprehension dataset of English exam passages and questions, chosen because it contains more reasoning-type questions than the alternatives.
      - cnn-and-squad-contrast | CNN and SQuAD as Contrast Datasets: Two earlier reading comprehension datasets named only to argue that RACE demands more reasoning than they do.
    - story-cloze-test | Story Cloze Test: A commonsense task: given a multi-sentence story, choose the correct ending from two options.
  - semantic-similarity | Semantic Similarity: Deciding whether two sentences mean the same thing, also called paraphrase detection. Evaluated on MRPC, QQP and STS-B.
    - mrpc | Microsoft Paraphrase Corpus (MRPC): A paraphrase detection dataset collected from news sources; the one dataset where the LSTM ablation beats the transformer.
    - qqp | Quora Question Pairs (QQP): A dataset of question pairs labelled for whether they ask the same thing, where the paper reports a 4.2% absolute improvement.
    - sts-b | Semantic Textual Similarity Benchmark (STS-B): A semantic similarity dataset of roughly 5.7k training examples, the smallest in the evaluation, where the paper reports a 1 point absolute gain.
  - text-classification | Text Classification: Single-sentence labelling tasks: CoLA for grammatical acceptability and SST-2 for binary sentiment.
    - cola | Corpus of Linguistic Acceptability (CoLA): Expert judgements on whether a sentence is grammatical. The paper reports a score of 45.4 against a previous best of 35.0.
    - sst-2 | Stanford Sentiment Treebank (SST-2): A binary sentiment classification task, on which the paper reports 91.3% accuracy, competitive with but not beating the state of the art.

PREREQUISITES:

  - natural-language-understanding | Natural Language Understanding: The umbrella label the paper uses for a set of diverse supervised text tasks, each with its own dataset and its own notion of a correct answer.
