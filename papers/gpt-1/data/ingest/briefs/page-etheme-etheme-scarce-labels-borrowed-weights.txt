NAME: Scarce Labels, Borrowed Weights

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
The paper opens on a mismatch: unlabelled text is abundant, while the labelled dataset for any one language understanding task is small and expensive to build. Its answer is the two-stage training procedure — fit a language model on raw text, then keep those weights and adapt the same network with labels — and it presents that recipe as one entry in semi-supervised learning for NLP, specifically the branch where unlabelled data chooses a starting point for the weights rather than adding a term to the supervised loss. The stance is drawn by contrast: feature-based transfer, and ELMo in particular, freezes a pre-trained model and feeds its hidden states into a separately built task model, whereas here the whole network is adjusted; word-embedding transfer moves one vector per word, whereas here every layer moves. Stage one and stage two also predict different things — the next token of the text against the label of the example — which is why the fine-tuning settings back off rather than restart: a learning rate of 6.25e-5 against the pre-training peak of 2.5e-4, batch size 32, and three passes over the task's training set on the assumption that most of the work is already done.
