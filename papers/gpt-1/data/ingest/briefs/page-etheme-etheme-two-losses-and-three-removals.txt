NAME: Two Losses, and Three Things Taken Away

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
Fine-tuning here optimizes two objectives at once: the task loss $L_2$ and the original language modelling loss kept alive on the labelled data, combined as $L_3 = L_2 + \lambda L_1$ with $\lambda$ set to 0.5. This is the same auxiliary-objective idea the related work describes as a branch of semi-supervised learning, used here as a supplement to pre-training rather than as a replacement for it, and it puts two losses in competition over the same weights — the language modelling head survives from stage one because the output layer reuses the token embeddings. Table 5 tests it by removal alongside two other removals, and the auxiliary term is the one whose effect is not uniform: it helps on the larger datasets and hurts on the smaller ones, with one $\lambda$ across the board. The other two removals are larger and cleaner. Training the same transformer directly on the supervised tasks, with no pre-training, costs 14.8 percent across the board; swapping the transformer for a single-layer 2048-unit LSTM inside the identical framework — a swap the shared input-transformation scheme makes possible — costs 5.6 average points, with MRPC the single dataset where the LSTM comes out ahead. Fourteen-point-eight against five-point-six is the paper's own ordering of what matters: the pre-training, then the architecture.
