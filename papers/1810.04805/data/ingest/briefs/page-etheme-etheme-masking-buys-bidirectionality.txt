NAME: Masking is what buys bidirectional training

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
A standard language model cannot simply be trained on both sides at once. With context available in both directions, a token in a multi-layer model reaches its own prediction through the layers below and can see itself. Masking removes the token from the input, so the see-itself path is cut and every layer may condition on both sides; the paper notes this is the old cloze task, run at a rate of 15% of WordPiece positions. The price is a mismatch: [MASK] appears constantly during pre-training and never during fine-tuning, which is why only 80% of chosen positions become [MASK], 10% become a random token and 10% are left alone. Table 8 shows the mixture barely moves fine-tuned scores, and predicting only 15% of tokens per batch gives fewer gradient signals per step, so Figure 5 has the masked model behind a left-to-right model early and ahead on accuracy well before the million steps are up.

  e003 [load-bearing] masking-recipe-80-10-10 (The 80/10/10 masking recipe) --depends-on--> pretrain-finetune-mismatch (Pre-train / fine-tune mismatch) | recipe exists to soften the mismatch: Of the 15% of positions chosen for prediction, 80% become [MASK], 10% become a random token, and 10% are left alone. Appendix C.2 then reports that fine-tuning is fairly robust to the mixture, so the recipe matters less in practice than the motivation suggests.

  e005 [supporting] mlm-output-softmax (Output softmax over the vocabulary) --depends-on--> wordpiece-vocabulary-30k (30,000-token vocabulary) | vocabulary size fixes softmax width: Each masked position's final hidden vector is projected to a distribution over the whole vocabulary.   e006 [supporting] mlm-convergence-cost (MLM's slower convergence) --depends-on--> masking-rate-15-percent (15% masking rate) | 15% rate causes slower convergence: A left-to-right model gets a prediction target at every position.   e076 [supporting] masking-rate-15-percent (15% masking rate) --instance-of--> masked-language-model (Masked LM (MLM)) | the rate MLM is run at: MLM says mask some fraction of tokens and predict them.   e077 [load-bearing] masking-recipe-80-10-10 (The 80/10/10 masking recipe) --instance-of--> mask-same-rnd-strategies (MASK, SAME and RND) | BERT's mixture of the three strategies: Once a token is selected for prediction, three things can happen to it: MASK, SAME or RND. BERT's recipe is one particular mixture of those three, 80/10/10.   e078 [supporting] mask-token ([MASK] token) --instance-of--> pretrain-finetune-mismatch (Pre-train / fine-tune mismatch) | the mismatch BERT actually has: A pre-train/fine-tune mismatch is any difference between training conditions and downstream conditions. BERT's concrete case is the [MASK] symbol: it is everywhere during pre-training and never appears at fine-tuning time. The 80/10/10 recipe exists to soften exactly this instance.
  e105 [load-bearing] masked-language-model (Masked LM (MLM)) --contrasts-with--> left-to-right-language-model (Left-to-right language model) | competing pre-training objectives: A left-to-right language model predicts each token from what came before it, so each prediction sees one side only. Masked LM instead hides a random subset of tokens and predicts them from both sides at once. The paper's whole case is that swapping the first objective for the second is what buys the downstream gains.
  e106 [supporting] mlm-convergence-cost (MLM's slower convergence) --contrasts-with--> left-to-right-language-model (Left-to-right language model) | slower per step, better per point: A left-to-right model gets a training signal at every position, while masked LM only gets one at the 15% of positions it hid. Appendix C.1 confirms the consequence the reader would expect: MLM does converge slightly slower. It also reports that MLM's absolute MNLI accuracy overtakes the left-to-right model's almost immediately, so the paper treats the slower convergence as a price worth paying.
  e108 [load-bearing] mask-token ([MASK] token) --contrasts-with--> pretrain-finetune-mismatch (Pre-train / fine-tune mismatch) | bidirectionality bought with a mismatch: The [MASK] placeholder is what makes bidirectional prediction trainable at all. But [MASK] never appears in any downstream task, so the model spends pre-training in conditions it will never meet again. The paper names this cost plainly as a downside of its own objective.
  e109 [supporting] masking-recipe-80-10-10 (The 80/10/10 masking recipe) --contrasts-with--> mask-token ([MASK] token) | mixed recipe versus always masking: The simplest thing to do with a selected token is replace it with [MASK] every time; BERT instead does so only 80% of the time. Table 8 puts the two side by side. Under fine-tuning the mixture barely matters, 84.2 against 84.3 on MNLI, but in the frozen feature-based NER setting the all-[MASK] run drops from 94.9 to 94.0.
