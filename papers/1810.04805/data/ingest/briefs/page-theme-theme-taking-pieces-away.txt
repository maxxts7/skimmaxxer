NAME: Taking pieces away

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
The experiments that remove or vary one piece of BERT at a time, to see what each is worth. Two cut-down pre-training runs charge a score drop to next sentence prediction and then to bidirectionality separately. A size ladder from 3 layers up to 24 finds that larger is better on every task tested, including the small ones, which the paper explains only as a hypothesis about how few fresh parameters fine-tuning adds. Two further ablations ask whether the full million steps are needed and whether the 80/10/10 masking mixture matters, the second run both fine-tuned and with BERT frozen on CoNLL-2003 NER.

EDGES BETWEEN MEMBERS OF THIS THEME:
  [supporting] feature-based-amplifies-mask-mismatch --depends-on--> feature-based-approach-with-bert | frozen model cannot adapt away mismatch: When BERT is fine-tuned, it can adjust its representations to the absence of [MASK] at test time. When it is frozen, it cannot. That is the paper's stated reason for expecting, and then observing, that a MASK-only masking strategy is more damaging in the feature-based NER setting.
  [minor] masking-procedure-ablation --depends-on--> concat-last-four-layers | reuses the best extraction setting: Table 8's feature-based column has to pick which hidden layers to read. It uses the concatenation of the top four layers, because Section 5.3 found that the best of the extraction options tested, at 0.3 F1 behind full fine-tuning. The masking ablation inherits that choice rather than re-deriving it.
  [load-bearing] no-nsp-ablation --contrasts-with--> ltr-no-nsp-ablation | isolating bidirectionality from NSP: These two cut-down models are trained on the same data, with the same fine-tuning scheme and hyperparameters, and differ in one thing: whether the pre-training objective sees both sides. Comparing them charges the difference to bidirectionality alone. The left-to-right model is worse on all five reported tasks, with MRPC falling from 86.5 to 77.5 and SQuAD F1 from 87.9 to 77.8.
  [supporting] bilstm-on-top-of-ltr --contrasts-with--> ltr-no-nsp-ablation | helps SQuAD, hurts GLUE: The added BiLSTM is a case where two sets of numbers point opposite ways. SQuAD F1 rises by 7.1 points, from 77.8 to 84.9. MRPC falls from 77.5 to 75.7 and SST-2 from 92.1 to 91.6, so the paper states plainly that the BiLSTM hurts the GLUE tasks.
  [supporting] effect-of-model-size --contrasts-with--> prior-scaling-evidence | strict gains against mixed prior results: Earlier work had found scaling less reliable: one result reported mixed effects from going from two to four bi-LM layers, another that raising hidden size from 200 to 600 helped but going to 1,000 did not. Table 6 instead shows accuracy rising at every step of the ladder on all four tasks, including MRPC with only 3,600 training examples. The paper notes that both earlier results used a feature-based approach, and hypothesises that fine-tuning with few randomly initialised parameters is what lets small tasks profit from large representations.
  [minor] tagging-without-crf --contrasts-with--> frozen-feature-bilstm-classifier | architecture dropped, then re-added: For fine-tuned NER the output is a plain per-token classifier, with no conditional random field on top, in keeping with the goal of adding as few new weights as possible. The frozen setting cannot be that thin. It puts a randomly initialised two-layer 768-dimensional BiLSTM between the extracted features and the classifier, which is task-specific architecture coming back in.

EVIDENCE ITEMS RELATED TO THIS THEME (link as [[item-id]]):
  - fig-1 | The Same Stack, Twice: The figure claims that pre-training and fine-tuning use one and the same network: an identical token-in, vector-out Transformer encoder appears in both panels, and the only thing that changes between them is the thin output layer bolted on top. The dotted arrows carry every pre-trained weight across to initialize the task model, so a downstream task inherits the whole network rather than a set of embeddings.
  - table-5 | Pre-training Task Ablation: Holding architecture, data and fine-tuning fixed, the table charges each drop in score to one removed piece of the pre-training recipe. Dropping next sentence prediction costs a little; replacing the masked LM with a left-to-right LM costs much more, above all on SQuAD (88.5 to 77.8 F1), and bolting a BiLSTM onto the left-to-right model repairs only part of that gap while making the GLUE numbers slightly worse.
  - table-6 | The Model-Size Ladder: Six separately pre-trained BERT models, from 3 layers up to 24, are lined up smallest to largest: held-out masked-LM perplexity falls at every step (5.84 to 3.23) while accuracy on MNLI-m, MRPC and SST-2 rises at every step. The paper reads this as evidence that scaling keeps paying off past sizes already considered large, and — its actual point — that the gains reach a task as small as MRPC, which has only 3,600 training examples.
  - table-7 | Fine-tuned versus frozen BERT on NER: On CoNLL-2003 named entity recognition, BERT scores close to the best published systems when fine-tuned, and — the point of the table — nearly as well when its weights are frozen and only its activations are fed to a small task model. Concatenating the top four hidden layers gives 96.1 Dev F1 against 96.4 for fine-tuning the same model, a gap of 0.3.
  - fig-4 | Four task shapes, one encoder: Four families of downstream task are drawn as four copies of the same diagram, differing only in what is packed into the input sequence and which output vectors an arrow is attached to. The paper uses this to show that adapting BERT to a task means plugging in task-specific inputs and one extra output layer, not redesigning the model.
  - fig-5 | MLM vs. Left-to-Right Over a Million Steps: Fine-tuning from progressively later pre-training checkpoints shows that MNLI accuracy is still climbing at 1,000,000 steps, so BERT's long pre-training run is not wasted. It also shows that the masked LM objective is behind a left-to-right objective at the very first checkpoints, overtakes it before 100k steps, and finishes roughly two points ahead.
  - table-8 | The masking mixture ablation: Six BERT pre-training runs that differ only in what happens to a token once it has been selected for prediction. Changing the mixture barely moves the fine-tuned scores, but pre-training with [MASK] alone costs 0.9 F1 on NER when BERT is frozen and used as a feature extractor. That single column is the paper's evidence for keeping the 80/10/10 mixture rather than always inserting [MASK].
