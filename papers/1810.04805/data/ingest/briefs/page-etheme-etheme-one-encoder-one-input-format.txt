NAME: One encoder, one input format, every task

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
BERT's encoder is the Transformer encoder, which the paper describes as almost identical to the original implementation; the architectural change is that self-attention is left unmasked, where GPT's is masked to the left. Everything else that makes tasks look alike happens in the input format. Each token's input vector is the sum of three learned rows — what the token is, whether it sits in span A or span B, and where it sits — and every sequence opens with [CLS], whose final vector C is the aggregate read-out. A pair of texts is concatenated into that one sequence, a single text is handled as a pair whose B half is empty, and a question with its passage becomes A and B separated by segment embeddings. Because the pair is encoded together, the paper argues self-attention already performs the cross-attention that two separately encoded texts would need; Figure 1 draws the same stack for pre-training and fine-tuning, and Figure 4 draws four task families as four copies of one diagram.

  e012 [load-bearing] self-attention-unifies-cross-attention (Self-attention subsuming cross-attention) --depends-on--> bidirectional-self-attention (Bidirectional self-attention) | argument needs unmasked attention: The paper argues that encoding a concatenated pair with self-attention already includes attention between the two texts in both directions. That holds only because every position may attend to every other position. Under left-only masking, tokens in the first span could never attend to the second, and the argument would fail.
  e013 [supporting] degenerate-text-pair (Degenerate text-∅ pair) --depends-on--> sentence-pair-packing (Sentence A / sentence B packing) | single text reuses the pair format: Classification and tagging tasks have only one piece of text. Rather than adding a second input format, the paper treats them as a pair whose second half is empty. The trick is available only because the pair format is the default.
  e014 [load-bearing] unified-architecture-across-tasks (Unified architecture across tasks) --depends-on--> input-representation (Input/output representation) | one format enables one architecture: The paper calls it a distinctive feature that the pre-trained and downstream architectures differ minimally. That is possible because a single token-sequence format already covers one span or two. Each task changes what goes into the slots, not the network that reads them.
  e015 [supporting] packed-question-passage-sequence (Question and passage as one packed sequence) --depends-on--> segment-embeddings (Segment embeddings) | segment tags separate question from passage: For SQuAD the question and the passage become one sequence. The model still has to tell them apart, and it does so through the learned A/B embedding added to every token: A for the question, B for the passage. Without segment embeddings the two would be indistinguishable apart from the [SEP] boundary.
  e020 [supporting] first-sub-token-classification (First sub-token as the token representation) --depends-on--> wordpiece-tokenization (WordPiece embeddings) | subword splitting forces a choice: NER labels are attached to words, but WordPiece may break a rare word into several pieces. Something has to decide which piece carries the tag. The paper's rule is to feed the first sub-token's final vector to the token-level classifier.
  e032 [load-bearing] bert-model-architecture (BERT's model architecture) --depends-on--> transformer-encoder (Transformer encoder) | reuses the Transformer unchanged: The paper does not describe the encoder in any detail. It states that the implementation is almost identical to the original Transformer and points readers to Vaswani et al. and to the Annotated Transformer guide. Everything BERT adds sits around a mechanism it borrows rather than defines.
  e033 [load-bearing] bert-framework (BERT's two-stage framework) --supported-by--> fig-1 (The Same Stack, Twice (fig-1)) | same encoder drawn twice: The paper's framing claim is that pre-training and fine-tuning are two stages of one network, not two networks. Figure 1 puts the two panels side by side and the encoder block is identical in both. What changes between them is only the output layer and the arrows feeding it.
  e034 [load-bearing] input-embedding-sum (Sum of token, segment and position embeddings) --supported-by--> fig-2 (The Three-Row Input Sum (fig-2)) | three rows added per token: Figure 2 draws each token's input as three stacked rows joined by plus signs: the token embedding, the segment embedding for span A or span B, and the position embedding.   e037 [supporting] unified-architecture-across-tasks (Unified architecture across tasks) --supported-by--> fig-4 (Four task shapes, one encoder (fig-4)) | four tasks, one diagram repeated: Figure 4 draws sentence-pair classification, single-sentence classification, question answering and tagging as four copies of the same encoder. Only two things differ between the panels: what gets packed into the input sequence, and which output vectors a small head reads. It is an illustration rather than a measurement, so it demonstrates the claim's shape and leaves the accuracy question to the result tables.
  e070 [load-bearing] bert-model-architecture (BERT's model architecture) --instance-of--> transformer-encoder (Transformer encoder) | a particular Transformer encoder stack: The paper says its architecture is a multi-layer bidirectional Transformer encoder, almost identical to the original implementation, and declines to re-describe it. So BERT's encoder is one configured instance of the cited Transformer encoder, not a new design.
  e071 [load-bearing] bidirectional-self-attention (Bidirectional self-attention) --instance-of--> self-attention (Self-attention) | self-attention with no mask: Self-attention in general lets each position mix information from the positions it may attend to. Bidirectional self-attention is the case where that permitted set is the whole sequence, left and right, at every layer.
  e072 [supporting] constrained-self-attention (Constrained (left-context-only) self-attention) --instance-of--> self-attention (Self-attention) | self-attention masked to the left: The GPT Transformer runs the same operation but with a mask that hides every position to the right of the current token. The paper notes this is the only architectural difference between BERT_BASE and GPT, apart from the masking.
  e073 [supporting] bert-base-and-bert-large (BERT_BASE and BERT_LARGE) --instance-of--> model-size-notation (Model size notation: L, H, A) | two settings of L, H, A: L, H and A are the paper's three dials: layers, hidden size, attention heads. BERT_BASE fixes them at 12, 768 and 12 for 110M parameters; BERT_LARGE at 24, 1024 and 16 for 340M. Every reported result belongs to one of these two settings.
  e074 [minor] wordpiece-vocabulary-30k (30,000-token vocabulary) --instance-of--> wordpiece-tokenization (WordPiece embeddings) | the vocabulary size actually used: WordPiece is a general subword scheme; the size of its vocabulary is a free choice.   e079 [supporting] final-hidden-vector-c (C, the final [CLS] vector) --instance-of--> final-hidden-vector-ti (T_i, the final vector for token i) | C is T at the [CLS] position: T_i is the notation for the top-layer vector at any position i. C is that same vector taken at position zero, where [CLS] sits, and given its own name because so many output heads read it. Both live in R^H.
  e082 [supporting] packed-question-passage-sequence (Question and passage as one packed sequence) --instance-of--> sentence-pair-packing (Sentence A / sentence B packing) | QA as an A/B pair: Pre-training already teaches BERT to read two spans packed into one sequence, tagged A and B. SQuAD reuses that format directly: the question takes the A embedding and the passage takes B. No new input machinery is added for question answering.
  e083 [supporting] degenerate-text-pair (Degenerate text-∅ pair) --instance-of--> sentence-pair-packing (Sentence A / sentence B packing) | single text as an empty-B pair: Classification and tagging have only one input text, so there is nothing to put in slot B. The paper handles this by calling it a degenerate text-empty pair, which keeps single-text tasks inside the same packing format rather than needing a second one.
  e104 [load-bearing] bidirectional-self-attention (Bidirectional self-attention) --contrasts-with--> constrained-self-attention (Constrained (left-context-only) self-attention) | attend everywhere versus attend leftward: The two are the same operation with different masks over which positions a token may look at. Constrained attention blanks out everything to a token's right, which is what makes standard left-to-right pre-training possible. Removing that mask is the single architectural change the paper says separates BERT from GPT.
  e117 [supporting] task-specific-architecture (Task-specific architecture) --contrasts-with--> minimal-task-specific-parameters (Minimal task-specific parameters) | hand-built model versus one layer: The feature-based route assumes a model designed by hand for each task, into which pre-trained vectors are inserted. The fine-tuning route treats new weights as something to minimise. BERT's contribution list makes the contrast a claim: pre-trained representations reduce the need for heavily-engineered task-specific architectures.
  e118 [supporting] unified-architecture-across-tasks (Unified architecture across tasks) --contrasts-with--> separate-fine-tuned-models-per-task (One fine-tuned model per task) | one architecture, eleven models: The paper calls it a distinctive feature that the pre-trained network and the downstream network differ only minimally. That uniformity is about shape, not about weights. Every task still ends up with its own fully fine-tuned copy, so eleven tasks means eleven models branching from one checkpoint.
  e120 [supporting] self-attention-unifies-cross-attention (Self-attention subsuming cross-attention) --contrasts-with--> bidirectional-cross-attention (Bidirectional cross attention) | concatenate instead of cross-attend: The usual way to relate two texts is to encode each one separately and then run attention between them in both directions. BERT packs both texts into one sequence and runs ordinary self-attention over the whole thing. The paper's argument is that this already includes the cross attention, so the separate mechanism is not needed.
