PAGE FOR CONCEPT: masked-language-model
NAME: Masked LM (MLM)

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
BERT's first pre-training task: mask a random 15% of input tokens and train the model to predict the originals from context on both sides.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
The stated motivation is that a deep bidirectional model should be more powerful than a left-to-right model or a shallow concatenation, but standard conditional language models cannot be trained bidirectionally without the target leaking into its own context. Masking sidesteps this: the token being predicted is hidden from the input, so the model may attend to everything on both sides without seeing the answer. The final hidden vectors at masked positions feed an output softmax over the vocabulary, trained with cross-entropy against the original tokens. The paper notes this is often called a Cloze task, and that unlike a denoising autoencoder BERT predicts only the masked words.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-the-two-pre-training-tasks | The two pre-training tasks - The two self-supervised objectives, and the run that trained them. Masked LM hides 15% of the tokens and predicts them from both sides, which gets around the fact that a bidirectional model would otherwise reach the token it is predicting through the layers below. Next sentence prediction asks, from the [CLS] vector, whether span B really followed span A in the corpus. The rest is the recipe: 3.3 billion words of books and Wikipedia, one million steps, and a schedule that saves the long sequences for the final tenth of training.

  [load-bearing] -> see-itself-problem (The "see itself" problem) | depends-on | masking answers the see-itself problem: If a token could attend to both sides while also being the prediction target, it would reach its own prediction through the layers below. The paper says such a model could then predict the target trivially. Masking exists to break that path: the target is removed from the input before the model reads it.
  [load-bearing] -> left-to-right-language-model (Left-to-right language model) | contrasts-with | competing pre-training objectives: A left-to-right language model predicts each token from what came before it, so each prediction sees one side only. Masked LM instead hides a random subset of tokens and predicts them from both sides at once. The paper's whole case is that swapping the first objective for the second is what buys the downstream gains.

  - table-5 | Pre-training Task Ablation: Holding architecture, data and fine-tuning fixed, the table charges each drop in score to one removed piece of the pre-training recipe. Dropping next sentence prediction costs a little; replacing the masked LM with a left-to-right LM costs much more, above all on SQuAD (88.5 to 77.8 F1), and bolting a BiLSTM onto the left-to-right model repairs only part of that gap while making the GLUE numbers slightly worse.
  - table-6 | The Model-Size Ladder: Six separately pre-trained BERT models, from 3 layers up to 24, are lined up smallest to largest: held-out masked-LM perplexity falls at every step (5.84 to 3.23) while accuracy on MNLI-m, MRPC and SST-2 rises at every step. The paper reads this as evidence that scaling keeps paying off past sizes already considered large, and — its actual point — that the gains reach a task as small as MRPC, which has only 3,600 training examples.
  - fig-5 | MLM vs. Left-to-Right Over a Million Steps: Fine-tuning from progressively later pre-training checkpoints shows that MNLI accuracy is still climbing at 1,000,000 steps, so BERT's long pre-training run is not wasted. It also shows that the masked LM objective is behind a left-to-right objective at the very first checkpoints, overtakes it before 100k steps, and finishes roughly two points ahead.
  - table-8 | The masking mixture ablation: Six BERT pre-training runs that differ only in what happens to a token once it has been selected for prediction. Changing the mixture barely moves the fine-tuned scores, but pre-training with [MASK] alone costs 0.9 F1 on NER when BERT is frozen and used as a feature extractor. That single column is the paper's evidence for keeping the 80/10/10 mixture rather than always inserting [MASK].
