NAME: Taking pieces away: what each part is worth

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
Two cut-down models are pre-trained under identical data, architecture and fine-tuning conditions, so any drop can be charged to the one removed piece. No NSP keeps masked prediction but drops the sentence-pair task; LTR & No NSP goes further and masks attention to the left, making an in-house stand-in for GPT that is kept left-only through fine-tuning as well. In Table 5 the sentence-pair task contributes modestly, while the left-only model loses around ten F1 on SQuAD, and a randomly initialised BiLSTM bolted on top to supply right-side context recovers part of SQuAD but hurts GLUE. The size ladder in Table 6 runs six pre-trained models from 3 layers to 24 and gains at every step, including on small tasks, against earlier feature-based work that found little benefit from scale; the explanation offered — that fine-tuning adds so few random parameters that small tasks can still use a large representation — is stated as a hypothesis, not tested. Finally BERT is frozen and used as features on CoNLL-2003 NER, where concatenating the top four layers comes within a fraction of a point of fine-tuning, and where the frozen model, unable to adapt, is the setting in which the masking mismatch shows up most.

  e121 [supporting] effect-of-model-size (Effect of model size) --contrasts-with--> prior-scaling-evidence (Prior mixed evidence on scaling) | strict gains against mixed prior results: Earlier work had found scaling less reliable: one result reported mixed effects from going from two to four bi-LM layers, another that raising hidden size from 200 to 600 helped but going to 1,000 did not.
