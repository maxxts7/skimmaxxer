NAME: Next Sentence Prediction (NSP)

SUMMARY (already shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat it verbatim):
BERT's second pre-training task: a binary classification of whether span B actually followed span A in the corpus, predicted from the [CLS] vector.

DRAFT EXPLANATION from the extraction stage (your page replaces this; keep what is right, go deeper):
The stated motivation is that tasks like question answering and inference depend on the relationship between two sentences, which language modeling does not directly capture. The task can be trivially generated from any monolingual corpus: half the time B is the real next span, half the time a random one. The paper calls it simple, reports the final model reaching 97-98% accuracy on NSP itself against a 50% chance baseline, and points to Section 5.1 rather than that number for evidence that it helps downstream.

THEME IT BELONGS TO: theme-the-two-pre-training-tasks | The two pre-training tasks - The two self-supervised objectives, and the run that trained them. Masked LM hides 15% of the tokens and predicts them from both sides, which gets around the fact that a bidirectional model would otherwise reach the token it is predicting through the layers below. Next sentence prediction asks, from the [CLS] vector, whether span B really followed span A in the corpus. The rest is the recipe: 3.3 billion words of books and Wikipedia, one million steps, and a schedule that saves the long sequences for the final tenth of training.

SUB-CONCEPTS (these render as expandable sections BELOW your page - reference them, do not duplicate them):
  - isnext-notnext | IsNext / NotNext labels: The two NSP labels: B genuinely followed A, or B is a random span from elsewhere in the corpus, balanced 50/50.
  - c-not-a-sentence-representation | C is not a sentence representation without fine-tuning: A footnote cautioning that the pre-trained [CLS] vector should not be read as a general-purpose sentence embedding.
  - full-parameter-transfer-vs-sentence-embeddings | Transferring all parameters, not just sentence embeddings: The paper's stated difference from prior sentence-representation objectives: those transfer only sentence embeddings downstream, while BERT transfers every parameter.

PREREQUISITES:
  - cls-token | [CLS] token: A special token placed first in every sequence, whose final hidden state is used as the aggregate representation of the whole input.
  - sentence-pair-packing | Sentence A / sentence B packing: Two spans are concatenated into one sequence, the first labeled A and the second B, so a pair of texts is handled by the same single-sequence model.
