NAME: The two pre-training tasks

THEME SUMMARY (shown above your page as the lede - do not repeat verbatim):
The two self-supervised objectives, and the run that trained them. Masked LM hides 15% of the tokens and predicts them from both sides, which gets around the fact that a bidirectional model would otherwise reach the token it is predicting through the layers below. Next sentence prediction asks, from the [CLS] vector, whether span B really followed span A in the corpus. The rest is the recipe: 3.3 billion words of books and Wikipedia, one million steps, and a schedule that saves the long sequences for the final tenth of training.

EDGES BETWEEN MEMBERS OF THIS THEME:
  [load-bearing] masked-language-model --depends-on--> see-itself-problem | masking answers the see-itself problem: If a token could attend to both sides while also being the prediction target, it would reach its own prediction through the layers below. The paper says such a model could then predict the target trivially. Masking exists to break that path: the target is removed from the input before the model reads it.
  [load-bearing] masking-recipe-80-10-10 --depends-on--> pretrain-finetune-mismatch | recipe exists to soften the mismatch: Of the 15% of positions chosen for prediction, 80% become [MASK], 10% become a random token, and 10% are left alone. The paper gives the mismatch as the reason for the last two cases: the model should not learn that a prediction is only ever needed where a [MASK] sits. Appendix C.2 then reports that fine-tuning is fairly robust to the mixture, so the recipe matters less in practice than the motivation suggests.
  [supporting] pretrain-finetune-mismatch --depends-on--> mask-token | mismatch arises from the placeholder: The [MASK] token is a symbol invented for pre-training. It never appears in a downstream task's input. That asymmetry is the whole content of the mismatch the paper is worried about.
  [supporting] mlm-convergence-cost --depends-on--> masking-rate-15-percent | 15% rate causes slower convergence: A left-to-right model gets a prediction target at every position. Masking 15% means only about one position in seven produces a gradient signal per sequence. The paper confirms the masked LM converges slightly slower, but reports it passes the left-to-right model on absolute MNLI accuracy almost immediately.
  [supporting] training-sequence-generation --depends-on--> document-level-corpus | contiguous spans need intact documents: Each training example samples two spans that are usually genuine neighbours, then caps their combined length at 512 tokens. Both the length and the neighbour relation require the corpus to keep documents whole. The paper names the Billion Word Benchmark, a shuffled sentence-level corpus, as the kind of data it deliberately avoids.
  [supporting] masked-language-model --instance-of--> cloze-task | MLM is a cloze task: The paper names its own objective a masked LM, then adds that this is what the literature has long called a Cloze task, citing Taylor (1953). Delete a word, ask a reader to restore it from what surrounds it on both sides: BERT's pre-training objective is that procedure run at scale.
  [supporting] masking-rate-15-percent --instance-of--> masked-language-model | the rate MLM is run at: MLM says mask some fraction of tokens and predict them. The paper fixes that fraction at 15% of WordPiece positions, uniformly, in every experiment. The choice is stated rather than tuned, and it is also what leaves only 15% of positions producing gradient per batch.
  [supporting] mask-token --instance-of--> pretrain-finetune-mismatch | the mismatch BERT actually has: A pre-train/fine-tune mismatch is any difference between training conditions and downstream conditions. BERT's concrete case is the [MASK] symbol: it is everywhere during pre-training and never appears at fine-tuning time. The 80/10/10 recipe exists to soften exactly this instance.
  [supporting] bookscorpus --instance-of--> document-level-corpus | one of the two document corpora: The paper insists on a corpus that keeps documents intact, so long contiguous spans can be sampled. BooksCorpus, at 800M words of unpublished books, is one of the two sources chosen to meet that requirement.
  [minor] english-wikipedia-corpus --instance-of--> document-level-corpus | the larger document-level source: English Wikipedia contributes 2,500M words, with lists, tables and headers stripped so only running text remains.   [load-bearing] mask-token --contrasts-with--> pretrain-finetune-mismatch | bidirectionality bought with a mismatch: The [MASK] placeholder is what makes bidirectional prediction trainable at all. But [MASK] never appears in any downstream task, so the model spends pre-training in conditions it will never meet again. The paper names this cost plainly as a downside of its own objective.
  [supporting] masking-recipe-80-10-10 --contrasts-with--> mask-token | mixed recipe versus always masking: The simplest thing to do with a selected token is replace it with [MASK] every time; BERT instead does so only 80% of the time. Table 8 puts the two side by side. Under fine-tuning the mixture barely matters, 84.2 against 84.3 on MNLI, but in the frozen feature-based NER setting the all-[MASK] run drops from 94.9 to 94.0.
  [supporting] document-level-corpus --contrasts-with--> billion-word-benchmark | documents versus shuffled sentences: BERT needs long contiguous text, both to sample 512-token sequences and to have a genuine next sentence for NSP. The Billion Word Benchmark is named as the kind of corpus that cannot supply this, because its sentences are shuffled. The paper calls keeping documents intact critical, which makes the choice of corpus a constraint rather than a convenience.

EVIDENCE ITEMS RELATED TO THIS THEME (link as [[item-id]]):
  - fig-1 | The Same Stack, Twice: The figure claims that pre-training and fine-tuning use one and the same network: an identical token-in, vector-out Transformer encoder appears in both panels, and the only thing that changes between them is the thin output layer bolted on top. The dotted arrows carry every pre-trained weight across to initialize the task model, so a downstream task inherits the whole network rather than a set of embeddings.
  - table-5 | Pre-training Task Ablation: Holding architecture, data and fine-tuning fixed, the table charges each drop in score to one removed piece of the pre-training recipe. Dropping next sentence prediction costs a little; replacing the masked LM with a left-to-right LM costs much more, above all on SQuAD (88.5 to 77.8 F1), and bolting a BiLSTM onto the left-to-right model repairs only part of that gap while making the GLUE numbers slightly worse.
  - table-6 | The Model-Size Ladder: Six separately pre-trained BERT models, from 3 layers up to 24, are lined up smallest to largest: held-out masked-LM perplexity falls at every step (5.84 to 3.23) while accuracy on MNLI-m, MRPC and SST-2 rises at every step. The paper reads this as evidence that scaling keeps paying off past sizes already considered large, and — its actual point — that the gains reach a task as small as MRPC, which has only 3,600 training examples.
  - fig-5 | MLM vs. Left-to-Right Over a Million Steps: Fine-tuning from progressively later pre-training checkpoints shows that MNLI accuracy is still climbing at 1,000,000 steps, so BERT's long pre-training run is not wasted. It also shows that the masked LM objective is behind a left-to-right objective at the very first checkpoints, overtakes it before 100k steps, and finishes roughly two points ahead.
  - table-8 | The masking mixture ablation: Six BERT pre-training runs that differ only in what happens to a token once it has been selected for prediction. Changing the mixture barely moves the fine-tuned scores, but pre-training with [MASK] alone costs 0.9 F1 on NER when BERT is frozen and used as a feature extractor. That single column is the paper's evidence for keeping the 80/10/10 mixture rather than always inserting [MASK].
